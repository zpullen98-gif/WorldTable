## 1. Bananas Foster, /recipes/bananas-foster/
(search, snippet) Ingredients: 1 ounce butter; half cup light brown sugar; quarter teaspoon cinnamon; 1 and a half ounces banana liqueur; 1 and a half ounces aged rum; half a banana per person. Method: combine butter, sugar and cinnamon in a flambé pan; as the butter melts over medium heat add the banana liqueur and stir; peel and add the bananas; cook until they begin to soften, about one to two minutes; tilt the pan back to heat the far edge, carefully add the rum and tilt the pan toward the flame to ignite; stir the sauce "to ensure all of the alcohol cooks out". History on the same page: in 1951 Owen Brennan challenged his chef Paul Blangé to create a dish featuring bananas; named for Richard Foster, a friend of Owen Brennan and chairman of the New Orleans Crime Commission, on which Brennan served.

This is the same list gap-fill-2 recorded, now seen a second time. It settles the rum and banana liqueur question for the published recipe: both, in equal measure, liqueur in first with the sugar, rum last for the flame. Cinnamon is in the pan from the start; the page has no step of shaking cinnamon into the flame.

Two notes. (a) This snippet names the "New Orleans Crime Commission", which matches the PDF (p49); the earlier research (dossier-the-floor, pdf-corrections) recorded the house recipe page as saying "Metropolitan Crime Commission". Either the page changed or the earlier snippet was from a different house page (the Traditions page is the likelier source). On present evidence the recipe page agrees with the guide. (b) The page's claim that stirring makes "all of the alcohol" cook out is the house's wording; (expert) it is not true in the kitchen sense, a flamed sauce keeps a good share of its alcohol, so the server line stays "flaming does not remove all the alcohol" (pdf p34), and the recipe page is not to be quoted on that point.

Also surfaced: the dessert menu PDF at /uploads/pages/bno-dessert.pdf headed "Brennan's Originals, World Famous Bananas Foster".

## 2. Turtle Soup, /recipes/turtle-soup/
(search, snippet, which attributes it to the Brennan's New Orleans Cookbook) Ingredients: 4 tablespoons unsalted butter; 1 and a half pounds turtle meat, trimmed of sinew and cut in quarter-inch cubes; 8 cloves garlic, minced; 2 medium onions, chopped fine; 4 stalks celery, chopped fine; 2 medium green peppers, chopped fine; 1 and a half tablespoons kosher salt plus to taste; 1 teaspoon black pepper; 2 medium Creole tomatoes, seeded and chopped fine; three-quarter cup all-purpose flour; 3 quarts beef stock; 1 cup Worcestershire sauce; 2 tablespoons Crystal pepper sauce; 2 tablespoons flat-leaf parsley; 2 tablespoons fresh thyme leaves; 5 large hard-boiled eggs, chopped fine; 6 ounces fresh spinach, sliced fine; zest of 3 lemons; one-third cup lemon juice; half cup good-quality sherry. The method was not in the snippet; the kitchen dossier's earlier summary (turtle and garlic in butter, vegetables, tomato, flour, stock, seasonings, thirty minutes, then egg, spinach, lemon and sherry for ten more) is consistent with this list but was not re-read.

What it settles: the published soup is turtle only (no veal), thickened with flour and beef stock, finished with hard-boiled egg, spinach, lemon and sherry. That rhymes with the 2026 menu line "100% turtle meat, brown butter spinach, grated egg, aged Sherry" (pdf p14). What it does not settle: whether the restaurant kitchen follows this build, whether the sherry is added in the kitchen or offered at the table, and gluten (the published recipe has flour; the kitchen's may differ).

## 3. Seafood Filé Gumbo, /recipes/seafood-fil-gumbo/
(search, snippet) A large-batch recipe: 2 cups canola oil; 1 cup all-purpose flour; 1 quart onions, small dice; 1 pint celery; 1 pint red bell pepper; 1 pint green bell pepper; 1 cup chopped garlic; 1 pound okra in quarter-inch slices; 5 pounds andouille, quartered lengthwise and sliced quarter inch; 1 teaspoon filé powder; 1 quart diced tomatoes; 3 gallons crab stock or shrimp stock; one-third cup kosher salt; one-eighth teaspoon Creole spice; 3 bay leaves; 4 sprigs thyme; 2 pounds jumbo lump crabmeat; 40 freshly shucked oysters; 2 pounds 21/25 shrimp, peeled and deveined; half cup Crystal hot sauce. The page also gives a crab or shrimp stock made from crab bodies or shrimp shells, onion, garlic, bay leaves, black peppercorns and water.

What it settles: the published gumbo is a flour and oil roux (gluten), with okra, tomato, filé and andouille, shellfish stock, and the three shellfish the menu names (shrimp, crab, oysters). The menu prints none of okra, tomato or filé (pdf p16), so the server says those only as "our published recipe uses", never as a menu fact; the restaurant bowl may differ, and the Gambit relaunch review already called it light on okra.

## 6. Eggs Sardou, /recipes/eggs-sardou/
(search, snippet) Components: crispy artichokes, poached eggs, Parmesan creamed spinach, Choron sauce. Creamed spinach: reduce cream by half and whisk in grated Parmesan; heat butter, add garlic and spinach, wilt quickly, drain on paper towel; fold the drained spinach into the Parmesan cream; season with salt and pepper. Artichokes: lemon water with salt; strip the outer leaves, trim the top to expose the choke, trim the tough outer flesh, cut off the stem, scoop out the choke; hold the hearts in lemon water; simmer 15 minutes, cool; bread in flour, beaten egg and panko; fry until golden, about 3 minutes; season with salt. Assembly: creamed spinach in the centre of a warm plate, two artichokes side by side on the spinach, a poached egg on each, Choron spooned over. Quantities were not in the snippet.

What it settles: the published artichokes are fresh hearts, panko-breaded and fried (so the dish carries gluten in the breading and egg in the breading, the poached eggs and the Choron); the spinach is a Parmesan cream, not a Mornay; the plate is two artichokes and two eggs. Choron's own build (hollandaise with tomato) was not in the snippet.
