## 1. Leidenheimer Baking Company (Creole Caesar)

1. Founded in 1896 by George Leidenheimer, an immigrant from Deidesheim, Germany; first on Dryades Street, moved in 1904 to Simon Bolivar Avenue.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leidenheimer_Baking_Company (snippet): "Leidenheimer Baking Company was started in 1896 by George Leidenheimer, an immigrant from Deidesheim, Germany. Initially located on Dryades Street, it moved in 1904 to Simon Bolivar Avenue, where it continues in business as the city's largest and best-known maker of po'boy bread."
2. He first baked dense German breads, then switched to the light New Orleans French loaf.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leidenheimer_Baking_Company (snippet): "Originally Leidenheimer baked the heavy, dense brown breads of his native Germany but it was by producing New Orleans French bread, with its crisp crust, that Leidenheimer found fame." Also https://www.chowhound.com/2111846/new-orleans-po-boy-bread-german-bakery-leidenheimer (summary): he "changed his style to cater to the French customer base in Old New Orleans, who preferred a light, airy texture to their bread."
3. The bread predates the po-boy, which dates to the late 1920s.
   Source: https://www.chowhound.com/2111846/new-orleans-po-boy-bread-german-bakery-leidenheimer (summary): "Leidenheimer's French bread predates the official birth of the po'boy, which dates to the late 1920s, but it has become the bread used by many of the city's po'boy shops."
4. Run today by Robert J. "Sandy" Whann IV, the fourth generation and George Leidenheimer's great-grandson.
   Source: https://www.bakingbusiness.com/articles/29433-whann-leidenheimer-new-orleans-stronger-10-years-after-storm (snippet): "Robert J. 'Sandy' Whann IV is the fourth generation of the family operating Leidenheimer, and he is the great-grandson of George Leidenheimer, who established the baking company in 1896."
5. After Hurricane Katrina the bakery kept going by baking in a friend's bakery in Chicago.
   Source: same bakingbusiness.com article (summary): "Within a week of the storm hitting, Whann had been in touch with friends around the country in the baking business and arranged for the operations manager to travel to Chicago and produce their product in a friend's bakery."
6. The dough is naturally fermented and hand-stretched to a 32-inch loaf.
   Source: https://www.myneworleans.com/chronicles-leidenheimer-baking-co/ (summary): "The bread dough is naturally fermented, helping to produce its signature flavor and crumb, and hand-stretched to its 32-inch length."
7. It also bakes pistolets and muffuletta bread and has served the city's restaurants for more than a century.
   Source: same myneworleans.com page (summary): "as a supplier of po-boys, pistolets, muffuletta and other local specialties, Leidenheimer Baking Co. has been strongly connected to the New Orleans restaurant scene for longer than a century."

Brennan's link: the menu prints "Leidenheimer Crisps" on the Creole Caesar. That is the whole public link.

Expert knowledge: pronounced roughly LIDE-en-hy-mer.

## 2. Tien Dat tofu and the VEGGI Farmers Cooperative (Blackened Tofu)

1. TIEN DAT TOFU LLC is a Louisiana company with West Bank addresses in Marrero and Harvey, Jefferson Parish.
   Source: research/gap-sourcing-live-2026-10-03.md section 1 (opencorporates.com and opengovus.com listings): "5141 Evans Dr, Marrero, LA 70072, another lists Harvey, LA 70058."
2. Tien Dat tofu is sold under the VEGGI Farmers Cooperative, as extra firm tofu made in small batches.
   Source: research/gap-sourcing-live-2026-10-03.md section 1 (marketumbrella.org Market Morsel, 22 March 2021, and the "Tien Dat Tofu / VEGGI Farmers Cooperative" vendor page, summary): extra firm tofu with non-GMO soy, made fresh weekly in small batches; styles include extra firm, black mushroom, and lemongrass and red chilli.
3. VEGGI stands for the Village de L'Est Green Growers Initiative and grew out of the aftermath of the BP oil spill.
   Source: https://crescentcityfarmersmarket.org/news/958/187/LOUISIANA-INSPIRED-VEGGI-Farmers-Cooperative-has-been-lifeline-for-many-Vietnamese-Americans-in-New-Orleans-East (summary): "VEGGI, Village de L'Est Green Growers Initiative, Farmers Cooperative grew out of the BP oil spill's aftermath with the mission of empowering growers in the Greater New Orleans area and creating sustainable, high-quality jobs in urban agriculture."
4. Established in 2011, community member owned, based in New Orleans East across the street from Mary Queen of Vietnam Church.
   Source: https://www.gnof.org/veggi-farmers-cooperative-creates-jobs-by-growing-healthy-food/ and https://edibleneworleans.ediblecommunities.com/shop/veggi-farmers-cooperative-cultures-cuisines-meld-community-co-op-garden (summary): "VEGGI Cooperative was established in 2011 and is a community-member-owned and operated farmer's co-op based out of New Orleans East. Located across the street from the Mary Queen of Vietnam Church."
5. Twelve growers and programme developers formed it; it supplies herbs, vegetables and tofu to restaurants, grocers and the community.
   Source: same (summary): "12 growers and program developers elected through a community forum decided to utilize their background in sustainable agriculture to form the co-op and provide a steady harvest of herbs, vegetables, and even tofu to restaurants, grocers, and community members."
6. The co-op is best known for its tofu and sells at the Crescent City Farmers Market, run by Market Umbrella since 1995.
   Source: https://www.crescentcityfarmersmarket.com/news/69/42/VEGGI-Co-Op (summary): "Most famously known for their freshly-made and locally-processed tofu, VEGGI Co-op also brings a rich array of other amazing products ... chili oil, seed bars, and loofah sponges." And: "Market Umbrella has operated the Crescent City Farmers Markets (CCFM) since 1995."
7. Village de L'Est is a Vietnamese American community in New Orleans East.
   Source: https://www.crescentcityfarmersmarket.com/news/69/42/VEGGI-Co-Op (summary): "cooperatively-growing farmers in Village de L'Est, a Vietnamese-American community in New Orleans East."
8. Daniel Nguyen has served as the co-op's project manager.
   Source: https://modernfarmer.com/2013/10/new-orleans-vietnamese-american-farmers-band-together/ (summary): "Daniel Nguyen serves as Project Manager." (Reported in 2013; not confirmed as current.)

Brennan's link: the menu prints "New Orleans Tien Dat Tofu"; the guide (guide.txt) says "made right here in New Orleans". The co-op is in New Orleans East; the registered maker's addresses are on the West Bank across the river.

Not found: who makes the tofu by hand, and the method. A search for "Tien Dat" tofu New Orleans returned nothing on the maker.

Expert knowledge: Vietnamese "Tiến Đạt" is roughly "tee-EN daht"; the house's own pronunciation is a lineup question.

## 3. Two Brooks Farm (Blackened Tofu, rice grits)

1. At Sumner, Mississippi, in the Delta, owned by Mike Wagner, a ninth-generation farmer.
   Source: https://southernladymagazine.com/?p=10120 (summary): "Two Brooks Farm is owned by ninth-generation farmer Mike Wagner, and it is located in Sumner, Mississippi."
2. The family counts ten generations of farmers back to 1742.
   Source: same (summary): "His family can count an unbroken line of ten generations of farmers back to 1742, with 281 consecutive harvests of rice and other crops."
3. Started in 1992 by Mike Wagner; about 2,000 acres of rice and 1,000 of soybeans.
   Source: research/gap-sourcing-live-2026-10-03.md section 6 (farmprogress.com, world-grain.com, thepacker.com snippets).
4. A line of 14 products including rice, grits and flour; a favourite is the Original Mississippi Midlands Brown Rice Grits.
   Source: https://www.eatdrinkmississippi.com/post/two-brooks-farm-homegrown-rice (summary): "Mike's environmentally sustainable long-grain rice has become the basis for Two Brooks Farm, which offers a line of 14 products that include grits and flour as well as rice. One of Mike's favorites is the Original Mississippi Midlands Brown Rice Grits."
5. Ducks and geese winter on the fields; Wagner cut fertiliser after seeing the change in his soil, and uses no fungicides or insecticides on the rice.
   Source: https://bakingbusiness.com/articles/45591-rice-grown-ecologically-begins-to-take-hold-at-retail-food-service (summary): "After noticing a change in his soil when ducks and geese flocked to the area, Wagner began experimenting with reduced fertilizer use ... No fungicides or insecticides are involved in the growing of the rice, and geese and ducks deposit a great deal of the fertilizer."
6. His son Lawrence and daughter Abbey, the tenth generation, have widened the product line.
   Source: https://southernladymagazine.com/?p=10120 (summary): "With the help of a tenth generation of Wagner farmers, son Lawrence and daughter Abbey, the farm has expanded its product line."
7. Mississippi's 2022 Swisher Sweets/Sunbelt Ag Expo Farmer of the Year.
   Source: https://extension.msstate.edu/news/feature-story/2022/longtime-rice-producer-named-mississippi-farmer-the-year (summary): "named Mississippi's 2022 Swisher Sweets/Sunbelt Ag Expo Southeastern Farmer of the Year."
8. Sold to food service through US Foods.
   Source: research/gap-sourcing-live-2026-10-03.md section 6.

Brennan's link: the menu prints "Two Brooks Farm Rice Grits" on the Blackened Tofu. Mississippi, not Louisiana.

The farm sits in the Mississippi Flyway, and the ducks and geese that arrive after harvest clear stubble and fertilise the fields.
   Source: research/gap-sourcing-live-2026-10-03.md section 6 (farmprogress.com and thepacker.com snippets).

Expert knowledge: rice grits are the broken pieces of grain that crack in milling.

## 4. Mushroom Maggie's Farm (Maggie's Mushrooms side; link inferred)

1. Owned and run since 2018 by the wife and husband Maggie Long and Cyrus Lester, in St Francisville, Louisiana.
   Source: https://www.wafb.com/2020/01/23/showcasing-louisiana-mushroom-maggies-farm (summary): "Maggie Long and Cyrus Lester are the wife and husband duo who have owned and operated Mushroom Maggie's Farm since 2018."
2. Grows 1,200 to 1,500 pounds a week, at least eight kinds, including lion's mane, oyster, pink trumpet, shiitake and chestnut.
   Source: https://www.wbrz.com/news/2-your-town-st-francisville-local-mushroom-farm-supplies-fresh-fungi-across-southeast-louisiana (summary): "grows and sells up to 1,200 to 1,500 pounds of mushrooms every week and cultivates ... at least eight different kinds like lion's mane, oyster, pink trumpet, shiitake and chestnut mushrooms."
3. Supplies about twenty top New Orleans restaurants; Maggie Long often works directly with chefs as they plan menus.
   Source: https://countryroadsmagazine.com/cuisine/Louisiana-foodways/mushroom-maggie/ (summary): "Mushroom Maggie's Farm now supplies fungi to twenty top New Orleans restaurants and many more closer to home, Long frequently collaborating directly with chefs as they plan their menus."
4. After the first year a fire destroyed the operation and left Cyrus Lester with burns over seventy percent of his body; they rebuilt over the following year.
   Source: same countryroadsmagazine.com page (summary): "a fire destroyed the entire operation and left Lester with burns over seventy percent of his body. They started again at ground zero and spent the next year rebuilding."

5. Sells to restaurants and farmers markets in St Francisville, New Roads, Baton Rouge, New Orleans and the Northshore; St Francisville lies upriver from Baton Rouge.
   Source: research/gap-sourcing-live-2026-10-03.md section 3 (wafb.com, wbrz.com, inregister.com snippets); https://www.theadvocate.com/baton_rouge/entertainment_life/food_restaurants/maggies-mushrooms-food-news/article_ee199280-ebb0-4b01-8280-a8e2d31a540f.html (summary): "sells 1,000 lbs per week of specialty fungi to high end restaurants in Baton Rouge and New Orleans."

## 5. Crystal hot sauce and Baumer Foods (Popcorn Shrimp)

1. In 1923 Alvin A. Baumer bought Mill's Fruit Products, a small snow-cone syrup company on Tchoupitoulas Street, with a loan from his future in-laws.
   Source: https://www.theadvocate.com/gambit/new_orleans/news/blake_pontchartrain/article_081c9c8f-c890-5f74-a4ed-c7009a69b4b7.html and https://bizneworleans.com/inside-the-industry-food-beverage/ (summary): "In 1923, Alvin A. Baumer took a loan from his future in-laws ... purchased a small New Orleans snow-cone syrup company on Tchoupitoulas Street called Mill's Fruit Products."
2. The purchase included a recipe labelled "Crystal Pure", which he renamed Crystal Pure Louisiana Hot Sauce.
   Source: same (summary): "Tucked away in a drawer of the Mill's building was a recipe for a spiced pepper sauce, which was labelled 'Crystal Pure.' Baumer renamed it Crystal Pure Louisiana Hot Sauce."
3. Made from aged red cayenne peppers, distilled vinegar and salt; reddish orange with medium heat.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Hot_Sauce (snippet): "Made from aged red cayenne peppers, distilled vinegar, and salt, Crystal Hot Sauce is reddish orange with medium heat."
4. Baumer once made jellies, preserves and mustard; Hurricane Katrina in 2005 destroyed the New Orleans plant and the company moved upriver to Reserve, Louisiana.
   Source: https://scenic98coastal.com/posts/crystal-hot-sauce-pepper-baumer-new-orleans-la and https://culinarylore.com/food-history:crystal-vs-louisiana-hot-sauce/ (summary): "Hurricane Katrina in 2005, which destroyed their original New Orleans facility. They relocated to Reserve, Louisiana." And https://www.hotsauceblog.com/... (summary): "Hurricane Katrina forced Baumer Foods to shed its historic lines of fruit preserves, jellies, and yellow mustard."
5. The Reserve factory is 196,000 square feet; Reserve is 9 feet above sea level.
   Source: https://bizneworleans.com/crystal-hot-sauce/ (summary): "the company's 196,000-square-foot factory in Reserve, La. ... relocating the manufacturing facilities upriver to Reserve, Louisiana, which is 9 feet above sea level."
6. Still family owned, led by Alvin "Al" Baumer Jr.; ships over 3 million gallons of hot sauce a year.
   Source: https://scenic98coastal.com/posts/crystal-hot-sauce-pepper-baumer-new-orleans-la (summary): "'Al' A. Baumer, Jr. serves as CEO of Baumer Foods, Inc. ... which annually ships over 3 million gallons of hot sauce worldwide."

Brennan's link: the menu prints "Crystal Gastrique" on the Popcorn Shrimp. Nothing more.

Expert knowledge: Crystal reads more vinegary and less fiery than many hot sauces; describe it as tangy first.

## 6. Abita Brewing Company (Shells & Cheese)

1. Founded in 1986 by Jim Patton and Rush Cumming in downtown Abita Springs, Louisiana; the first beer debuted on 4 July that year.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abita_Brewing_Company (snippet): "Abita was founded by Jim Patton and Rush Cumming in 1986 in downtown Abita Springs, Louisiana. The first Abita Beer debuted on July 4 that same year in New Orleans and Mandeville, Louisiana."
2. Described as the first craft brewery to open in the South.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Patton_(brewer) (summary): "Abita Brewing Company was the first craft brewery to open in the South."
3. Patton wrote the recipes for the flagships including Amber, Turbo Dog, Purple Haze and the root beer, and sold his share in 1997.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Patton_(brewer) (summary): "Patton's legacy continued in the recipes for Abita's flagship beers: Purple Haze, Turbo Dog, Amber, Andygator and Abita root beer. Patton sold his share in the Abita Brewing Company in 1997 (though another source mentions 1998)."
4. Amber was the brewery's first beer: a Munich-style lager of pale and caramel malts with German Perle hops, 4.5 percent, 17 IBU.
   Source: https://www.beerandbrewing.com/review/amber (summary): "Abita Amber is a Munich style lager brewed with pale and caramel malts and German Perle hops. It has a smooth, malty, slightly caramel flavor ... Abita Amber was the first beer offered by the brewery ... 4.5% ABV and an IBU ... rating of 17."
5. In April 2015 Abita became a founding partner of Enjoy Beer, a venture set up by Harpoon's co-founder Rich Doyle; president David Blossman did not disclose terms.
   Source: https://brauwelt.com/en/international-report/the-americas/622873-abita-brewery-sells-stake-to-private-equity-backed-craft-venture and https://www.brewbound.com/?p=47491 (summary): "Abita Brewing Co. became a founding brewery partner of Enjoy Beer ... in April 2015 ... set up by Harpoon Brewery's co-founder and former CEO Rich Doyle." "David Blossman, president of Abita Brewing Co., would not confirm whether ... Friedman Fleischer & Lowe acquired an ownership interest."

Expert knowledge: Abita Springs is in St Tammany Parish, on the Northshore of Lake Pontchartrain, across the lake from New Orleans.

Brennan's link: the menu prints "Abita Amber" in the Shells & Cheese; the guide notes the alcohol is mostly but not entirely cooked off. The dessert list also uses Abita Root Beer (Amarula float).

Not given to servers: a retailer's claim that Abita's well reaches over 3,000 feet into water more than 2,000 years old (totalwine.com listing only).
