## 1. Domaine Leflaive, Puligny-Montrachet (five wines, from Mâcon-Verzé to Chevalier-Montrachet)

1. The family's roots in 1717: "The earliest roots of the domaine goes back to 1717 and a Claude Leflaive in Puligny-Montrachet." Early on, the family worked as coopers and sold grapes from a few hectares of vines (summary).
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domaine_Leflaive (search 1).
2. Joseph Leflaive, 1870 to 1953: trained as an engineer and helped design the first French submarine before returning to Puligny; "it was in 1920 that Joseph Leflaive founded the winery as we know it today." Another account places it in the 1920s, when he began replanting the family vineyards; Leflaive's own site says he bought vines and buildings in the 1910 to 1930 period; a third says he founded the modern domaine in 1905 by buying 25 hectares of phylloxera-ravaged vineyards (summary).
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domaine_Leflaive and https://www.liv-ex.com/?p=38892 and https://www.leflaive.fr/en_US/a-emotion-for-4-generations (search 1, summary) and https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/domaine-leflaive/ (search 2, summary).
3. After phylloxera he replanted and expanded the vineyard and began selling wine under the family label; the company's own timeline records first bottles exported to the United States in 1932 to 1939 (summary).
   Source: https://www.leflaive.fr/en_US/a-emotion-for-4-generations (search 1, summary).
4. The next generation: "Joseph's sons Vincent and Jo ran the estate after his death." Anne-Claude, Vincent's daughter, led the shift to biodynamics (summary).
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domaine_Leflaive and https://www.thewinesociety.com/product/domaine-leflaive-puligny-montrachet-premier-cru-les-pucelles-2023-en.aspx (search 1, summary).
5. Anne-Claude Leflaive: she "co-managed the domaine from 1990 and became sole director in 1994"; Wine Spectator reports she died on 6 April at the age of 59 ("Burgundy's Anne-Claude Leflaive Dies at 59"); other sources give her tenure as 1990 to 2015 (summary).
   Source: https://www.winespectator.com/articles/burgundy-s-anne-claude-leflaive-dies-at-59-51449 and https://www.idealwine.com/fr/corporate/presse-articles/20191216-thedrinksbusiness.pdf and https://www.millesima-usa.com/producer-domaine-leflaive.html (search 4, summary).
6. Biodynamics: the work began with experimental treatments on one hectare in 1990; the full conversion is dated 1997 by Wilson Daniels and Millesima, 1998 by Wine Spectator ("all vineyards were biodynamic by 1998") and 1996 by one trade article (summary).
   Source: https://www.championwinecellars.com/products/2021-domaine-leflaive-puligny-montrachet and https://wilsondaniels.com/?p=1976 and https://www.millesima-usa.com/producer-domaine-leflaive.html and https://www.winespectator.com/articles/burgundy-s-anne-claude-leflaive-dies-at-59-51449 (search 4, summary).
7. The next head: "Brice de la Morandière, Joseph's great-grandson, became managing partner after Anne-Claude died in 2015" (summary); search 4 calls him her nephew. A dated fact, which can change.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domaine_Leflaive (search 1, summary) and https://www.millesima-usa.com/producer-domaine-leflaive.html (search 4, summary).
8. The Mâconnais: "the vines have been cultivated biodynamically since their purchase in early 2004"; the 2004 vintage was the first harvest. Per Wilson Daniels, the sellers were an older couple with no heirs, seeking to retire; "Pierre Morey and Dominique Lafon had recommended the site." With no winemaking facilities at Verzé at first, the grapes were vinified in the Puligny-Montrachet cellars. Leflaive's product page gives 9 ha 33 ares in the commune of Verzé in five parcels; another page gives 14.65 hectares; a later news page gives 37 hectares in all through successive acquisitions (sizes undated, so not used as present facts).
   Source: https://www.leflaive.fr/en_US/wine/macon-verze-blanc and https://wilsondaniels.com/?p=3886 and https://issuu.com/corneyandbarrowltd/docs/2023_domaine_leflaive_release_single and https://leflaive.fr/en_US/news/news-15 (search 3, summary).
9. Sous le Dos d'Ane: the estate's sheet describes a single parcel of about 4 acres (1 ha 62 a), planted in 1995 and 2004; "Pinot Noir grew on the site until 2002, and the wine was then called Blagny Premier Cru instead of Meursault"; the name refers to the climat's position just below a small mound to the west, going up the hill (summary). No acquisition date was found.
   Source: https://images.salsify.com/images/a0tk2qagcflkoqjwzbyv/LF_2024_Meursault_SousDosAnne_FactSheet.pdf and https://wilsondaniels.com/wine/domaine-leflaive/meursault-premier-cru-sous-le-dos-d-ane/ (search 2, summary).
10. Chevalier-Montrachet: one source lists the domaine as holding grand cru plots across all four Puligny grands crus, including Chevalier-Montrachet, with no parcel detail (summary). The Codex library (p-dom-leflaive) names Chevalier-Montrachet "The estate's grand vin, from the steep upper slope", with Bâtard-Montrachet, Bienvenues-Bâtard-Montrachet and a small parcel of Le Montrachet acquired in the early 1990s; research/dossier-the-cellar.md gives 1991 for that parcel (expert, not re-checked here).
    Source: https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/domaine-leflaive/ (search 4, summary) and sommelierscodex/js/data-producers.js (p-dom-leflaive).
11. The negociant with the same name: the Codex keeps Olivier Leflaive, "A negociant house in Puligny founded by Olivier Leflaive" in 1984, apart from the domaine; the wine list keeps Leflaive & Associés ("made by the Domaine Leflaive team from partner growers' grapes") and Clau de Nell (Anjou, "owned by the Leflaive family of Burgundy") as producers of their own.
    Source: sommelierscodex/js/data-producers.js (p-olivier-leflaive, p-dom-leflaive traps) and the shipped wine list (producers Leflaive & Associés and Clau de Nell).

Expert knowledge: Leflaive is said luh-FLEV, as the pack already prints it (no source found). Chevalier-Montrachet is said sheh-vahl-YAY mohn-rah-SHAY.

Brennan's link: the Mâcon-Verzé is the white Burgundy by the glass, poured by Coravin at $40, and the four bottles climb from the Mâcon-Verzé 2021 at $160 through the village Puligny 2016 and the Sous le Dos d'Ane 2017 to the Chevalier-Montrachet 2015 at $1850; the wine list carries more Leflaive premiers and grands crus beyond these.

## 2. Jean-Philippe Fichet, Meursault (Bourgogne Blanc 2023)

1. The start and the rebuilding: "He began as a grower in 1981 but was forced to rebuild his domaine from scratch in the 1990s, having lost all his best fruit sources, including a piece of Meursault-Perrières, for lack of long-term contracts."
   Source: https://www.rarewineco.com/media/167312/fichet-article.pdf (search 5 and 7).
2. The Perrières loss, a second account: "although he used to have a bit of Perrières, which he lost in 1996 when the owner died" (Jamie Goode).
   Source: https://wineanorak.com/burgundy/JP_Fichet.htm (search 7).
3. The new domaine: "By 2000, he had used carefully negotiated long-term fermage and mètayage agreements to create an extraordinary new domaine, brimming with exceptional sites." A tech sheet: the domaine was assembled "parcel by parcel over two decades" (summary).
   Source: https://www.rarewineco.com/media/167312/fichet-article.pdf and https://www.rarewineco.com/media/183328/france-fichet-tech-sheet-0121.pdf (search 5).
4. The holdings: "Over the years, Jean-Philippe has built up an array of Meursaults, all from lieux-dits. He has no Premiers or Grands Crus in the village, though there's a small parcel of Premier Cru Referts in Puligny." Wine Anorak: most of the vines are in Meursault, "but there are small holdings in Monthelie, Auxey-Duresses and Puligny-Montrachet"; it gives 7 hectares in 18 parcels (undated, not used). A monopole, Meix sous le Château (printed "Miex" in the snippet), with 40 year old vines (summary).
   Source: https://www.rarewineco.com/media/167312/fichet-article.pdf and https://wineanorak.com/burgundy/JP_Fichet.htm (search 5 and 7).
5. Les Chevalières: "Fichet's Chevalières lieu-dit sits on the same slope as the premiers crus, with soil like that of Perrières" (summary).
   Source: https://wineanorak.com/burgundy/JP_Fichet.htm (search 7, summary).
6. Farming: "Viticulture is 'lutte raisonée' (reasoned organic). He eschews chemical fertilizers, herbicides or pesticides. Harvest is by hand."
   Source: https://www.rarewineco.com/media/183328/france-fichet-tech-sheet-0121.pdf (search 5 and 6).
7. The cellars: he settled into his current cellars at Creux du Coche, by the Hôpital de Meursault (summary).
   Source: https://wineanorak.com/burgundy/JP_Fichet.htm and https://www.rarewineco.com/media/167312/fichet-article.pdf (search 5, summary; the tool did not say which).
8. The Bourgogne Blanc: from three parcels in the village of Meursault with an average vine age over 50 years; fermented in stainless steel tanks, then about 12 months in cement tanks, so it is one of the whites not raised in barrel (summary). Inside Burgundy: "for those on a budget, the Bourgogne Blanc Vieilles Vignes is often spectacular at its level"; the Vieilles Vignes is a separate cuvée, aged longer in barrel (summary). The sheets give about 250 cases; another listing 350.
   Source: https://www.rarewineco.com/media/183328/france-fichet-tech-sheet-0121.pdf and https://binends.rarewineco.com/media/184170/france-fichet-tech-sheet-0224.pdf and https://www.insideburgundy.com/producer/jean-philippe-fichet/ (search 6, summary).
9. The style: importers describe the house style as "prioritizing mineral expression, texture, and clarity over richness or overt winemaking influence"; bâtonnage is limited, depending on the health of the lees and the vintage; Jancis Robinson notes that Fichet knocked through a high wall to let cool winter air in, to help the wines fall bright naturally (summary).
   Source: https://www.rarewineco.com/media/183328/france-fichet-tech-sheet-0121.pdf and https://jancisrobinson.com/ja/node/196470 (search 6, summary).
10. Two Fichets: research/master-review-somm-2026-10-04.md and the component wine-story-two-fichets keep Jean-Philippe Fichet of Meursault apart from Domaine Fichet of Igé, whose Château London Mâcon-Igé is the dinner tasting white; whether the families are related stays a lineup question.
    Source: research/master-review-somm-2026-10-04.md and components/wines.json (wine-story-two-fichets).

Expert knowledge: Fichet is said fee-SHAY, as the component prints it, and Jean-Philippe Fichet in full zhahn fee-LEEP fee-SHAY (no source found).

Brennan's link: the Bourgogne Blanc 2023 at $95 is our white Burgundy anchor under $100 and the value bottle for the BBQ lobster; his Rully is on the list beside it.

## 3. Thierry et Pascale Matrot, Meursault (Bourgogne Blanc half-bottle)

1. Joseph Matrot and 1914: "Joseph Matrot, the grandson of vineyard owners in Puligny-Montrachet, settled in Meursault at his wife's family domaine in 1914." Gourmethunters: a family estate founded in 1914 by Joseph Matrot in Meursault (summary).
   Source: https://idealwine.com/en/wine-prices/191991-2015-Bottle-Burgundy-Meursault-Domaine-Matrot-white and https://www.gourmethunters.com/en_GB/wines/Winery_Domaine+Matrot (search 8 and 10).
2. Thierry Matrot and 1976: the Burgundy Report quotes Thierry saying he started at the domaine in 1976; Gourmethunters ties 1976 to the move away from chemical fertilisers when he joined (summary). A further page says "Thierry, Pierre's son, took over the estate in 1976."
   Source: https://www.burgundy-report.com/burgundy-report-extra/10-2015/domaine-matrot-2014/ and https://www.gourmethunters.com/en_GB/wines/Winery_Domaine+Matrot (search 10, summary) and search 8 (summary; page not named).
3. Three structures, two labels: "the domaine comprises 3 domaines: Héritiers du Domaine Joseph Matrot (the grandfather), Pierre Matrot (the father) and Thierry and Pascale Matrot"; Thierry and Pascale buy grapes from the rest of the family; two labels are in production, Thierry et Pascale Matrot and Pierre Matrot (summary).
   Source: https://www.burgundy-report.com/burgundy-report-extra/10-2015/domaine-matrot-2014/ (search 10, summary).
4. The name on the label: the estate was known as Domaine Pierre Matrot before 2009, and "from 2009 all the wines, including older stocks, leaving the cellars will come under the name of Thierry and his wife Pascale."
   Source: search 8 (https://www.bbr.com/producer-2774-domaine-thierry-and-pascale-matrot and https://insideburgundy.com/producer/thierry-pascale-matrot; the tool did not say which).
5. The daughters: "In 2008, after obtaining viticulture qualifications, Elsa joined her parents at the Domaine. Two years later, after achieving her own impressive wine business credentials, Elsa's sister Adèle arrived." "Sisters Adèle and Elsa Matrot took over from their father, Thierry Matrot, in the 2016 vintage." Elsa holds a university degree in oenology; Adèle a master's degree in wine business, and is also an oenologist.
   Source: search 8 (https://www.bbr.com/producer-2774-domaine-thierry-and-pascale-matrot, https://www.corneyandbarrow.com/domaine-matrot-en-primeur-2022 and https://www.grandcruwijnen.nl/en/2021-domaine-matrot-meursault; the tool did not tie each passage to one page). Idealwine: the sisters Adèle and Elsa Matrot joined their parents Thierry and Pascale (search 10, summary).
6. Farming: "The vineyards have been farmed organically since 2000, though are not certified as such."
   Source: https://www.bbr.com/producer-2774-domaine-thierry-and-pascale-matrot (search 8 and 9).
7. The cellar: the whites get "a long, slow fermentation in barrel, with little new oak, and the wines are bottled just before the next harvest"; the reds are made without stems (summary).
   Source: https://www.bbr.com/producer-2774-domaine-thierry-and-pascale-matrot (search 9, summary).
8. The range: Meursault-Blagny 1er Cru, Meursault-Charmes 1er Cru, Meursault-Perrières 1er Cru and the Puligny-Montrachet premiers crus Les Chalumeaux and Les Combettes (summary).
   Source: https://insideburgundy.com/producer/thierry-pascale-matrot and https://mwwines.com.au/museum-wine-shop/about-us/news-offers/news-item/2023/03/24/wines-of-poise---thierry-et-pascale-matrot (search 9, summary).
9. At Brennan's: the dinner tasting page indexed online pairs the BBQ lobster with Domaine Matrot Meursault 2023, where the printed guide names Fichet Mâcon-Igé 2024.
   Source: research/second-pass.md (chef-dishes-a-7) and research/summary-keyfacts.md.

Expert knowledge: Matrot is said mah-TROH, and Thierry et Pascale Matrot tee-ay-REE ay pas-KAL mah-TROH (no source found).

Brennan's link: the Bourgogne Blanc 2022 half-bottle, $45, is the half-bottle for the crab claws and suits one or two guests; the wine list adds the family's Meursault Charmes and Perrières and two light reds.

## 4. Raymond Dupont-Fahn, Tailly, Meursault (Les Tillets 2022)

1. Where: the domaine "is based in Tailly, just south of Meursault proper, and is now run by Raymond Dupont-Fahn in its fifth generation" (one retailer, summary); another page: "The Domaine Dupont-Fahn is based in Tailly, in the commune of Meursault." Hachette: "Ce producteur exploite 9 ha de vignes. Il est installé à Tailly, petite localité pittoresque qui campe dans la plaine." Another Hachette entry: "Un domaine régulier en qualité, établi dans la plaine de Meursault."
