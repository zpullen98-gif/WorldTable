## 1. Tien Dat tofu (closed, with one caveat)

(search: opencorporates.com and opengovus.com, query "Tien Dat" tofu New Orleans) TIEN DAT TOFU LLC is a Louisiana company; one listing gives 5141 Evans Dr, Marrero, LA 70072, another lists Harvey, LA 70058. Both are West Bank addresses in Jefferson Parish, across the river from the French Quarter.

(search: marketumbrella.org Market Morsel, 22 March 2021, and squarespace vendor page "Tien Dat Tofu / VEGGI Farmers Cooperative") The summary printed with the results: Tien Dat is a Vietnamese co-op that makes extra firm tofu with non-GMO soy; the tofu is made fresh weekly from organic soybeans in small batches; the products include extra firm tofu, black mushroom tofu, lemongrass and red chilli tofu, fried versions of both, kimchi and chilli oil; it is part of the VEGGI Farmers Cooperative, a member-owned farmers' cooperative based in New Orleans East; one listing places the production in Westwego, Louisiana; the tofu is sold at SPROUT NOLA markets (Tuesday Uptown, Thursday Mid-City, Sunday City Park). Pagoda Cafe (Toast online ordering) serves Tien Dat lemongrass tofu in tacos.

(search: brennansneworleans.com/menus/dinner/, same query) The summary states that Brennan's blackened tofu entrée uses Tien Dat tofu with rice grits and preserved shiitake mushrooms, which matches the PDF (p28).

(expert, medium) The guide's "made right here in New Orleans" is defensible in the loose sense: the co-op is a New Orleans East organisation and the maker is in the metro area, but the registered addresses are Marrero, Harvey or Westwego, all on the West Bank of Jefferson Parish, not Orleans Parish. A safer sentence is "made across the river by a Vietnamese family co-op". Which of the Tien Dat styles Brennan's buys (plain extra firm is the likely one for blackening) is not in any snippet. Pronunciation was not found; (expert, low) Vietnamese "Tiến Đạt" is roughly "tee-EN daht".

## 2. Grand Isle Jewels oysters (confirmed and extended)

(search: jedco.org, seafoodsource.com, axios.com 25 April 2025, wwltv.com, louisianalife.com, chefsbrigade.org) Grand Isle Jewels is a brand created by the Jefferson Parish Economic Development Commission (JEDCO) with Grand Isle's off-bottom oyster growers, launched in April 2025. It unites small off-bottom oyster brands under one umbrella, with a marketing idea compared to Tennessee whiskey and New York strip. The oysters are raised in cages above the seafloor (floating cages), which keeps them cleaner and suits Grand Isle's storm-scoured seabed and eroding coast. The brand name nods to Grand Isle's pirate legacy and honours the late Jules Melancon, the pioneering farmer who was the first permitted to farm off-bottom oysters there in 2012. Inland Foods tested about 10,000 Grand Isle Jewels products and is expanding distribution across the Southeast and Texas (JEDCO, September 2025).

(pdf p13) The menu prints "Grand Isle Jewel" singular; the brand is "Grand Isle Jewels". The oyster count per order and how the Fresno chilli is preserved remain open.

## 6. Two Brooks Farm (confirmed)

(search: farmprogress.com, world-grain.com, thepacker.com, eatdrinkmississippi.com, southernladymagazine.com, sunbeltexpo.com) Two Brooks Farm is at Sumner, Mississippi, in the Delta, started in 1992 by Mike Wagner; about 2,000 acres of rice and 1,000 of soybeans; a line of 14 products including rice, grits and flour, one of his favourites being the Original Mississippi Midlands Brown Rice Grits; sold in a few dozen retail outlets in Mississippi and Memphis, on Amazon, and through US Foods for food service. The farm sits in the Mississippi Flyway and uses the ducks and geese that arrive after harvest to clear stubble and fertilise the fields. Michael L. Wagner was Mississippi Farmer of the Year 2022 (Sunbelt Expo).

(pdf p28) The guide's tofu block pairs Tien Dat with Two Brooks rice grits; the grits are Mississippi, not Louisiana, which gap-fill-5 already noted.

## 7. The eight brands not searched (expert only)

These are national or regional names with stable public facts. None of the following is a Brennan's statement.

- Creekstone Farms (expert, high): Black Angus beef processor at Arkansas City, Kansas; USDA Prime and Choice programmes; the "Creekstone Farm Prime" on the breakfast hanger (pdf p21) is its Prime grade.
- Vital Farms (expert, high): Austin, Texas, founded 2007 by Matt O'Hayer; pasture-raised eggs from a network of small family farms, with a minimum of 108 square feet of outdoor space per hen; publicly traded since 2020.
- Rohan duck (expert, high): a D'Artagnan proprietary hybrid, crossing Pekin and Mallard lines, raised in New York's Hudson Valley; D'Artagnan (Union, New Jersey, founded 1985 by Ariane Daguin) describes it as a smaller, leaner duck with a thin fat cap and rosy flesh, not a heritage breed. The guide's "specialty duck breed" (p7) should read "a specialty duck raised for D'Artagnan".
- Nueske's (expert, high): Wittenberg, Wisconsin, founded 1933 by R. C. Nueske; applewood-smoked bacon from a family recipe, smoked over applewood embers for about 24 hours. Pronounced NOO-skeez.
- Leidenheimer (expert, high): Leidenheimer Baking Company, New Orleans, founded 1896 by George Leidenheimer from Deidesheim, Germany; the city's dominant po'boy French bread baker, still family run, on Simon Bolivar Avenue. Pronounced LY-den-hy-mer.
- Abita Amber (expert, high): Abita Brewing Company, Abita Springs (St Tammany Parish, Northshore), founded 1986; Amber is its flagship Munich style lager, about 4.5 percent, brewed with artesian water.
- Tullamore Dew (expert, high): Irish whiskey from Tullamore, County Offaly, first made 1829; the DEW initials are Daniel E. Williams, the distillery manager who became owner; owned by William Grant & Sons since 2010, with a new Tullamore distillery opened 2014; a triple blend of pot still, malt and grain whiskeys.
- Luxardo (expert, high): founded 1821 by Girolamo Luxardo in Zara (now Zadar, Croatia), rebuilt at Torreglia near Padua after 1947; famous for Maraschino liqueur and the Luxardo maraschino cherry, a marasca cherry candied in its own syrup with no artificial colour. (pdf p40) The guide says the Cherries Jubilee uses a "Luxardo sauce"; which Luxardo product (the Maraschino liqueur, the cherry syrup, or the Sangue Morlacco cherry liqueur) is not printed and stays open.
