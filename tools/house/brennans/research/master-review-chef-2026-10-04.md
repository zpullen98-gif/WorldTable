# Master review, the kitchen: facts the chef's review adds, 4 October 2026

A top-level research file for the master chef's review of My Menu (rev-chef.json). Every fact the review's
overrides state that the guide, the page snapshots and the earlier research do not already hold is set down
here with how it is known. (expert) is professional knowledge of a classically trained chef and is never a
statement about Brennan's own recipe; (dossier) points at research/dossier-the-kitchen.md, which already
holds the fact with its own source; (page) is a pages/ snapshot. Where a fact matters at the table and no
source holds what Brennan's does, the review files a lineup question instead of a claim.

## Sauces

- (expert, high) Escoffier's Le Guide culinaire (1903) names five mother sauces: espagnole, velouté, béchamel,
  tomate and hollandaise. The first five courtyard turtles carry exactly these names (the must-know "The
  courtyard turtles", from the house press release in dossier-the-kitchen.md section 1).
- (dossier, section 10) The hollandaise family on our menus: hollandaise (Eggs Hussarde, Eggs Owen); béarnaise,
  hollandaise built on a tarragon, shallot and vinegar reduction (the breakfast Creole-Spiced Hanger Steak);
  Choron, béarnaise with reduced tomato (Eggs Sardou); Foyot, béarnaise with glace de viande (the Roasted
  Chateaubriand). Beurre blanc (Redfish Véronique) is the Loire butter sauce, reduced wine, vinegar and shallot
  with cold butter whisked in, and is not a hollandaise. Marchand de vin and bordelaise are the red wine sauces;
  in New Orleans "bordelaise" often means garlic butter (pdf p22, dossier).
- (expert, high) All the egg yolk and butter sauces are warm emulsions held below the heat that scrambles the
  yolk; that is why a hollandaise is never served piping hot and why a kitchen holds it in a warm place, not on
  the flame.
- (expert, high) A gastrique is sugar cooked to caramel and deglazed with vinegar, reduced to a sweet and sour
  sauce. The popcorn shrimp's Crystal gastrique adds the hot sauce to that base (the menu prints only "Crystal
  gastrique"; what else goes in is the kitchen's).
- (expert, high) A coulis is a smooth, sieved purée of fruit (or vegetable) used as a sauce.
- (expert, high) Crème anglaise is a pourable vanilla custard of egg yolks, sugar and milk or cream, cooked
  gently until it coats a spoon.
- (expert, high) A roux is flour cooked in fat. The longer it cooks the darker and nuttier it gets, blond to
  peanut butter to milk chocolate to dark chocolate, and the less it thickens, so a dark gumbo roux is there for
  flavor as much as body.
- (dossier, section 6) The holy trinity is onion, celery and green bell pepper; the phrase was popularised by
  Paul Prudhomme.

## Ingredients and terms

- (dossier, section 5) Okra came to Louisiana from West Africa; the word gumbo comes from ki ngombo, a Bantu
  word for okra.
- (dossier, section 4) A Creole tomato is a vine-ripened tomato from the river parishes below New Orleans,
  Plaquemines and St Bernard above all; LSU released a cultivar named 'Creole' in 1969; the season runs late
  May to early July. So by October the tostada's tomatoes are at the end of their season: check at lineup.
- (dossier, section 6) Lump crab is large body meat, below jumbo lump and above backfin. Claw meat is the darker,
  sweeter meat of the claws.
- (dossier, sections 6 and 7) Rice grits are broken rice cooked creamy like grits (Two Brooks Farm, Mississippi);
  corn grits are coarsely ground corn. The menus carry three grits: the cheddar grits side, the fried pimento
  cheese grit cake on the shrimp and grits, and the rice grits under the blackened tofu.
- (dossier, section 6) Pimento cheese is the Southern spread of cheddar, mayonnaise and pimento peppers.
- (dossier, section 8) Nougat is honey, sugar, egg white and nuts.
- (dossier, section 6) Chili crisp is the Chinese condiment of chili flakes fried in oil with garlic, often with
  soybeans and sesame; pepita is the hulled pumpkin seed; Madeira is the Portuguese fortified wine.
- (dossier, section 6) Sorghum, as the dossier reads the papillote, is an African grain cooked like farro. The
  menu prints only "sorghum", and Louisiana also makes sorghum syrup; which one the kitchen uses is a lineup
  question.
- (expert, high) Brioche is a French bread enriched with eggs and butter. Beurre noisette is butter cooked until
  its milk solids turn brown and smell of hazelnut.
- (expert, high) Tartare is raw beef, hand cut and seasoned, never ground (dossier section 4 gives the Paris
  brasserie build).

## Stories a server can tell, each with its source

- (dossier, section 4) New Orleans barbecue shrimp was born at Pascal's Manale, Uptown, in the mid-1950s, usually
  dated 1954; the name is usually explained by the brick color of the sauce. Both the Louisiana Oysters and the
  Louisiana BBQ Lobster are in that buttery, peppery style.
- (dossier, section 6) Antoine's credits Jules Alciatore with Pompano en Papillote in honor of the Brazilian
  aviation pioneer Alberto Santos-Dumont, the puffed parchment standing for his balloon. Cooking in paper is
  older French practice.
- (dossier, section 6) Sole Véronique is traditionally credited to Escoffier, named for André Messager's operetta
  Véronique; the classic is poached sole with peeled grapes in a glazed sauce.
- (dossier, section 6) Blackening is Paul Prudhomme's technique from K-Paul's Louisiana Kitchen, opened in 1979;
  blackened redfish dates to 1980: fillets dipped in melted butter, coated in a paprika and cayenne blend and
  seared in a dry cast iron skillet heated past smoking.
- (dossier, section 6) Shrimp and grits is a Lowcountry dish, Charleston's breakfast shrimp, made national by Bill
  Neal at Crook's Corner in the 1980s; Brennan's version is Creole, a tomato sauce rather than a bacon and lemon
  pan gravy.
- (dossier, section 6) Antoine's names Eggs Sardou for the playwright Victorien Sardou; 1908 is the date usually
  given, and whether Sardou ever sat at the table is legend, so a server says "named for", not "created for".
- (dossier, section 8) Cherries jubilee is credited to Escoffier for one of Queen Victoria's jubilees; the Diamond
  Jubilee of 1897 fits best. Say "credited to".
- (dossier, section 6) The classic Brennan's Eggs Hussarde sat on Holland rusks with a grilled tomato half; the
  English muffin and the dropped tomato date from the 2014 reopening.
- (expert, high) Hank Williams sang "Jambalaya and a crawfish pie and filé gumbo" in "Jambalaya (On the Bayou)",
  1952. Louisiana's best-known meat pie is the Natchitoches meat pie, a fried half-moon turnover from the old
  river town of Natchitoches (claims.json records the Natchitoches style as expert knowledge too).

## Cooking facts behind the new scenarios (expert, high)

- A roast tenderloin cooked whole comes out with its outer slices and ends more done than its center, so a table
  that wants two temperatures from one Chateaubriand can sometimes be served from different parts of the roast;
  whether this kitchen does so is a lineup question, never a promise.
- A duck breast is served rosy because cooked through it turns grey, dry and livery; a kitchen can cook it further
  on request, and how far this kitchen will go is a lineup question.
- A poached egg is soft by nature; a firmer poach takes longer and changes the dish, so a server asks the kitchen
  before promising it.
