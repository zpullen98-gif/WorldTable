# Producers in the cellar: Chablis, the Mâconnais and two Beaune houses (10 October 2026)

## Method

Thirty-one WebSearch calls, standard mode, 10 October 2026, for the producer group chablis-maconnais of the Sommelier's Codex: Domaine Fichet of Igé, Domaine du Roc des Boutires, Les Héritiers du Comte Lafon, Jean-Paul & Benoît Droin, Domaine René et Vincent Dauvissat, Domaine François Raveneau, Domaine Louis Michel, Bouchard Père & Fils and Louis Jadot. That is three to five a producer: Fichet needed five (the family, the Château London cuvée, the vine age, the name of the parcel, the hamlet), Roc des Boutires four (one returned nothing). A thirty-second and thirty-third search (the list of the twenty-two Pouilly-Fuissé premiers crus of 2020, and the exact Burgundy Report passage on the Parinet purchase) were refused because the shared search budget for the turn was used up, so those two points stand as recorded below and become lineup questions. WebFetch was tried once, on the page that mattered most for the least documented producer (https://wineanorak.com/2024/07/27/discovering-the-maconnais-5-domaine-fichet/), and failed at the network egress ("getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND wineanorak.com"), so every passage below is the search engine's snippet or summary of the page named, not a full read of it. Passages marked "(summary)" are the search tool's paraphrase rather than a verbatim quotation; where the tool did not say which of several pages carried a passage, all are named. Where a quoted page carried a dash, it is written here as a colon, a comma or the word "to".

What could not be confirmed, and is therefore left out of every profile or turned into a lineup question:
- Any source for the pack's line that some Château London vines are "over a hundred years old". The nearest is one Québec listing of the 2018 that names the oldest vine of the place as 86 years old.
- Whether the Fichet family of Igé is related to Jean-Philippe Fichet of Meursault (no search touched it).
- Which year the Fichet domaine counts as its founding: 1976 (Francis Fichet leaves the cooperative) and 1988 (the domaine formed, or Pierre-Yves joins) are both printed.
- Whether En Bertilionne is one of the Pouilly-Fuissé premiers crus of 2020 (the search was refused; Aux Bouthières is, by the importer's sheet). The list prints no Premier Cru for our bottle.
- The year the Parinet family bought Roc des Boutires: 2016 by Burgundy Report and the Wilson Daniels booklet, 2017 by Vinorandum.
- When the Dauvissat label changed from René et Vincent Dauvissat to Vincent Dauvissat, and whether the vines are farmed organically (the Codex) or biodynamically without certification (one merchant).
- The oak regime of our Droin Vaillons 2023 (one merchant gives about a fifth in oak for the 2022).
- The exact generation count at Louis Michel (fifth and sixth are both printed) and the spelling of Guillaume's surname (Gicqueau-Michel and Gicqueteau-Michel).
- Bouchard's first vineyard purchase (1775 in Pommard by the house, 1755 in Volnay by Decanter); the claim, from one site, that Bouchard moved its top parcels to a sister label from the 2023 vintage.
- Which premiers crus go into the Louis Jadot Beaune Premier Cru 2023 we pour, and whether it is a Maison or a Domaine bottling.
- When the Jadot Bacchus emblem was adopted.
- A pronunciation source for any of the nine names. The respellings below are marked expert knowledge.
- Hectares, scores, prices and current ownership are not stated as present facts anywhere below; where a source gives them they are recorded as that source's dated or undated claim.

## 1. Domaine Fichet, Igé, Mâconnais (Château London Mâcon-Igé 2024, the dinner tasting white)

1. The oldest roots: the estate's tourism listing traces the family's winegrowing "back over the generations to the Middle Ages to Stéphus Fichet, who was growing vines as far back as 1408" (summary).
   Source: https://www.burgundy-tourism.com/wine-tasting/fichet and https://www.burgundy-tourism.com/sit/fichet (searches 1 and 8, summary).
2. The modern start: "The modern estate dates to 1976, when Francis Fichet took the risk of creating his own estate after leaving the village cooperative" during a wine crisis (summary). Wine Anorak: their father "began in 1976 with 12 hectares, all of which was sold to the cooperative in Igé" (summary).
   Source: https://www.burgundy-tourism.com/wine-tasting/fichet (searches 5 and 8, summary) and https://wineanorak.com/2024/07/27/discovering-the-maconnais-5-domaine-fichet/ (search 5, summary).
3. The brothers: Wine Anorak reports that "the brothers Pierre-Yves (who looks after viticulture) and Olivier (winemaking) run the current domaine, which was formed in 1988"; Burgundy Report says Pierre-Yves joined their parents in 1988 and Olivier followed two years later (summary). So 1976 and 1988 are both used, one for the father's start, one for the brothers'.
   Source: https://wineanorak.com/2024/07/27/discovering-the-maconnais-5-domaine-fichet/ and https://www.burgundy-report.com/?p=36712 (searches 1 and 5, summary).
4. Where: the estate is in Igé, in the hamlet of Martoret (Burgundy Tourism); a 2015 Burgundy Report note gives the address as 651 route d'Azé, Le Martorêt, 71960 Igé (summary). La Cra and La Frasière are two of the estate's blocks (item 6), not its address, which corrects the dossier's "hamlet of La Cra".
   Source: https://www.burgundy-tourism.com/sit/fichet and https://www.burgundy-report.com/?p=36712 (search 8, summary).
5. Size, undated and not used as a present fact: Wine Anorak 30 hectares, Burgundy Report 34, a SAQ listing 11 (summary).
   Source: searches 1 and 5 (wineanorak.com, burgundy-report.com, saq.com).
6. Farming: Wine Anorak says the estate is HVE-certified sustainable and that only two of its blocks, La Cra and La Frasière, are farmed organically; a retailer labels the Crémant as organic (summary). So never call the Château London organic.
   Source: https://wineanorak.com/2024/07/27/discovering-the-maconnais-5-domaine-fichet/ and https://primalwine.com/products/domaine-fichet-cremant-de-bourgogne-brut (search 5, summary).
7. Château London is a named slope shared by several producers: Hachette calls it a "coteau réputé d'Igé" in a Fichet note, and of Domaine des Roches' own Château London "un des meilleurs lieux-dits d'Igé"; Jean-Claude Boisset bottles a Mâcon-Igé Château London too (summary).
   Source: https://www.hachette-vins.com/guide-vins/les-vins/domaine-des-roches-ige-chateau-london-2005-2009/20092404 and https://www.hachette-vins.com/guide-vins/les-vins/domaine-fichet-ige-la-cra-cuvee-prestige-2000-2003/20032696/ (searches 1 and 6) and https://www.libertywines.co.uk/our-portfolio/BO263-macon-ige-chateau-london (search 6).
8. The name: Boisset's 2021 sheet says Château London "is not a brand name, but a vineyard whose name refers to the fact that its original owner lived in rue Landon in Paris. Over time, 'Landon' became 'London'." The owner is not named. So there is no castle and no English link, and the guest-pleasing story now has a source.
   Source: https://s3.amazonaws.com/boissetcollectionweb-assets/pdfs/tech-sheets/macon-ige_chateau_london_2021.pdf and https://www.boissetcollection.com/brands/jean-claude-boisset/portfolio/macon-ige-chateau-london (search 7, summary with quoted phrases).
9. The Fichet cuvée: Wine Anorak on the 2023 Château London: "about 10% barrel-fermented in older oak and 90% in stainless steel" (summary). A Québec listing of the 2018: "10% in oak barrels for the oldest vine (86 years old) of the place called 'Château London'". One older review gives the parcel as 5 hectares, with long élevage on fine lees, and the lieu-dit "first labelled in 1978" (the tool did not name the page; not used).
   Source: https://wineanorak.com/2024/07/27/discovering-the-maconnais-5-domaine-fichet/ (search 4, summary) and https://divinparadis.kork.ca/qc/en/our-wineries/domaine-fichet/macon-ige-chardonnay-chateau-london-domaine-fichet/2018/917781 (search 6).
10. The hundred-year-old vines: "The search didn't turn up any source describing Château London vines as 100 years old or as centenarian." The pack's "some vines over a hundred years old" and the component's "some vines over a century old" are unsourced; the 86-year figure is the nearest, for the 2018.
    Source: search 6 (summary).
11. Two Fichets: research/master-review-somm-2026-10-04.md and the component wine-story-two-fichets keep Jean-Philippe Fichet of Meursault, whose Bourgogne Blanc 2023 is on the bottle list (w-ekfo9ytb), apart from Domaine Fichet of Igé; Burgundy Tourism also warns of a Fichet in Touraine (summary).
    Source: research/master-review-somm-2026-10-04.md and https://www.burgundy-tourism.com/sit/fichet (search 8, summary).

Expert knowledge: Fichet is said fee-SHAY and Igé ee-ZHAY, as the pack prints them (no source found). Mâcon-Igé is the Mâcon appellation with a village name, the same tier as Mâcon-Verzé and Mâcon-Milly-Lamartine.

Brennan's link: the dinner tasting's white, a 4 oz pour with the Louisiana BBQ Lobster on the $120 pairing; it has no glass price, and whether it can be sold à la carte is a question for the manager.

## 2. Domaine du Roc des Boutires, Solutré-Pouilly, Pouilly-Fuissé (En Bertilionne 2020)

1. The name: the importer says it "draws on both terroir and tradition: its proximity with the Roche de Solutré, an emblem of the appellation and a testament to the extraordinary geologic richness of this vineyard; as well as the domaine's settlement in the very heart of one of the best terroirs of the appellation, 'Aux Bouthières.' In the ancient French vocabulary, a 'boutière' was a secondary road that mule skinners used for the transportation of oils and wines in remote parts of the country."
   Source: https://wilsondaniels.com/winery/roc-des-boutires/story and https://wilsondaniels.com/wine/pouilly-fuisse (searches 2 and 12).
2. The estate: "Domaine du Roc des Boutires has 11 acres (4.2 hectares) on an east-west oriented strip, and the vines of Chardonnay, mostly planted in the beginning of the '70s" (the early 1970s); Burgundy Report: "4.2 hectares on an east-west oriented strip of Jurassic limestone" (size undated, not used as a present fact).
   Source: https://images.salsify.com/images/z0d5opezetg3w1he2lkv/RDB 2022 Pouilly-Fuisse Aux Bouthieres Fact Sheet.pdf (search 12) and https://www.burgundy-report.com/?p=55432 (search 2, summary).
3. Aux Bouthières became a premier cru: "Aux Bouthières was one of 22 sites promoted to Premier Cru status in 2020"; the 2021 sheet says the vineyard "was officially classified as Premier Cru in 2020".
   Source: https://wilsondaniels.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/RDB-2021-Pouilly-Fuisse-Aux-Bouthieres-fact-sheet.pdf (searches 2 and 12).
4. The owners, a dated fact: a 2018 Burgundy Report article describes the estate as "a relatively new addition to the stable of wines of the Parinet family, the owners of Château du Moulin à Vent", who "bought the produce of the domaine as must since 2011, and finally managed to buy the domaine itself in 2016". The Wilson Daniels 2024 anniversary booklet for Château du Moulin-à-Vent agrees on 2016; Vinorandum gives 2017. The Parinet family bought Château du Moulin-à-Vent in 2009 (summary).
   Source: https://www.burgundy-report.com/?p=55432 (searches 2 and 9) and https://wilsondaniels.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/CMV_100thAnni_Booklet_FullBook_SnglPgs.pdf and https://www.vinorandum.com/producers/domaine-du-roc-des-boutires (search 9, summary).
5. Where it is made: "Because the domaine has no winemaking facility in the village, the team bring the grapes to Moulin à Vent, which is allowed because Romanèche-Thorins is one of the villages authorized to vinify Pouilly-Fuissé" (summary). The estate lies "in the shadows of the iconic Roche du Solutré", between 210 and 430 metres (summary).
   Source: https://www.burgundy-report.com/?p=55432 (search 9, summary).
6. En Bertilionne: "made only from the 'En Bertilionne' climat, where the estate works a 0.57 hectare plot", listed at 350 m, "the highest terroir in the commune", on limestone with marl banks; exposure "East South East"; vines 45 years old by one sheet, more than 35 by another (summary). The earlier card (bottle-facts-cards-a) gives "a 0.57 hectare east-facing parcel in the heart of Pouilly, facing the Roche de Solutré".
   Source: https://images.salsify.com/images/rqnzlpuiglvqdwghehhl/RDB_2020_Pouilly_Fuissé_En_Bertilionne_fact_sheet.pdf and https://www.moevenpick-wein.com/fr/customization_catalog/product/pdf/product_id/96592/file/2020-pouilly-fuisse-aoc-en-bertilionne-domaine-du-roc-des-boutires.pdf (search 10, summary) and research/bottle-facts-cards-a-2026-10-04.json.
7. The cellar, which varies by vintage: a 2022 sheet gives 15 months, 70% in oak barrels and 30% in stainless steel; a Mövenpick 2020 sheet 17 months in barrel; a 2019 review "a third of the wine aged in barrels" (summary). So "partly in barrel" is the safe phrase.
   Source: https://wilsondaniels.com/wine/en-bertilionne and https://www.moevenpick-wein.com/fr/customization_catalog/product/pdf/product_id/96592/file/2020-pouilly-fuisse-aoc-en-bertilionne-domaine-du-roc-des-boutires.pdf and https://wilsondaniels.com/?p=60757 (search 10, summary).
8. Whether En Bertilionne is itself a premier cru was not settled (the search was refused); the wine list prints none.

Expert knowledge: Roc des Boutires is said rock day boo-TEER and En Bertilionne ahn bair-tee-LYON, as the pack prints them (no source found). Pouilly-Fuissé is Mâconnais Chardonnay; Pouilly-Fumé is Loire Sauvignon Blanc.

Brennan's link: at $90 it is the value white Burgundy for the Louisiana crab claws; the 2020 has some bottle age.

## 3. Les Héritiers du Comte Lafon, Milly-Lamartine, Mâconnais (Mâcon-Milly-Lamartine 2023)

1. The founding: the estate "was established in 1999 when the famed Lafon family of Meursault purchased a small domaine in Milly-Lamartine"; it was bought in September 1999 and sold under the Les Héritiers du Comte Lafon label (summary).
   Source: https://www.bbr.com/products-20181175134-2018-macon-milly-lamartine-les-heritiers-du-comte-lafon-burgundy and https://www.tanners-wines.co.uk/collections/heritiers-du-comte-lafon?page=1 (search 3, summary; and research/producers-cellar-white-burgundy-cote-2026-10-10.md, section 8, item 7).
2. Growth: "Six hectares were added in Uchizy and Chardonnay in 2003, followed by Viré-Clessé in 2009 and Saint-Véran and Pouilly-Fuissé in 2014" (summary). Size estimates run from about 14 hectares early on to 26 or 30 later (undated, not used); the later sources give the vines as "all farmed biodynamically" (summary).
   Source: search 3 (insideburgundy.com, idealwine.info, academievin.org; the tool did not say which, summary).
3. Who runs it, dated facts that can change: "Caroline Gon runs the estate day to day under Dominique Lafon's supervision. Since his retirement at the end of 2021, his daughter Léa and nephew Pierre have taken on larger roles" (summary).
   Source: https://insideburgundy.com/overview/domaine-des-comtes-lafon and https://www.idealwine.info/?p=5205 (search 3, summary).
4. Farming: importer pages describe the Clos du Four parcel as certified organic and farmed with biodynamic methods; "Dominique Lafon ... bought the Milly-Lamartine estate and introduced organic and biodynamic farming there"; harvest by hand (summary).
   Source: https://www.skurnik.com/sku/macon-milly-lamartine-clos-du-four-heritiers-du-comte-lafon/ and https://www.finevines.com/portfolio/les-heritiers-du-comte-lafon/les-heritiers-du-comte-lafon-macon-milly-clos-du-four (search 13, summary).
5. The Clos du Four, the estate's named Milly parcel (not our bottle, which the list prints as plain Mâcon Milly-Lamartine): 1.4 hectares, mid-slope, facing east at about 250 metres, more clay than most of the commune, vines from 1982 to 1985; native yeasts, large barrels (500 to 700 litre demi-muids), nine to ten months on the lees (summary).
   Source: https://beckywasserman.com/domaines/heritiers-du-comte-lafon/macon-milly-lamartine-clos-du-four and https://www.skurnik.com/sku/macon-milly-lamartine-clos-du-four-heritiers-du-comte-lafon/ (search 13, summary).
6. The village: "The name 'Lamartine' was added to the name of the village of Milly in 1902 in honour of the famous poet, who was mayor of the village and owned an estate here." Alphonse de Lamartine's family house there dates to 1705; "The vineyards of Milly-Lamartine occupy the westernmost slopes of the Mâconnais' vineyards" (summary).
   Source: https://www.macon-tourisme.com/en/nos-essentiels/pays-alphonse-de-lamartine/les-sites-lamartiniens/ and https://www.macon-tourisme.com/en/nos-essentiels/pays-alphonse-de-lamartine/ (search 14, summary; the tool did not say which page carried which line).
7. The family behind it: Domaine des Comtes Lafon of Meursault, founded by Jules Lafon after his marriage in 1894; Dominique Lafon in charge from the mid 1980s (1984, 1985 and 1986 are all printed). Tanners calls the Mâconnais estate "the baby brother" of the Meursault domaine (summary).
   Source: research/producers-cellar-white-burgundy-cote-2026-10-10.md (section 8, items 1, 4 and 7) and search 3.
8. The Codex (p-dom-comtes-lafon): "The family also owns Heritiers du Comte Lafon in the Mâconnais", with the trap "Heritiers du Comte Lafon is the family's separate Mâconnais estate". Agrees.
   Source: sommelierscodex/js/data-producers.js.

Expert knowledge: Les Héritiers du Comte Lafon means the heirs of Count Lafon; it is said layz ay-ree-TYAY doo KOHNT lah-FOHN, and Milly-Lamartine mee-YEE lah-mar-TEEN, as the pack prints them (no source found).

Brennan's link: $100, the sweet spot bottle for the Crab & Corn Omelette; the family's Clos de la Baronne Meursault is on the list at $425.

## 4. Jean-Paul & Benoît Droin, Chablis (Vaillons Premier Cru 2023)

1. The family: "Sources agree the family has made wine in Chablis since 1620, and that Benoît is the 14th generation"; one source cites a record of 1547, but "Benoît Droin can trace his winemaking ancestors with certainty only to 1620" (summary).
   Source: https://www.wineaccess.com/winery/domaine-jean-paul-benoit-droin and https://bourgogne-info.eu/html/benoit_droin.html and https://www.bbr.com/products-47987-2016-chablis-les-clos-grand-cru-jean-paul-and-benoit-droin (searches 15 and 17, summary).
2. Father and son: "Jean-Paul joined the estate in 1965 and built its modern reputation. Benoît joined in 1999"; WineWithSeth: Benoît has run the domaine "since 1999 and fully since 2014", when Jean-Paul retired (summary).
   Source: https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/domaine-jean-paul-benoit-droin/ (searches 15 and 17, summary).
3. Oak, then less of it: Jean-Paul "was one of the first to introduce oak aging to the region in the 1980s" (one source); reviewers describe Benoît's cellar changes as "a principal change away from new oak". Tank for the Petit Chablis, Chablis and some premiers and grands crus, 25 to 50 per cent oak for others, at most ten per cent new oak in the grands crus (summary).
   Source: search 15 (bbr.com, insideburgundy.com, burgundy-report.com; the tool did not say which, summary).
4. Five of the seven grands crus: the domaine "Holds five of the seven Chablis Grand Crus: Blanchot, Valmur, Vaudésir, Grenouilles, Les Clos (not Bougros or Preuses)". Total area is unsettled, 13 hectares by Berry Bros, about 26 by WineWithSeth (not used).
