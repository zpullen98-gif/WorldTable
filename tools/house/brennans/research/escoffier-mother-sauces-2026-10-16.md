# Escoffier's four, the kitchens' five: a correction to five house cards, 16 October 2026

Five places in the Brennan's house pack said that Escoffier named five mother sauces, hollandaise among them: the Hollandaise technique card, the story "Escoffier and the mother sauces", the courtyard turtles story ("five for Escoffier's mother sauces"), the lineup must-know on learning the sauces first, and the lexicon term Mother sauces ("Escoffier's five base sauces"). The Cuisine authors (the World Table's French and Creole tab, written the same week) checked the claim and found it is later teaching laid on Escoffier, not his.

## What the sources say

- Auguste Escoffier, Le Guide Culinaire (Paris, 1903). Its chapter on sauces opens with the grandes sauces de base, the leading sauces from which the others are made: espagnole, velouté, béchamel and tomate. Hollandaise appears later, among the smaller sauces, with the warm emulsified sauces. Read in the Wikisource transcription of the 1903 edition (fr.wikisource.org, Le Guide culinaire), confirmed by the mother-sauces author's search of 12 October 2026.
- Auguste Escoffier, A Guide to Modern Cookery (London, 1907), the English translation: Project Gutenberg ebook 71395, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/71395, confirmed by search on 12 October 2026. It is the edition sometimes said to list hollandaise beside the leading sauces; that reading is not confirmed here and the house cards do not lean on it.
- Marie-Antoine Carême, earlier in the nineteenth century, grouped the French sauces under four: espagnole, velouté, allemande and béchamel.
- "The five mother sauces" (espagnole, velouté, béchamel, tomate and hollandaise) is how culinary schools teach the families today, in the twentieth century's American and British kitchen textbooks. It is the list a cook is asked for, and it is not Escoffier's own count.

## What changes on the cards

- Hollandaise: Escoffier set it among the warm emulsified sauces, outside his four base sauces; kitchens now teach it as the fifth mother sauce, with béarnaise, Choron and Foyot its daughters.
- Escoffier and the mother sauces: Escoffier built Le Guide Culinaire (1903) on four base sauces; kitchens now add hollandaise and teach five. The card still asks for the five, the list a cook is expected to know.
- The courtyard turtles: five are named for the French mother sauces as kitchens teach them, not "Escoffier's".
- The must-know on learning the sauces: the five mother sauces, as kitchens teach them; Escoffier's own base sauces were four.
- The term Mother sauces: the five kitchens teach, Escoffier having built on the first four.

Nothing else about these cards changes: the turtles' names, the dishes on each sauce and the house's advice to learn the sauce on each plate first all stand.
