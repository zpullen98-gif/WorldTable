## 1. Pierre Sparr, Alsace (Brut Rosé Crémant d'Alsace NV, by the glass)

1. The family and 1680: "One source says Jean Sparr founded the Sparr winery in 1680. The Profile Wine Group page instead says the origin of the winemaking family Sparr goes back to 1680, as Jean Sparr was born." A second search: "The producer dates its founding to 1680 in Sigolsheim, and says the family has farmed vines there for more than 300 years ... A 2011 Hachette guide entry gives a different founding date, saying the Sparr family has cultivated vines since 1634."
   Source: https://greenegrapewine.com/collections/pierre-sparr and https://profilewinegroup.com/pages/pierre-sparr and https://www.hachette-vins.com/guide-vins/les-vins/sparr-tradition-2008-2011/20114658 (summary).
2. Later generations: "Under the influence of François Pierre Sparr in 1785 the vineyards were increased. Later, Charles Sparr developed the business, increasing the activities on the area of trade and regarding wine ageing."
   Source: https://profilewinegroup.com/pages/pierre-sparr (summary).
3. Pierre Sparr himself: "He took over young, in the early 1900s at the age of 20, and became one of the pioneers of estate bottling in Alsace. His motto was 'invest, progress and maintain', which the house still uses."
   Source: https://profilewinegroup.com/pages/pierre-sparr and https://greenegrapewine.com/collections/pierre-sparr (summary).
4. The war: "During WWII Sigolsheim and its vineyards were completely destroyed. Pierre rebuilt the estate, and his sons René and Charles contributed to the enlargement of the vineyards and the development of the European markets." The regional listing: Pierre Sparr "rebuilt one of the finest and most prestigious estates in Alsace after the Second World War, when Sigolsheim and its vineyards had been destroyed."
   Source: https://profilewinegroup.com/pages/pierre-sparr and https://www.gralon.net/evenements/68/programme-maison-pierre-sparr-successeurs-288259.htm (summary).
5. The 2009 takeover: the Hachette note on the 2008 Sélection de Grains Nobles places it among the last harvests "before the brand was taken over in 2009 by the Beblenheim cooperative cellar (cave de Beblenheim)". The regional listing for "Maison Pierre Sparr Successeurs": "the house was taken over in 2009 by the Cave de Beblenheim"; the house now stands on the Beblenheim cooperative's street. The importer's blog: "the winery is now a cooperative-sourced producer, with fruit coming from member growers." An undated importer page still says "The ninth generation of Sparrs is at the helm of this historic domaine."
   Source: https://www.hachette-vins.com/guide-vins/les-vins/pierre-sparr-selection-de-grains-nobles-2008-2012/20121512/ and https://www.gralon.net/evenements/68/programme-maison-pierre-sparr-successeurs-288259.htm and https://wilsondaniels.com/?p=33873 and https://profilewinegroup.com/pages/pierre-sparr (summary).
6. The cooperative: "The Cave de Beblenheim was created in 1952 near Riquewihr and counts about 170 members. It sells the Baron de Hoen and Heimberger brands." (Member counts differ: 165 growers in another source.)
   Source: https://www.hachette-vins.com/guide-vins/producteurs/12999/cave-de-beblenheim and https://www.lapassionduvin.com/alsace/12878-cave-de-beblenheim and https://profilewinegroup.com/pages/cave-de-beblenheim (summary).
7. The Brut Rosé: "Aging: a minimum of 18 months on the lees prior to disgorgement"; "Filtration: Microfiltration; Bottle Aging: 18 months aging on lees"; a factsheet gives "18 months in stainless steel"; "100% Pinot Noir with 12.5% alcohol"; "salmon pink color; fresh aromas of soft red berries, predominantly strawberry and raspberry."
   Source: https://wilsondaniels.com/wine/cremant-d-alsace-brut-rose-nv/ and https://wilsondaniels.com/?p=8004 and https://images.salsify.com/images/unshahmftwrwfcgch33w/SP_NV_Cremant_D'alsace_Brut_FactSheet.pdf (summary). Earlier: research/second-pass.md and research/verified-facts.json (wilsondaniels.com, wine.com): "Pierre Sparr Crémant d'Alsace Brut Rosé is 100 percent Pinot Noir, traditional method, 12.3 to 12.5 percent; one page gives 14.8 g/l, above the brut ceiling, so say 'dry' rather than a figure."
8. The grand crus: the Schoenenbourg Riesling comes from "100% estate-grown fruit from just three rows of vines", the grand cru stretching "across a steep south and southeast-facing hillside between the villages of Riquewihr and Zellenberg"; the Mambourg "sits on the south-facing slope of Mount Sigolsheim", with records of its wines "as early as the year 783" (producer sheet), "one of the earliest ripening areas due to its southern exposure".
   Source: https://wilsondaniels.com/wine/schoenenbourg-grand-cru-riesling/ and https://images.salsify.com/images/rcna8vz9bnxybuayhwpx/SP_2021_RieslingSchoenGC_FactSheet.pdf and https://wilsondaniels.com/?p=68307 (summary).
9. The appellation rule: research/dossier-the-cellar.md and research/claims.json (expert, high): "Crémant d'Alsace AOC, created 1976, requires second fermentation in bottle and at least nine months on lees, and its rosé may be made only from Pinot Noir." The pack (pdf p41): "Crémant d'Alsace rosé must be all Pinot Noir."

Expert knowledge: Sigolsheim and Beblenheim are villages of the Haut-Rhin, the southern département of Alsace. The name is said pee-AIR SHPAR, Sparr rhyming with car, and Crémant kray-MAHN (the pack's say line and research/gap-fill-5.md).

Brennan's link: the Brut Rosé is the pink bubbles by the glass at $18, at breakfast, lunch and dinner, first pick with the Steak Tartare Cannoli and second choice with the Eggs Sardou, the Eggs Owen and the Poussin à la Moutarde; the list adds the Mambourg and Schoenenbourg grand crus of 2020.

## 3. Schramsberg Vineyards and Mirabelle, Calistoga, Napa Valley (Blanc de Blancs 2021, 375 ml; Mirabelle Brut Rosé NV)

1. Jacob Schram and 1862: "Jacob Schram, a German immigrant and trained barber, bought about 200 acres on Diamond Mountain in 1862 and began planting vines." He had Chinese workers, "many of them former railroad laborers", dig "tunnels dug into the hillside to a depth of about one hundred feet".
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schramsberg_Vineyards and https://www.theclio.com/entry/102459 and https://napahistory.org/?p=5007 (summary).
2. Robert Louis Stevenson: one source says he visited in 1875 and wrote of it in Silverado Squatters; the Napa History site "places his visit in summer 1880, with the book published in 1883", and Vinography gives 1880 too.
   Source: https://napahistory.org/?p=5007 and https://www.vinography.com/2015/12/50_years_of_american_sparkling and https://www.napawineproject.com/schramsberg-vineyards/ (summary).
3. Decline and revival: "Schram died in 1905. In 1912, the winery ceased operation and was sold. In 1965, the property was purchased by Jack and Jamie Davies, who started making sparkling wine by the traditional method. The Davies family still runs the winery today." The caves: a California Historical Landmark in 1956 (Wikipedia; 1957 in another source).
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schramsberg_Vineyards and https://noehill.com/napa/nat1998001251.asp (summary).
4. The first wine and the Toast to Peace: "Blanc de Blancs was the first wine Schramsberg produced in 1965 and was Americas first commercially produced Chardonnay-based brut sparkling wine"; "Schramsberg Blanc de Blancs gained international recognition in 1972 when then President Nixon served the wine at the historic Toast to Peace in Beijing, China"; its sparkling wines "have been served at official State functions by every U.S. Presidential administration since Nixon's 'Toast to Peace' in 1972." Research/components-wines-2026-10-05.md (search 1): "In February 1972 the Schramsberg Blanc de Blancs 1969 was poured at the Toast to Peace in Beijing hosted by Richard Nixon for Premier Zhou Enlai."
   Source: https://www.wine.com/product/schramsberg-blanc-de-blancs-375ml-half-bottle-1993/34996 and https://67wine.com/collections/chardonnay-wine/products/schramsberg-vineyards-blanc-de-blancs-2020-north-coast-750ml (summary).
5. Mirabelle: the multi-vintage brut line "was introduced in 1992, when the brut and brut rosé versions debuted after several years of experimentation"; "a Pinot Noir and Chardonnay blend from cool-climate vineyards in Napa-Carneros, Sonoma Coast, Anderson Valley, and Marin County"; "Since 2022, each release carries a bottling number and highlights the dominant vintage in the blend"; "The 32nd bottling is 77% 2020 vintage with 23% reserve wines"; "A 34th bottling sheet lists a 96% 2022 blend."
   Source: https://wilsondaniels.com/?p=71024 and https://www.schramsberg.com/wp-content/uploads/Mirabelle-Brut-Rose_31st-Bottling.pdf and https://www.schramsberg.com/wp-content/uploads/Mirabelle-Brut-Rose_v20_MP32.pdf and https://images.salsify.com/images/jmd7lwkflq6utfdley2m/Mirabelle_34th_Bottling_Rosé_FS.pdf (summary). Earlier: research/master-review-somm-2026-10-04.md: "Pinot Noir and Chardonnay from cool North Coast vineyards (Carneros, Sonoma Coast, Anderson Valley, Marin), traditional method, two years on its lees. Schramsberg Vineyards shop, Mirabelle Brut Rosé, 34th bottling."
6. The family's red label: the wine list file's line for J. Davies: "The red wine estate of the Davies family of Schramsberg, making Cabernet on Diamond Mountain." The Codex (p-schramsberg): "Schramsberg makes sparkling wine; the still Cabernet trades as J. Davies"; J. Schram is "The tete de cuvee of the house".

Expert knowledge: Schramsberg is said SHRAMZ-berg and Mirabelle meer-ah-BELL (the pack's say lines).

## 4. Cesarini Sforza, Trento, Trentino (Rosé Brut Trento NV)

1.
