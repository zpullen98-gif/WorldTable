# The floor: the formula, the conversations, the facts a server is caught out on

Brennan's, 417 Royal Street, New Orleans. Built for a new server to read on a phone between shifts. Every price is as the server guide printed it from menus read on 26 September 2026; confirm before quoting.

## 0. How to read this

Three labels, every time.

- (pdf) is what the server guide says, with the page.
- (search) is what a search snippet said, with the site named. Live search could not run in this session (the budget was spent), so every (search) line below is quoted from snippets saved earlier in this workflow. Nothing was re-read today.
- (expert) is professional knowledge. It is never a claim about Brennan's specifically.

Four status words.

- Confirmed: the guide and at least one outside source agree.
- Disputed: two sources disagree. Both are given. Ask the house.
- Unverifiable: only the guide, or only memory, says it. Say "confirm" before you repeat it.
- Corrected: a claim that was checked and found wrong appears only in its corrected form.

Punctuation note. Lines quoted from the guide have had their dashes replaced and their spelling made British. Dish names stay as the menu prints them.

## 1. The formula, on one card

(pdf, p2) Five parts, always in this order: main ingredient, technique, sauce and key flavours, accompaniments, how it tastes. Three lengths: 10 seconds at 25 words or fewer (parts 1 and 3, sometimes 5), 20 seconds at 50 words or fewer (parts 1 to 4, ending on flavour), 45 seconds at 110 words or fewer (all five, plus why it is special here, plus a gentle recommendation).

(pdf, p2) Voice rules: first person, present tense, warm and plain. Explain every French or Creole word the moment you say it. Describe what the menu says plus what is generally true of the technique. Never invent a farm, a brine time or a house story. Lead with the dish, end with the taste. Recommend gently. Allergens, rare cooking, bones, heat, alcohol and fire go through your head on every dish.

(pdf, p3) Pairing scaffold: acid, fat, weight, sweetness, heat, tannin, salt and umami, bridge, method and region. Each pairing is written as why, say it, "why this wine?", on the palate, second choice, serve, avoid. Most dishes add a step up and a zero-proof.

(expert, confirmed by arithmetic) The word caps run at about 150 words a minute, which is a normal speaking pace. Train with a stopwatch at 150; at 130 the 45-second line runs six seconds long. Say "about 150 words a minute", not "130 to 150".

(expert) What the card does not carry: steps of service, a wine presentation sequence (the guide gives pour-level notes such as temperature, glass, decanting, Coravin and sediment, but no present, taste, approve, pour order), a table-reading method, a recovery model, and the floor's org chart. The guide itself does not know who performs the flambé (pdf, p49: "ask at training who performs it, server or captain"). Get those four things from the house in week one. Keep the formula exactly as it is; it is the best part of the document.

## 3. The facts a server is caught out on

Short, in the order a guest is most likely to raise them.

1. Owen never served a meal here. (search, Find a Grave; Wikipedia "Ella Brennan") Owen Brennan died of a heart attack in his sleep on Friday 4 November 1955, aged 45. The banks pulled out; Ella found backing; Royal Street opened in spring 1956. The guide omits this (pdf, p50 implies continuity).

2. The house closed and came back. (search, house press release; Fox News; WWL-TV) Building lost at a foreclosure sale in spring 2013, closure 28 June 2013, bought by Ralph Brennan and Terry White, reopened 25 November 2014 after 17 months and an estimated 20 million dollars (search, SeafoodSource). Pip Brennan's court bid to undo the sale failed. The guide omits all of it.

3. Eggs Sardou is an Antoine's dish. (pdf, p18) says only that the name goes back to the playwright Victorien Sardou. (expert) It was created at Antoine's, one block away at Royal and St Louis, with artichoke bottoms, anchovy and hollandaise. Brennan's serves the creamed-spinach version with Choron. Never let a guest infer Brennan's invented it.

4. Eggs Hussarde is the one that is ours. (pdf, p17) undersells it as "the egg dish we're known for". (search, CooksInfo; Southern Foodways Alliance) Paul Blangé created Eggs Hussarde, Bananas Foster and Chicken Pontalba. Medium confidence on the Hussarde attribution; confirm the house wording before saying "created here".

5. Bananas Foster has two tellings. (pdf, p49) Owen challenged Blangé. (search, house Traditions page; Axios 6 April 2026 quoting Ralph Brennan) Ella Brennan and Blangé created it together, named for Richard Foster of the Foster Awning Company, chairman of the crime commission. The house's own page and its current proprietor name Ella. The commission is called the New Orleans Crime Commission (pdf) and the Metropolitan Crime Commission (search, house recipe page); same body. Tell the house version; know the Ella version.

6. The alcohol does not mostly burn off. (pdf, p34) "Flambéing doesn't remove all the alcohol" is right. (pdf, p47) "Most of it burns off" is wrong. (expert) USDA retention data put a flamed dish at roughly three quarters of its alcohol retained. Say "some burns off, most stays".

7. Rum, and maybe banana liqueur. (pdf, p34) The printed menu says rum; Brennan's own published recipe also uses banana liqueur. Follow the house procedure you are trained on.

8. The Sazerac is the city's cocktail, not the state's. (pdf, p39) "New Orleans' official cocktail" is right. (expert) The Louisiana Legislature named it the official cocktail of the City of New Orleans in 2008 after a bid for state status failed. The user's own Codex already says this (codex/js/data-advanced.js).

9. Herbsaint was born here and is made in Kentucky. (pdf, p39, p51) calls it "our local anise liqueur". (expert) Created in New Orleans in 1934 by Legendre and Parker, owned by the Sazerac Company, distilled today at Buffalo Trace in Frankfort. Say "New Orleans-born".

10. Peychaud's pharmacy is a few doors up. (pdf, p39) bright red New Orleans bitters with anise and cherry notes: right. (expert) Antoine Amédée Peychaud's pharmacy is given as 437 Royal Street, same block as 417. The 1830s date is the brand's convention; verify before you point at the building.

11. Which Rare is open. (pdf, p41) Rare Brut 2012 by Coravin at $75; the restaurant's own drinks page lists a 2013 Rare Millésime at the same price. (expert) The Rare blanc vintages known are 2002, 2006, 2008, 2013; Rare Rosé includes 2012. A 2012 blanc is not known. Read the label at lineup. Disputed.

12. The house Champagne is spelt Essentiel. (pdf, p41) prints "Brennan's Essential by Piper-Heidsieck Extra Brut" at $28, with the drinks page at $30. (expert) Piper-Heidsieck's cuvée is Essentiel, Pinot Noir-led with Meunier and Chardonnay, dosage about 5 to 6 g/L. "Very dry" is safer than "bone-dry". Whether a Brennan's-labelled bottling exists is unconfirmed. Quote the price on the list you are handed.

13. Moulin d'Issan is Merlot-led. (pdf, p45) "Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot blend". (expert) From the Château d'Issan team, from vines outside Margaux, typically about two thirds Merlot. Say "a Merlot-led Bordeaux with Cabernet".

14. Which Champagne goes with the Hussarde on the tasting. (pdf, p18, p42, p49) Charles Lafitte Brut, 4 oz. (search, house breakfast tasting page and OpenTable) "Brennan's Essential by Piper-Heidsieck". No saved snippet mentions Charles Lafitte at Brennan's at all. Disputed. Confirm before you name it.

15. The duck has moved with the season. (pdf, p6 to p7) Roasted Rohan duck breast, cane syrup-glazed peaches, candied hazelnuts. (search, house dinner tasting page) now "Hazelnut-Crusted Duck: 14-day dry-aged Rohan duck, brown butter and sweet potato purée, local delicata squash, sage, duck jus". The guide warned of a fall change (pdf, p49). Disputed; the live menu wins.

16. Bubbles at Brennan's hours. (pdf, p49) Monday to Friday 2 to 6, sabering Fridays at 5. (search, undated, probably an older Tripadvisor review) Tuesday to Friday 2 to 7. The guide is the newer reading. Confirm on shift.

17. Two Brooks Farm is in Mississippi. (pdf, p27) names the rice grits without a state. (expert) The Wagner family farm at Sumner, in the Mississippi Delta. Do not say "Louisiana rice grits" by analogy with the popcorn rice.

18. Marchand de vin: pick one wording. (pdf, p2) "a red wine and mushroom sauce"; (pdf, p17, p19, p50) "a red wine reduction" or "a savoury red wine sauce". (expert) The classical sauce has no mushrooms; Brennan's published recipe has mushrooms, ham and onion. Ask the kitchen which it serves, then say one thing.

19. Kitchen lineage, with one correction. (search; expert) Blangé 1946 to 1977; Michael Roussel, who started in 1956, trained under Blangé, rose from dishwasher to executive chef and died in 2005 still in post after 49 years (he did not retire); Lazone Randolph, dishwasher from 1965, chef 2005 to the 2013 closure, the man behind Chicken Lazone; Slade Rushing 2014 to 2019; Ryan Hacker from November 2019 (announced February 2020) to spring 2025, with Texas roots, the first chef promoted from within since the reopening; Kris Padalino from June 2025. Every one but Rushing came up inside the building.

20. The kids' menu exists and the guide does not have it. (pdf, p51) The sources list omits brennansneworleans.com/menus/kidsmenus/. Nobody read it this session. Ask the host stand for the card.

21. The shellfish script clears dishes too early. (pdf, p47) names the Eggs Hussarde and Duck Confit Waffle as "great choices" in the same breath as "I'll let the kitchen know", and its skip list leaves out the Louisiana Oysters (p10) and the Shrimp & Grits (p20). Name every dish the menu lists with shellfish, then let the kitchen clear the choice. See the rewritten scenario in section 8.

22. Pronunciations a local judges you on. (pdf, p50 to p51) all usable. Refinements (expert): pralines are PRAH-leen, never PRAY-leen (the guide's PRAW-leens on p35 is the same sound); Choron sho-RAWN with a nasal ending; beurre blanc is burr BLAHN, no k; Herbsaint is heard as ERB-saint and ERB-sant; Hussarde's H is silent in French but hoo-SARD is what New Orleans floors say.

(pdf, p49) Breakfast and lunch Monday to Friday 9 to 2, Saturday and Sunday 8 to 2. Dinner nightly 6 to 10. (search, house About page) the same, plus Roost Bar and courtyard 9 am (8 am weekends) to 10 pm. Status: as printed; not live-checked this session. Holiday and festival hours unknown.

(pdf, p50) "Dressy casual; jackets preferred at dinner. No athletic or cut-off shorts, sleeveless or decal T-shirts, hats, or open-toed shoes for men." (pdf, p49) Tasting portions and every listed drink included. Order: Brandy Milk Punch; baked apple; turtle soup or seafood gumbo with a Bloody Bull; Eggs Hussarde with a 4 oz Champagne pour; petite filet with Domaine de Châteaumar "Cuvée Vincent" Côtes du Rhône, 4 oz; Bananas Foster tableside with Congregation Coffee & Chicory. (search, OpenTable, older) an earlier page paired the filet with Stéphane Ogier "La Rosine" Syrah; both the guide and the live page say Châteaumar; the Ogier line is stale.

### Dinner tasting, $80, pairing $120
(pdf, p49) Grand Isle Jewel oysters; Louisiana BBQ lobster; Redfish Véronique; Rohan duck; The Snickers. ### Bubbles at Brennan's
(pdf, p49) Roost Bar and Courtyard, Monday to Friday 2 to 6. Sabering Fridays at 5. Rosé bottles $50 to $125 (Mirabelle to Pommery). Bubbly cocktails $12 to $20 (Brennan's Champagne Cocktail $20). (search, older snippet) a bottle list of Henriot, Taittinger and Dom Pérignon 2015, which has clearly moved on. Hours disputed (item 16). Sabering is a bar-team ritual; never attempt it yourself.

### The 80th year
(pdf, p50) Opened 1946, at 417 Royal since 1956, 80 years in 2026 with anniversary tasting menus through the year. (search, house 80th Kick Off page) Friday 2 January 2026 in the courtyard, with WDSU reviving its 1950s live noonday broadcasts. ### Rooms
(search, Restaurant Hospitality; house Chanteclair Room page) The Chanteclair Room, about 100 seats, made by removing four smaller rooms, an orangerie fantasy opening to the courtyard through glass; designer Richard Keith Langham. The Morphy Room, an upstairs parlour. (search, house Wine Room page; Private Dining Guide) The Wine Room sits in the old carriage house and stables beside the cellar: brick arches, timber beams, a merlot-coloured stone floor, a 16-foot table milled from one sinker cypress out of a Mississippi bayou, sixteen leather chairs. Room names in daily use, capacities and minimums: ask the events team.

### Kids, reservations, parking, private rooms
All unverifiable this session. (pdf, p50) only says guests should note allergies at the reservation. (expert) Online booking through a third-party platform plus phone, with an events office for large parties, is the normal pattern; nothing here confirms it for Brennan's. ## 5. Dish by dish

Format: one breath (the guide's 10-second line, adapted), the story, confirmed, ask. Prices are B (breakfast and lunch), D (dinner), T (tasting), as printed 26 September 2026.

### Tasting courses

#### Baked Apple (T, breakfast, first course)
One breath: "Our first course: a warm baked apple with oatmeal, pecan and raisin crumble, brown sugar glaze and sweetened crème fraîche." (pdf, p4)
Story: a gentle start to a long breakfast; like a warm apple crisp; comes after the milk punch, before the soup. Wine only as an extra: Dr Hermann Erdener Prälat Auslese, $80 half-bottle, which carries through to the Foster.
Confirmed: tree nuts, dairy. Ask: whether the oats carry gluten.

#### Petite Filet Mignon (T, breakfast, fourth course)
One breath: "A petite filet mignon with potato rösti and garlic spinach, served with a Côtes du Rhône." (pdf, p5)
Story: the last savoury course of a grand New Orleans breakfast; rösti (RUHSH-tee) is a Swiss shredded potato cake; the 4 oz Châteaumar is juicy and soft, right for red meat in the morning. Step up: Inglenook Rubicon 2010. Zero-proof: Black Hills.
Confirmed: likely dairy in the rösti. Ask: take the temperature with the tasting order, not when the course fires.

#### Louisiana BBQ Lobster (T, dinner, second course)
One breath: "Louisiana BBQ lobster with a white bean stew and 'nduja, a spicy Calabrian sausage." (pdf, p5)
Story: New Orleans barbecue is not smoke or tomato; it is the buttery, peppery style made famous by barbecue shrimp (expert: Pascal's Manale, 1950s). 'Nduja (en-DOO-yah) melts into the beans. Pour: Fichet Mâcon-Igé 2024, 4 oz, just before the course lands.
Confirmed: shellfish, pork, dairy, moderate heat. Ask: the kitchen's BBQ sauce, at lineup.

#### Roasted Rohan Duck Breast (T, dinner, fourth course)
One breath: "Roasted Rohan duck breast with cane syrup-glazed peaches and candied hazelnuts." (pdf, p6)
Story: duck and fruit is a French classic; cane syrup is Louisiana's dark sugarcane syrup; Rohan is a speciality duck (expert: a hybrid raised for D'Artagnan, so "proprietary", not "breed"). Pour: Paul Hobbs Coombsville Cabernet 2021, opened 30 to 60 minutes ahead.
Disputed: (search, house dinner tasting page) the course is now hazelnut-crusted, with brown butter and sweet potato purée, delicata squash, sage and duck jus. Re-learn the lines from the live card. Ask: the kitchen's doneness standard (usually medium-rare to medium); tree nuts.

### Starters

#### Blackberry Trifle (B $14)
One breath: "A sweet start: blackberry trifle with coconut chia pudding, lemon curd and blackberry financiers." (pdf, p7)
Story: curds layered with chia pudding and small buttery almond cakes; tart more than sugary; put it in the middle of the table. Wine: the Auslese half-bottle, about four dessert pours; second: Pierre Sparr rosé; zero: Catalina Island.
Confirmed: coconut, eggs, dairy, gluten, chia's seedy texture. Ask: whether the financiers are almond-based.

#### Framboise French Toast (B $16)
One breath: "Framboise French Toast: our citrus brioche with mascarpone mousse, raspberry coulis and local raspberries." (pdf, p8)
Story: framboise (frahm-BWAHZ) is raspberry; mascarpone is a rich Italian cream cheese whipped into a light mousse (the guide's "light whipped cream cheese" has the whipping on the wrong noun). Wine: Auslese; zero: chicory coffee.
Confirmed: gluten, eggs, dairy. Listed as a starter; upsell it to share.

#### Café Brûlot Coffee Cake (B $14)
One breath: "Chicory coffee and bittersweet chocolate cake, pecans, spiced citrus glaze and whipped crème fraîche." (pdf, p9)
Story: named for café brûlot (broo-LOH), the flamed brandy coffee with citrus peel, cinnamon and clove (expert: an Antoine's tradition). Wine: Banyuls, if the house pours it at breakfast.
Confirmed: tree nuts, gluten, eggs, dairy, caffeine. Ask: can Banyuls be poured à la carte at breakfast.

#### Louisiana Oysters (B $19)
One breath: "Louisiana oysters in New Orleans BBQ sauce, with pickled collards, shallots and a fried saltine crumble." (pdf, p10)
Story: cooked, not raw; rich and peppery, a sharp pickled bite, crunch on top. Wine: Gainey Chardonnay $17; second: the house Piper; step up: Leflaive Mâcon-Verzé by Coravin; zero: Black Hills.
Confirmed: shellfish, dairy, gluten; Worcestershire traditionally contains anchovy. Ask: how they are cooked and plated, and how many per order.

#### Creole Tomato Tostada (D $18)
One breath: "Louisiana Creole and heirloom tomatoes with creamy burrata on a housemade okra tostada, with smoked tomato water." (pdf, p11)
Story: Creole tomatoes are Louisiana's summer crop; gone when the season turns. Wine: Minuty rosé $17; second: Durand Sauvignon Blanc; zero: Catalina Island.
Confirmed: dairy; seasonal. Ask: vegetarian confirmation; gluten in the okra tostada.

#### Louisiana Crab Claws (D $22)
One breath: "Louisiana crab claws in a warm brown butter vinaigrette with fines herbes." (pdf, p12)
Story: finger food; fines herbes (feen ZAIRB) are parsley, chives, tarragon, chervil. Wine: Leflaive Mâcon-Verzé by Coravin $40; second: Durand; zero: Black Hills.
Confirmed: shellfish, dairy. Bring a shell plate and napkins as the claws land.

#### Grand Isle Jewel Oysters (D $22; T dinner, first course)
One breath: "Grand Isle Jewel oysters, served raw with a preserved Fresno chilli mignonette and parsley." (pdf, p12)
Story: Grand Isle is a barrier island on the Louisiana coast; "Jewel" is likely a farm or brand name, unconfirmed. Mignonette (min-yo-NET) is the shallot-and-vinegar oyster sauce. Wine: house Piper $28; second: Albariño; step up: Rare by Coravin; on the tasting, Charles Lafitte 4 oz (pdf).
Confirmed: consumer advisory on raw shellfish, especially for pregnant, immunocompromised or liver-disease guests; Fresno is mild. Ask: count per order.

#### Steak Tartare Cannoli (D $17)
One breath: "Steak tartare piped into a crisp potato cannoli, with crème fraîche and grated cured egg yolk." (pdf, p13)
Story: two or three bites; the yolk is cured and grated like bottarga (boh-TAR-gah). Wine: Pierre Sparr Crémant rosé $18; second: Damien Martin Pinot; zero: Catalina Island.
Confirmed: raw beef and cured yolk, consumer advisory; dairy. Ask: gluten and the shared fryer for the shell.

### Soups and salad

#### Turtle Soup (B $13, D $13; T breakfast second course, or gumbo)
One breath: "Our Turtle Soup: 100 percent turtle meat, brown butter spinach and grated egg, finished with aged Sherry. A Brennan's classic." (pdf, p14)
Story: a rich, finely chopped stew; Sherry at the end cuts the richness; the soup of the Traditional Breakfast. Say "turtle" plainly; many guests are trying it for the first time. Wine: house Piper; second: Leflaive; step up: Rare; zero: Café Glacé de la Maison or Catalina Island.
Confirmed: contains alcohol (Sherry), egg, dairy. Ask: roux and gluten. The courtyard turtles are not in it.

#### Seafood Gumbo (B $13, D $13; T breakfast second course, or turtle soup)
One breath: "Our Seafood Gumbo: shrimp, crab and oysters with smoky andouille, over Louisiana popcorn rice." (pdf, p15)

## 13. For Lizzy, the maître d' agent

Rules that generalise to any house. Hold a dated must-know sheet separate from the menu; the menu changes seasonally, the history does not. ## 14. For the app

What should become structured records, so World Table, the Bartender's Ledger, the Sommelier's Codex and Lizzy read one source. The design principle: one `house` folder per restaurant that a user can make "mine", with every record carrying `house`, `status`, `sources[]` and `lastChecked`, so the same shapes serve any user's house later.

8. `disagreement`: id, house, subject, pdfSays {text, page}, searchSays {text, site, date}, resolution, confirmWith, status.

## Key facts
- Brennan's opened in July 1946 on Bourbon Street and has been at 417 Royal Street since 1956; Owen Brennan died on 4 November 1955 and never served a meal at Royal Street.
- The restaurant closed on 28 June 2013 after a foreclosure and family dispute, and reopened on 25 November 2014 under Ralph Brennan and Terry White after a 17-month, roughly 20 million dollar restoration.
- Bananas Foster was created in 1951 by chef Paul Blange and named for Richard Foster, chairman of the crime commission; the house's own page and Ralph Brennan also credit Ella Brennan, and 2026 is its 75th year.
- Eggs Hussarde is the egg dish that is Brennan's own (medium confidence, credited to Blange); Eggs Sardou was created at Antoine's and must never be presented as a Brennan's invention.
- Kris Padalino has been executive chef since June 2025, the first female executive chef in eight decades, confirmed by Axios New Orleans; one review pass could not verify it, so confirm on day one.
- The Wine Spectator Grand Award ran 1983 to 2005, was lost after Katrina killed about 35,000 bottles, and was regained in 2021 at more than 15,000 bottles; the cellar sits in the old carriage house.
- The Traditional Breakfast is $80 with every drink included; the dinner tasting is $80 with a $120 five-pour pairing; Bananas Foster is $14 with a minimum of two per order (prices as printed 26 September 2026).
- The Champagne poured with Eggs Hussarde on the breakfast tasting is disputed: the guide says Charles Lafitte Brut, the restaurant's own page and OpenTable say Piper-Heidsieck Essentiel.
- The dinner tasting's duck course has moved from peaches and candied hazelnuts to a hazelnut-crusted, sweet potato and delicata squash plate on the live site; the live menu wins.
- Bubbles at Brennan's is Monday to Friday 2 to 6 with Champagne sabering Fridays at 5 per the guide; an older snippet says Tuesday to Friday 2 to 7; hours are not live-verified and must be confirmed on shift.
- Flambeing leaves roughly three quarters of the alcohol in the dish; the guide's p47 line 'most of it burns off' is wrong and p34's wording is right.
- The Sazerac is the official cocktail of the City of New Orleans by a 2008 act of the Louisiana Legislature, not the state's; Herbsaint was created in New Orleans in 1934 but is distilled in Kentucky today.
- Rare Brut 2012 on the live list is probably Rare Rose 2012 or the 2013 Millesime the drinks page lists; the house Champagne is spelt Essentiel and shows $28 on Binwise and $30 on the restaurant's page; Moulin d'Issan is Merlot-led.
- Two Brooks Farm rice grits come from Sumner, Mississippi, not Louisiana.
- The ten courtyard turtles were found during the 2014 renovation and named by Slade Rushing for five French mother sauces and five New Orleans sauces; Cocktail is the only male; the 11th parade was 2 May 2026.
- The kitchen lineage is Blange (1946 to 1977), Michael Roussel (died in post in 2005 after 49 years, he did not retire), Lazone Randolph (to 2013), Slade Rushing (2014 to 2019), Ryan Hacker (2019 to 2025), Kris Padalino (2025 on); all but Rushing came up inside the building.
