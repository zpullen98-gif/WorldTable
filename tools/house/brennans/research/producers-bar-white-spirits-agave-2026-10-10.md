## 1. Chopin (the Flamingo)

1. A single-ingredient vodka, distilled four times from potatoes, rye or wheat, made by Podlaska Wytwórnia Wódek Polmos of Siedlce.
   Source: https://mybartender.com/brands/chopin-vodka/ (summary): "Chopin is a single-ingredient vodka, 4 times distilled from either potatoes, rye or wheat. It is produced by Siedlce-based Podlaska Wytwórnia Wódek Polmos."
2. The distillery traces back to 1896 and was bought by the Dorda family in 1993; the brand says its founder took on a distillery that had made vodka since 1896.
   Source: same (summary): "The distillery traces back to 1896, and was purchased by the Dorda family in 1993." And https://chopinvodka.com/?p=11 (summary): "When our founder, Tadeusz Dorda, bought Chopin he took on a distillery that had been making vodka since 1896."
3. The founding year differs by source: 1993 (Chilled Magazine), 1992 (Flaviar), and a French retailer says the family took over the company in 2003.
   Source: https://chilledmagazine.com/?p=47652 (summary): "Dorda founded Chopin Vodka in 1993." https://flaviar.com/products/chopin-potato-vodka-1750 (summary): "Chopin Vodka was founded by Tad Dorda in 1992." https://www.closdesspiritueux.com/chopin-potato-vodka-polonaise.html (summary): "the family took over the company in 2003 and then restored the distillery."
4. Chopin was first introduced to North America in 1997; Difford's gives 1997 as its launch.
   Source: https://digital.copcomm.com/i/26650/42 (summary): "Chopin was first introduced to North America in 1997." And https://www.diffordsguide.com/beer-wine-spirits/1749/chopin-potato-vodka (summary): "One says it launched in 1992, while Difford's says 1997. The 1997 date matches its arrival in North America."
5. Named after the composer Frédéric Chopin, "a famous composer from the first half of the 1800s and a favored son of Poland".
   Source: https://mybartender.com/?p=50429 (summary): "The brand is named after the composer Frédéric Chopin, and was a famous composer from the first half of the 1800s and a favored son of Poland."
6. Family owned and run, in its second generation; the brand calls itself Poland's last family owned vodka brand.
   Source: https://chopinvodka.com/distillery/ (summary): "a family owned and operated business ... Poland's last family owned vodka brand." And retailer listings (summary): "The producer's own material says the company is 100% family-owned and now in its second generation."
7. Ingredients are grown within 20 miles; the water comes from an artesian spring beneath the grounds; the estate covers 17 acres near the hamlet of Krzesk; its still dates to 1896, "one of the oldest remaining stills in Poland" by the brand's account.
   Source: https://chopinvodka.com/distillery/ (summary): "our ingredients are all grown within 20 miles, and our water comes directly from an artesian spring beneath our grounds." And: "The estate covers 17 acres of farmland near the hamlet of Krzesk in Poland. The still dates to 1896, and the site says it is one of the oldest remaining stills in Poland." Also https://vinepair.com/articles/vp-pro-qa-tad-dora/ (summary): the company farms some of its own ingredients and works with "family-owned farms within a 20-mile radius of our distillery."
8. The potato bottling is made from Stobrawa potatoes grown in Podlasie, by retailers' accounts; Difford's places the potatoes in Mazovia.
   Source: https://unwindbottleshop.com/products/chopin-potato-vodka-750ml and other retailers (summary): "made from Stobrawa potatoes grown in Podlasie, using nothing but that one ingredient. Difford's Guide instead places the potatoes in the Mazovia region."
9. The producer is listed variously as Podlaska Wytwórnia Wódek in Krzesk, Podlaska in Siedlce, and Polmos Siedlce, about 60 miles east of Warsaw.
   Source: retailer listings (summary): "One retailer lists the distillery as Podlaska Wytwórnia Wódek in Krzesk. Another says it is produced by Podlaska in Siedlce. A third names Polmos Siedlce, about 60 miles east of Warsaw."
10. Potatoes per bottle: seven pounds in one source, five kilos in another. Not used.
    Source: https://mybartender.com/brands/chopin-vodka/ and https://www.masterofmalt.com/vodka/chopin/chopin-potato-vodka/ (summary): "the brand uses seven pounds of potatoes for every bottle of vodka produced. Another says five kilos of potatoes go into each bottle."
11. Awards: gold medals from the Beverage Testing Institute in Chicago and double gold at the San Francisco World Spirits Competition. Not used.
    Source: https://chilledmagazine.com/?p=47652 (summary).

Expert knowledge: potato vodka is fuller and creamier in texture than most grain vodkas, with a faint earthiness, and the brand is said SHOH-pan, like the composer (the reading the existing component already uses).

## 2. Monkey 47 Schwarzwald Dry Gin (the Flamingo)

1. The brand story: Montgomery "Monty" Collins, a Royal Air Force officer posted to Berlin after the Second World War, sponsored a monkey named Max at the Berlin Zoo; he left the Royal Air Force in 1951, moved to the northern Black Forest and opened a guesthouse, Zum Wilden Affen, the Wild Monkey; his Schwarzwald Dry Gin was its house drink until the 1970s.
   Source: https://vinepair.com/articles/ntk-monkey-47/ and https://theabsolutgroup.com/story/monkey-47-how-a-crazy-creature-has-taken-the-gin-world-by-storm (summary): "a British RAF officer, Montgomery "Monty" Collins, who was posted to Berlin after the Second World War and sponsored a monkey named Max at the Berlin Zoo. He left the Royal Air Force in 1951, moved to the northern Black Forest, and opened a guesthouse named Zum Wilden Affen, a tribute to Max. He made his Schwarzwald Dry Gin there, and it was the guesthouse's signature drink until the 1970s." Also https://monkey47.com/montys-heritage (summary): "He later opened a country guesthouse in his honour and called it 'Zum wilden Affen' ('The Wild Monkey')."
2. The story is not independently confirmed: every source is the brand, its distributor or a retailer; dates and details vary between them (a bottle, letter and recipe found in the 1960s or at the turn of the century; 120 or 140 trial distillations).
   Source: search 19 (summary): "I found nothing from archives, historians, or independent reporting confirming the bottle, letter, or Collins's recipe. The discrepancies in dates and trial counts suggest the story has been told loosely. Treat it as a brand legend unless you find an independent source."
3. The founder, of the Jacobi German brandy family, heard of the recipe in 2006, left his job at Nokia at the end of 2008 and returned to the Black Forest to recreate the gin, testing it over more than 120 trial distillations; one source dates Black Forest Distillery to 2008, others date the brand to 2010 with a distiller as co-founder, and the launch is commonly given as 2010.
   Source: https://vinepair.com/articles/ntk-monkey-47/ and https://www.diffordsguide.com/producer/1194/monkey-47?e=12690 (summary): "Alexander Stein, of the Jacobi German brandy family, heard about the Commander's recipe in 2006. He left his job at Nokia at the end of 2008 and returned to the Black Forest to recreate the gin. He tested many proportions across more than 120 trial distillations before settling on the final recipe." And: "One says Stein founded Black Forest Distillery in 2008, while others say he started the brand together with distiller Christoph Keller in 2010, and the launch year is commonly given as 2010."
4. Pernod Ricard Deutschland bought a majority stake in Monkey 47 from Black Forest Distillers GmbH, announced in late January 2016 and signed in Berlin; price and share undisclosed; the founder kept his role; then sold in more than 50 countries.
   Source: https://harpers.co.uk/news/fullstory.php/aid/18738/Pernod_Ricard_snaps_up_German_premium_gin_brand_Monkey_47.html (summary, dated 29 January 2016): "Pernod Ricard has bought a majority stake in leading German craft gin brand Monkey 47." https://moodiedavittreport.com/pernod-ricard-deutschland-acquires-majority-interest-in-monkey-47 and https://www.thespiritsbusiness.com/2016/01/pernod-ricard-buys-majority-stake-in-monkey-47 (summary): "The buyer was Pernod Ricard Deutschland, and the seller side was Black Forest Distillers GmbH, which owns the brand." "The price was not disclosed. Nor has the percentage shareholding between both Pernod and Black Forest Distillers been published." "Founder Alexander Stein will maintain his role at Black Forest Distillers." "Monkey 47 is now available in more than 50 countries, including the UK and US."
5. The distillery: built by Black Forest Distillers in 2015 in the village of 24 Höfe near Loßburg, around a custom still from the coppersmith Arnold Holstein.
   Source: https://world-architects.com/de/projects/view/monkey-47 (summary): "Black Forest Distillers built a distillery with an attached office building in the village of 24 Höfe near Loßburg in the Black Forest." "The still house holds a custom still from the German coppersmith Arnold Holstein." "The project is listed as built in 2015."
6. 47 botanicals at 47 percent; about a third come from the Black Forest: spruce, lingonberries, elderflower, blackthorn and bramble leaves. Citrus peels are used fresh; lingonberries are macerated separately in molasses-based French neutral spirit and spring water at 70 percent.
   Source: https://www.diffordsguide.com/producer/1194/monkey-47/production and https://alcademics.com/a-visit-to-the-monkey-47-distillery-in-germanys-black-forest/ (summary): "Ingredients sourced straight from the Black Forest include spruce, lingonberries, elderflower, blackthorn, and bramble leaves." Lingonberries are "ground and macerated in molasses-based French neutral spirit and spring water at 70% alc/vol." Also https://theabsolutgroup.com/brands/gin/monkey-47 (summary): "one-third of the ingredients are handpicked from the Black Forest itself."
7. Most botanicals macerate in spirit cut to 70 percent, about 36 hours by one account; some sit in a basket in the steam; the stills hold about 100 litres; only water is added after distillation, no more neutral alcohol.
   Source: same (summary): "one source reports a 36 hour maceration before being distilled." "The basket holds fresh lavender and lemon peels." "After distillation, only water is added, no additional neutral alcohol." "they hold just 100 liters of capacity."
8. The distillate rests in 1,000-litre earthenware vessels, 100 days by one account and 90 by another, before slow dilution; bottled at 47 percent and not chill filtered.
   Source: same (summary): "the distillate rests in 1,000-litre earthenware vessels for 100 days to rest and oxidise prior to bottling." "the spirit at full proof is rested for 90 days in these large urns before being diluted and bottled." "the product is bottled at 47% ABV, which is above the level that it would louche, so the gin is not chill-filtered."

## 3. Hendrick's Gin (the Spoonbill)

1. The stills: a Bennett still of 1860 and Carter-Head stills built in 1948 by John Dore & Co.; Charles Gordon, great-grandson of William Grant, bought both at auction in 1966. Falstaff gives 1868 for the Bennett; the Scottish Gin Society gives 1943 for the Carter-Head.
   Source: https://global.hendricksgin.com/unusual-times/read/a-grand-history-of-hendrick-s-gin and https://www.spiritsanddistilling.com/dictionary/acref-9780199311132-e-135 (The Oxford Companion to Spirits and Cocktails) (summary): "The Bennett still dates to 1860 ... Carter-Head stills were built in 1948 by John Dore & Co. The company's own history says Charles Gordon, great-grandson of William Grant, bought both at auction in 1966." And https://www.thescottishginsociety.com/gins/hendricks-gin-2/, https://falstaff.com/en/news/hendricks-gin-a-very-british-revolution (summary): "The Scottish Gin Society gives the Carter-Head date as 1943 ... Falstaff gives the Bennett date as 1868."
2. He asked a company distiller to create a spirit from both stills, in 1988 by the company's account (1989 by the Scottish Gin Society); after years of experiments it launched in 1999, combining distillate from both stills with cucumber and rose.
   Source: same (summary): "Gordon brought in Lesley Gracie to create a spirit using both stills. After years of experiments, the 1999 launch combined distillate from both stills with cucumber and rose." "The Scottish Gin Society also says Gordon tasked Lesley Gracie in 1989, while Hendrick's says 1988."
3. Made at Girvan Distillery in Scotland; rose and cucumber are added after distillation, which is why it cannot be labelled a London Dry gin.
   Source: https://www.spiritsanddistilling.com/dictionary/acref-9780199311132-e-135 (summary): "Hendrick's is made at Girvan Distillery in Scotland. Rose and cucumber are added after distillation, which is why it cannot be labeled a London Dry gin."
4. Eleven botanicals besides the rose and cucumber; one list includes lemon peel, chamomile, cubeb berries, orange peel, elderflower, yarrow and caraway seeds, with juniper and orris root; the rose is called the famed Bulgarian rose; rose oil is gently pressed from the petals; the cucumbers are from Holland or Belgium.
   Source: https://www.masterofmalt.com/distilleries/hendricks-gin-distillery/, https://vpstats.vinepair.com/articles/10-things-know-hendricks/, https://pageandsons.co.uk/producers/hendricks-gin (summary): "The gin uses 11 botanicals besides the rose and cucumber. One list includes lemon peel, chamomile, cubeb berries, orange peel, elderflower, yarrow, and caraway seeds, alongside juniper and orris root. Another source names the famed Bulgarian rose and cucumber as key ingredients." "Rose is extracted by gently pressing the essential oils from the petals. Cucumbers are from Holland or Belgium."
5. The name: most accounts credit a member of the Grant family (Janet Sheed Roberts) with naming the gin after a family gardener called Hendrick, who took particular care of the roses.
   Source: same (summary): "Most accounts credit Janet Sheed Roberts, a member of the Grant family, with choosing the name. One says she named the gin after an expert family gardener named Hendrick. Another describes the gardener as an estate gardener called Hendrick who took particular care of the roses."
6. The Hendrick's Gin Palace officially opened on 1 October 2018 in the grounds of William Grant & Sons' Girvan facility: about £13 million, two new still houses that doubled capacity, six stills including the 1860 original and a 1948 Carter-Head, and two glasshouses of exotic botanicals; construction began in 2016.
   Source: https://sltn.co.uk/2018/10/11/gin-palace-opens-up-in-girvan/, https://www.malaymail.com/news/eat-drink/2018/10/04/hendricks-opens-doors-to-newly-expanded-gin-palace-in-scotland/1679104, https://gourmantic.com/hendricks-gin-palace/ (summary): "Officially opened on October 1, the new distillery and venue, located in the grounds of the company's Girvan facility". "an estimated investment of £13m ($15m); the distillery has doubled its capacity, with the addition of two new still houses". "two smaller glasshouses on either side which house a variety of exotic botanicals". "The construction of Hendrick's Gin Palace began in 2016." Gourmantic calls the owner "the family owned independent global sprits company."
7. William Grant & Sons was established in 1887 by William Grant and is run by his descendants; Glenfiddich began production on Christmas Day that year; Girvan grain distillery was built in 1963 under Charles Gordon, a great-grandson of William Grant (1964 in Grace's Guide).
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Grant_%26_Sons and https://scotchwhisky.com/whiskypedia/2001/girvan/ (summary): "The company was established in 1887 by William Grant, and it is run by his descendants. The Glenfiddich distillery began production on Christmas Day that year." "The Girvan grain distillery was built in 1963 under Charles Gordon, a great-grandson of William Grant." "Grace's Guide lists its start date as 1964." Wikipedia (summary): "family-owned and currently led by Glenn Gordon."

## 4. Gentilly Gin and Seven Three Distilling (the OBX)

1. Owned by a husband and wife who developed the concept and brand while overseeing the build-out of a custom distillery; the husband a New Orleans native and local attorney; a head distiller came from Two James Spirits in Detroit.
   Source: https://tecrealtors-neworleans-la.cbcworldwide.com/news/anewdistilleryopensonclaiborneavenue and https://bizneworleans.com/new-orleans-spirit/ (summary): "Owners Sal and Eileen Bivalacqua developed the concept and brand." "Sal is a New Orleans native and local attorney, and Eileen is from the Azores by way of New Hampshire. Head Distiller Erik Morningstar from Two James Spirits in Detroit was also brought on early."
2. Opening: in development from 2015, open in March 2017 by one source, early 2017 in Tremé by another, Tuesday 21 February by a report from the opening week; one source says founded in 2016.
   Source: same and https://offbeat.com/articles/seven-3-distilling-bottle-every-neighborhood (summary): "the brand went into development in 2015 and the distillery opened its doors in March 2017. Another says the operation opened in early 2017 in Tremé." "A report from the opening week gives the date as Tuesday (Feb. 21)." "One source says the company was founded in 2016."
3. The address is 301 N. Claiborne Ave., on the corner of Claiborne Avenue and Bienville Street in the Treme; a business profile places it in Tulane Gravier, in a warehouse of about 13,780 square feet. A tasting room and tours; call ahead, hours change.
   Source: https://www.explorelouisiana.com/distilleries/seven-three-distilling-co and https://bizneworleans.com/new-orleans-spirit/ (summary): "The address is 301 N. Claiborne Ave., New Orleans, LA 70112. One article describes it as in a warehouse on the corner of Claiborne Avenue and Bienville Street in the Treme." "Tours and a tasting room are available. Contact the business directly for current hours and services."
4. Named for the city's 73 officially recognised neighbourhoods, and each spirit takes a neighbourhood's name.
   Source: https://offbeat.com/articles/seven-3-distilling-bottle-every-neighborhood (summary): "The distillery takes its name, and the liquors it produces, from New Orleans' 73 neighborhoods." And: "the company was named for the 73 officially recognized New Orleans neighborhoods."
5. The first product was a 1,000-bottle run of St. Roch Vodka: a neutral spirit re-distilled and filtered for five days through copper, limestone and charcoal. What the neutral spirit is made from is not stated.
   Source: https://tecrealtors-neworleans-la.cbcworldwide.com/news/anewdistilleryopensonclaiborneavenue (summary): "The first product was a 1,000 bottle run of its vodka, called St. Roch Vodka. It starts with a neutral spirit, which is re-distilled and filtered for five days through copper, limestone and charcoal."
6. The range (a 2025 press release): Bywater Bourbon, St. Roch Vodka, St. Roch Cucumber Vodka, Irish Channel Whiskey, Marigny Moonshine, Gentilly Gin, Black Pearl Rum and the Whiskey Tree Bourbons; Marigny Moonshine uses rice from Opelousas; the wheat and corn of Bywater Bourbon come from Erwinville in West Baton Rouge Parish; distributed in Louisiana and Mississippi as of 2025; Whiskey Tree High Rye Bourbon won Double Gold, 99 points, at the 2025 San Francisco World Spirits Competition.
   Source: https://bevnet.com/pr/2025/05/16/whiskey-tree-high-rye-bourbon-scores-double-gold-99-points-and-bestofclass-finalist-at-2025-san-francisco-world-spirits-competition and https://bizneworleans.com/think-local-drink-local/ (summary): the lineup as listed; "the distillery's products are currently distributed across Louisiana and Mississippi, with expansion to Alabama and Texas slated for Q3 2025."
7. Gentilly Gin starts from the same base as the vodka, with juniper and, for local flavour, persimmon and elderflower; 17 botanicals inspired by traiteur healers, the old faith healers; named after one of the city's neighbourhoods.
   Source: https://tecrealtors-neworleans-la.cbcworldwide.com/news/anewdistilleryopensonclaiborneavenue (summary, a pre-launch article of 2017): "it will start with the same base as the vodka to which will be added juniper and, for local flavor, persimmon and elderflower along with other botanicals." https://www.masterofmalt.com/gin/seven-three-distilling/seven-three-distilling-gentilly-gin/?sku=102856 (summary): "the name nods to the city's neighborhood group, and the recipe was inspired by old faith healers"; "its 17-botanical recipe draws on traiteur healers, with elderflower and persimmon among the ingredients."
8. Tasting: elderflower and subtle anise over vanilla on the nose; honeyed persimmon, stem ginger and juniper on the palate; peppery warmth on the finish. Bottled at 87 proof, 43.50% ABV.
   Source: same and https://shop.bigthirst.com/products/gentilly-gin (summary): "Elderflower and subtle anise sit under vanilla on the nose. The palate brings honeyed persimmon, stem ginger, and juniper. Peppery warmth and juniper linger on the finish." "87 proof | 43.50% ABV | 750 mL."
9. Gentilly Gin Barrel Reserve is barrel-rested before bottling at 87 proof.
   Source: https://distiller.com/spirits/gentilly-gin-barrel-reserve/tastes (summary): "A separate Gentilly Gin Barrel Reserve is barrel-rested before bottling at 87 proof, based on the standard gin."
10. The neighbourhood: in 1727 the colonists Mathurin and Pierre Dreux chose the natural levee along Bayou Sauvage for their plantation and named it Gentilly after the French community they came from; a review of a book by Nathalie Dessens and Virginia Meacham Gould argues the name is a corruption of Chantilly, a northern suburb of Paris, which appears on old maps.
