## 1. Sweetened condensed milk, and Gail Borden

1. Gail Borden received a United States patent for condensed milk in 1856; one account dates it 19 August 1856, after an earlier application had been refused as nothing new. His process removed water from milk and added sugar; the vacuum pan idea came from the Shakers, who condensed fruit juice and extracts that way.
   Source: https://americacomesalive.com/borden-dairy-the-beginning/ (the refused first application, the 1856 patent, the Shaker vacuum pan); https://www.cooksinfo.com/sweetened-condensed-milk (the 19 August 1856 date).
2. The first factories were in Connecticut: one account has a factory at Wolcottville two months after the patent, which failed, and another has the first factory built in 1857 at Burrville. The accounts differ, so the cards say only Connecticut and New York.
   Source: https://americacomesalive.com/borden-dairy-the-beginning/ ; https://reginajeffers.blog/2021/04/28/what-do-you-know-of-gail-borden-american-inventor-of-condensed-milk/
3. The Union army became Borden's biggest customer in the Civil War, and he licensed the patent to other makers to meet demand. The Eagle Brand name: the company's own timeline lists the Eagle Brand and American Bald Eagle trademarks as first used in 1858; a Borden history says the Eagle name came after the war. Borden sold the brand to Eagle Family Foods in 1998; the J.M. Smucker Co. bought that business in 2007 and sold its United States canned milk brands, Eagle Brand among them, to Eagle Family Foods Group, a company backed by the investment firm Kelso & Company, in a sale that closed on 31 December 2015, so Eagle Family Foods is the owner as of 2026 and the J.M. Smucker Co. is not.
   Source: https://eaglebrand.com/eagle-brand-history/ (the 1858 trademark date); https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000091419/000119312516421451/d115799d8k.htm (the J.M. Smucker Co.'s 8-K: the sale to Eagle Family Foods Group closed 31 December 2015); https://www.foodprocessing.com/business-of-food-beverage/business-strategies/news/11323522/jm-smucker-to-sell-us-canned-milk-brands-and-operations-eagle-brand-magnolia (the November 2015 announcement); https://kelso.com/node/247 (Kelso's announcement); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Foods (the 1998 sale by Borden and the 2007 purchase by Smucker); https://americacomesalive.com/borden-dairy-the-beginning/ (the Union army, the licences); https://barrypopik.com/blog/condensed_milk_bordens_eagle_brand
4. Gail Borden was born in New York in 1801, lived in Texas from 1829 as a surveyor and newspaper publisher, and died in 1874; the cards may name him because he is long dead. He laid out the city of Galveston as its surveyor.
   Source: https://foodqualityandsafety.com/article/gail-borden-jr-got-milk (born 1801 in Norwich, New York; Texas from 1829; surveyor; died 1874); https://reginajeffers.blog/2021/04/28/what-do-you-know-of-gail-borden-american-inventor-of-condensed-milk/
5. Sweetened condensed milk is whole milk with about 60 per cent of its water drawn off under vacuum and roughly 40 to 45 per cent sugar in the finished tin; (expert) the sugar is the preservative, so an unopened tin keeps for years without refrigeration. It is thick, glossy and sweet with a cooked, caramel-milk flavour; heated long enough, in the tin or a pan, it becomes dulce de leche.
   Source: https://www.finedininglovers.com/explore/articles/whats-difference-between-condensed-and-evaporated-milk (about 60 per cent of the water removed); https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Cookbook:Sweetened_Condensed_Milk (the sugar at about 40 to 50 per cent of the finished product). The guide calls the Tatin's anglaise "a pourable vanilla custard made with sweetened condensed milk for a rich, caramel-milk flavor" (guide.txt, pdf p38). No brand is printed.

## 2. The American lobster fishery, the profile attached to the shipped Lobster component

1. Maine's preliminary 2024 figures from its Department of Marine Resources: lobster landings of about 86.1 million pounds, worth $528,421,645 at the boat, an average of $6.14 a pound, the second highest price on record; the catch was down from 96.9 million pounds in 2023 and was the lowest in fifteen years. Fishermen set about 285,000 fewer traps than the year before (Maine Public), and a Department of Marine Resources scientist told the Maine Fishermen's Forum that the Gulf of Maine was generally cooler in 2024 and the lobsters started later (the AP report of 28 February 2025). Lobster ranked first of all Maine's fisheries, whose total value was $709.5 million.
   Source: https://www.mainepublic.org/business-and-economy/2025-02-28/maine-lobster-landings-hit-a-15-year-low-in-2024 ; https://www.bangordailynews.com/2025/02/28/nation/us-lobster-catch-drops-as-crustaceans-migrate-to-colder-canadian-waters/ (the AP report, the cooler Gulf) ; https://www.islandinstitute.org/working-waterfront/lobster-landings-dip-but-price-was-up/ ; https://mainebiz.biz/article/maine-lobster-catch-hits-a-6-year-low-in-2024 ; https://www.maine.gov/dmr/fisheries/commercial/landings-program/landings-data (the data are preliminary ex-vessel values).
2. Canada is by far the largest exporter of live and frozen North American lobster, Homarus americanus, with the United States second.
   Source: https://fao.org/in-action/globefish/species-analysis/lobster/en (FAO GLOBEFISH species analysis).
3. Maine's conservation rules, as the industry states them: a minimum carapace length of 3 1/4 inches and a maximum of 5 inches, measured from the eye socket to the end of the body shell; egg-bearing females may not be landed and are marked with a V-notch in the right tail flipper so they are recognised and returned, under a zero-tolerance standard for any mark on that flipper; Maine's rule names the flipper to the right of the centre one. The notch is not for life: a Maine Department of Marine Resources scientist puts it at about two moults, roughly two to three years, depending on the size of the cut, so the cards say "while the notch lasts, about two moults".
   Source: https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/maine/13-188-C-M-R-ch-25-SS-15 (Maine's V-notching rule, the right-of-centre flipper, a notch of any size); https://nationalfisherman.com/northeast/maine-s-lobster-v-notch-decline-a-threat (the notch lasts about two moults, two to three years); https://lobsteranywhere.com/seafood-savvy/maine-lobster-laws/ ; https://www.mlcalliance.org/post/protecting-female-lobsters-a-maine-tradition-for-generations ; https://view.ceros.com/boston-globe/maine-lobster (the 3 1/4 and 5 inch limits). The interstate commission's own V-notch definition is a notch at least 1/8 inch deep in the base of the flipper: https://www.govinfo.gov/link/fr/72/56937 . The commission's Addendum XXVII would have raised the minimum to 3 5/16 inches in 2025, but Maine withdrew its own version in January 2025 and the commission repealed the gauge and vent changes in May 2025 through Addendum XXXII, so the 3 1/4 inch minimum stands as of 2026 and 3 5/16 is not printed on the card: https://asmfc.org/?p=18592 (the repeal, Addendum XXXII); https://www.seafoodsource.com/news/supply-trade/asmfc-repeals-lobster-gauge-and-vent-size-changes ; https://www.mass.gov/doc/new-rules-affecting-commercial-and-recreational-lobster-fishers-and-seafood-dealers/download (the earlier rule)
4. (expert) The American lobster, Homarus americanus, is the clawed lobster of the cold North Atlantic, from Labrador and Nova Scotia down past New England; the warm Gulf of Mexico has only the clawless spiny lobster, which is why the shipped Lobster component says ours comes from the North Atlantic. It is caught in baited traps, not nets, most of the fleet working within a few miles of shore, though an offshore trap fishery exists, so the card says "mostly". A lobster that has just moulted, in summer and early autumn, has a soft shell and sweeter, wetter meat and ships poorly; a hard-shell lobster from the colder months is fuller and travels live for days in a cold, damp box. Restaurants far from the coast buy live lobsters by air freight, or cooked and frozen tails and claw meat. The menu prints no supplier, state or fishery, so the kitchen's source is a question for the chef.

## 3. Lemon

1. The lemon, Citrus limon, is thought to have begun in India; the University of Florida sheet says it was introduced into southern Italy as early as 200 A.D., so the card says "by about the year 200". In the United States it is grown almost entirely in California, about 45,000 acres, and Arizona, about 13,500 acres, with under 600 acres in Florida. The world's largest growers are India, Argentina, Spain, Iran and the United States.
   Source: https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/HS402 (University of Florida: the Indian origin, the spread to southern Italy, the acreages, the top five countries).
2. Eureka and Lisbon are the two main American commercial varieties, both the sharp, thin-skinned true lemon; Eureka was first planted in California in 1858.
   Source: https://thenibble.com/REVIEWS/Main/fruits/lemon-types.asp ; https://wikifarmer.com/library/en/article/the-most-popular-lemon-tree-varieties
3. (expert) The acid of lemon juice is almost all citric acid, about five to six per cent of the juice by weight, the cook's sharpest clean acid, so the card says the juice's acid is almost all citric and not that the juice is; the zest's oils carry the perfume. Preserved lemon is lemon cured in salt until the peel is soft and savoury, a North African method. The menus print lemon on five plates: the lemon curd of the Blackberry Trifle, the lemon in the Crab & Corn Omelette, the preserved lemon beurre blanc of the Redfish Véronique, the preserved lemon and harissa on the Smoked Cauliflower, and the lemon jelly of the Lemon Tart (guide.txt and the menus page). The bar's lemons are a separate shipped component. Which lemons the kitchen buys is a question for the chef.

## 4. Apples, the Baked Apple's fruit

1. The domestic apple descends from Malus sieversii, the wild apple of the Tian Shan mountains of Kazakhstan and its neighbours; Nikolai Vavilov named the region as the apple's origin in 1929 and later genetic work agreed. Wild apple forests survive near Almaty.
   Source: https://applesandpeople.org.uk/stories/heavenly-mountains/ ; https://www.intechopen.com/online-first/1193192 ; https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/05/is-kazakhstan-the-birthplace-of-apples-heres-how-the-fruit-spread-across-the-world
2. Washington state grows around two thirds of the American apple crop: the USDA's August 2024 forecast put Washington at 7.50 billion pounds, about 68 per cent of the national figure for the top seven states, and the USApple 2024 estimate put it at 155 million of 259.5 million bushels, about 60 per cent.
   Source: https://nass.usda.gov/Statistics_by_State/Washington/Publications/Fruit/2024/FR08.pdf ; https://usapple.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/USApple-Production-Estimate-2024.pdf
3. (expert) Varieties differ most in how they bake: a firm, tart apple holds its shape and keeps its acid, a soft sweet one collapses into sauce. The house's own recipe page, as recorded in research/verified-facts.json (chef-dishes-a-9) and research/dossier-the-kitchen.md (Baked Apple), peels the apples, trims them flat to stand, cores them with a melon baller and tops them with a pecan oat mix of flour, butter, light brown sugar, cinnamon, rolled oats, raisins and pecans; the plate adds the brown sugar glaze and sweetened crème fraîche the menu prints. The guide's own line is "baked until soft, with a crumble of oatmeal, pecans and raisins, then glazed with brown sugar" (guide.txt, pdf p4), a glaze after the bake; the shipped technique component reads the glaze as the crumble's brown sugar caramelising in the apple's juice. The two readings are not the same, so the Apples card says baked until soft and glazed with brown sugar and sends which it is to the chef. Which apple the kitchen uses, and whether the tasting portion is a whole or a half, are open questions for the chef (dossier-the-kitchen.md, Baked Apple).

## 5. Rolled oats

1. Oats, Avena sativa, began as a weed in the wheat and barley fields of the Near East; their wild ancestor Avena sterilis grew in the Fertile Crescent, and the crop was domesticated late, in Bronze Age Europe, as cereal farming spread into cooler, wetter country where oats thrive and wheat struggles. Researchers call it potentially the only European cereal.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oat (the secondary crop origin, the Fertile Crescent ancestor, the Bronze Age European domestication, the cool wet climates); https://research.aber.ac.uk/en/projects/oat-domestication-understanding-the-origin-of-a-european-cereal/
2. Rolled oats are oat groats, the hulled whole grain, steamed to soften them and to still the enzyme that turns their oil rancid, flattened under heavy rollers and lightly toasted; the toast adds flavour and finishes the job of stopping the enzyme. Quick oats are rolled thinner; steel-cut oats are chopped groats never steamed.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolled_oats ; https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/food-network-essentials/what-are-rolled-oats
3. (expert) Oats are the grain of Scottish porridge and of the British crumble; in a crumble they give chew and a toasty, nutty flavour and brown without burning. The guide's service note on the Baked Apple says "oats may carry gluten, confirm" (guide.txt, pdf p4), because oats are often milled beside wheat; the question goes to the kitchen.

## 6. Raisins

1. Nearly all American raisins come from California's San Joaquin Valley, almost all within about sixty miles of Fresno; the Thompson Seedless grape makes about 90 per cent of the supply. Natural, dark raisins are dried in the sun; golden raisins are the same grape dried by machine and treated with sulphur to keep their colour.
   Source: https://www.agmrc.org/commodities-products/fruits/raisins (the 90 per cent Thompson Seedless figure, 2024); https://migration.ucdavis.edu/cf/more.php?id=124 (within sixty miles of Fresno); https://www.californiabountiful.com/magazine-features/magazine-issues/januaryfebruary-2010/raisin-growers-find-their-place-in-the-sun (the San Joaquin growers, sun drying); https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/2024RaisinHearing_Exhibit27.pdf
2. (expert) Sun-dried raisins lie on paper trays between the vine rows for two to three weeks in late summer. A raisin is a grape with most of its water gone and its sugar concentrated. In a crumble the raisins plump in the heat and turn jammy, sweet with a little tartness. The Baked Apple prints them; whether raisins go into the New Orleans Bread Pudding, as the classic recipe would have them, is an open question for the chef (dossier-the-kitchen.md, New Orleans Bread Pudding: "whiskey, bread, raisins").

## 7. Gelatin, for the setting technique

1. Pastry sheet gelatin is graded by bloom strength: bronze about 125 to 155, silver about 160, gold about 190 to 220, platinum about 220 to 265; sheets of different grades weigh different amounts but one sheet of any grade sets about the same volume. Commercial gelatin is collagen from skin, bone and connective tissue, much of it a by-product of pigskin.
   Source: https://www.pastrydepot.com/blogs/blog/a-guide-to-gelatin/ ; https://blog.modernistpantry.com/advice/the-confusion-over-conversion/ ; https://modernistpantry.com/products/gelatin-sheets-bronze-strength-140-bloom.html
2. (expert) Bovine and fish gelatins exist; agar, from seaweed, and pectin, from fruit, set without any animal. Gelatin melts at about body temperature, which is why a jelly or a bavarois feels so clean in the mouth. The guide says Bavarian creams are usually set with gelatin and sends the vegetarian question to the kitchen (guide.txt, pdf p36); the kitchen dossier reads the Lemon Tart's yuzu mousse as likely gelatin-set and lists "Lemon Tart: gelatin, meringue style" and "Snickers: gelatin, tree nuts" among the questions for the chef (dossier-the-kitchen.md, sections on the Lemon Tart and The Snickers, and section 15).

## 8. The techniques, a cook's working knowledge

- (expert) Caramel: sucrose begins to caramelise above about 160 C, about 320 F, breaking into hundreds of new compounds that taste toasty, bitter and nutty; a dry caramel is sugar alone, a wet caramel starts with water and resists crystallising; light amber is mild, dark amber bitter and deep, black is burnt; cream, butter or a spirit added at the end stops the cooking and makes a sauce. The shipped components already describe the Foster's blond butter and brown sugar caramel, the whiskey caramel, the caramel custard, the charred pineapple, the brown sugar glaze that forms as the apple bakes, the glassy coat on the candied hazelnuts and the Crystal gastrique's "sugar cooked to caramel" (components/dishes.json).
- (expert) Ice cream: a sweetened cream or custard frozen while stirred; the paddle scrapes crystals off the cold wall and folds in air; the air taken on is called overrun, and a dense restaurant ice cream carries much less of it than a supermarket tub. A French style base is a crème anglaise with egg yolks; a Philadelphia style base is cream, milk and sugar with no egg. The base is aged cold overnight, churned, hardened in the freezer and tempered before scooping. The guide says the Foster's ice cream likely contains egg, confirm (guide.txt, pdf p34).
- (expert) Custard: egg set gently in milk or cream; stirred anglaise comes off the heat at about 82 to 84 C, when it coats the back of a spoon, because a few degrees more scrambles the egg; baked custard sets in a water bath; a bavarois is anglaise set with gelatin and lightened with whipped cream (research/escoffier-mother-sauces-2026-10-16.md and components/dishes.json, Bavarian cream).
- (expert) Mousse: the French word for foam; a flavoured base lightened with whipped cream or whipped egg white folded in, often with a little gelatin so it holds a shape in service; cold cream whips, warm cream will not. Three on the menus: the yuzu mousse, the whipped vanilla mousse and the mascarpone mousse.
- (expert) Charring: a fast, hard surface cook on a grill, under a salamander, in dry cast iron or with a torch, until the edges blacken; sugars caramelise and proteins brown, and the blackened edges add a bitter, smoky note; the inside stays juicy. The guide says the pineapple is "charred so it's caramelized with a little smoky edge" (guide.txt, pdf p38), and the dossier notes pineapple is wetter than apple and needs more browning.
- (expert) Candying nuts: cooked in sugar syrup; stirred as it cools, the sugar crystallises into a sandy coat (sablé), and the New Orleans praline is that finish taken to a soft, creamy patty of pecans, brown sugar, butter and cream; left to caramelise, the nuts take a glassy amber shell (praliné), crushed for crunch; both go sticky in humid air, so they are made close to service (components/dishes.json, Pralines and Candied hazelnuts; dossier-the-kitchen.md, Roasted Rohan Duck Breast cook's notes). Toasting the nuts first matters.
- (expert) Bread pudding: day-old bread soaked in a sweet custard of eggs, milk or cream, sugar and vanilla, often with spice and dried fruit, baked, sometimes in a water bath, until set soft inside and golden on top; stale bread holds its shape while fresh bread turns to paste; the airy New Orleans French loaf is the traditional bread because its open crumb drinks custard without collapsing (dossier-the-kitchen.md, New Orleans Bread Pudding). Which bread, which whiskey and whether raisins go in are the dossier's open questions for the chef.
