Primary sources opened and read on 27 October 2026

1. Agrapart, the producer's English site
https://www.champagnepascalagrapart.com/default-en.aspx
The estate is in Avize. The current 7 Crus description includes Chardonnay
and Pinot Noir; Complantée names six varieties: Chardonnay, Pinot Noir,
Meunier, Arbane, Petit Meslier and Pinot Blanc. Thus a producer-wide statement
that all Agrapart wines are pure Chardonnay is false. The corrected summary
asks for the cuvée; it does not assign a new blend to the house's Atoma or to
any historic vintage. Current cuvée percentages are not copied to old bottles.

2. Château des Tours, the estate's wines page
https://chateaurayas.fr/en/vinsdestours.php
The current red Vacqueyras description lists Grenache and Syrah; the red
Côtes du Rhône lists Grenache, Cinsault and Syrah. Both are Grenache led
blends. White Côtes du Rhône is separately described as Grenache. The
producer-wide claim of pure Grenache for both reds is corrected without
projecting today's percentages onto the printed 2015 or 2016 vintages.

3. Château Pontet-Canet, the estate's 2015 technical sheet
https://www.pontet-canet.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/CHATEAU-PONTET-CANET-2015-1.pdf
The one-page English sheet gives 65 percent Cabernet Sauvignon, 30 percent
Merlot, 4 percent Cabernet Franc and 1 percent Petit Verdot. The house list
includes 2015. The summary now distinguishes a Cabernet Sauvignon led blend
from pure Cabernet and identifies the vintage-specific source. It does not
apply that blend to other years. Existing estate geography and farming
descriptions are retained rather than broadened.

4. DEFRA, registered Portuguese traditional wine term Solera
https://www.gov.uk/protected-food-drink-names/solera-portuguese
The registered definition concerns Madeira PDO and associates the date with
the harvest forming the base of the lot. It allows no more than ten percent
annual withdrawal for bottling and replacement with another quality wine,
with a maximum of ten additions. This category is not an indefinitely
replenished Sherry solera. The recorded legal basis is Portuguese Portaria
125/98 of 29 July 1998. This definition explains the term; it cannot prove
the nineteenth-century history of a particular bottle.

5. ViniPortugal, Madeira Wine, Solera paragraph
https://winesofportugal.com/en/portuguese-wines/wine-styles/vinho-da-madeira/
Exact short statement: "the date on a bottle of Solera is always that of
the original base wine." Together with the registered definition above,
this means base harvest year, not simply an unrelated foundation date.
The old wording 'not a harvest' is wrong. The correct distinction is that
the finished wine is not a single vintage bottling and its date does not
establish the youngest component's age or a minimum century in cask for
every component. Other categories on this page were not used or updated.

6. Henriques & Henriques, Founders Solera 1894 technical sheet
https://henriquesehenriques.pt/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Founders-Solera-1894-ALL.pdf.pdf
Four pages in Portuguese, English, French and German; English is page two.
The notes describe amber with gold and green tones, dried fruit, spice,
honey, raisins and old wood, sweetness, velvety texture and an intense
finish. These support the corrected sensory summary. The sheet does not
name a grape, specify closure in 1899, give a bottling date or certify the
age of every component. English ABV is 20.0 while the other language pages
show 20.3, so this correction introduces no exact ABV assertion.
