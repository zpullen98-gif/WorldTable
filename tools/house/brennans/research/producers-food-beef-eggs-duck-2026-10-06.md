## 1. Creekstone Farms (Creole-Spiced Hanger Steak, Creole Hanger Steak, Roasted Chateaubriand)

1. Founded in 1995 by John and Carol Stewart.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creekstone_Farms_Premium_Beef (search snippet, summary): "The company was founded in 1995 by John and Carol Stewart."
2. It began as a purebred Black Angus farm in Campbellsburg, Kentucky, and entered processing in 2003.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creekstone_Farms_Premium_Beef (snippet): "Originally a purebred Black Angus farm in Campbellsburg, Kentucky, Creekstone Farms entered the processing business in 2003." and "began processing Creekstone Farms Premium Black Angus cattle in May 2003."
3. The plant is a 450,000-square-foot processing plant in Arkansas City, Kansas. One trade account gives an earlier start: John Stewart's Triad Foods in Kentucky in 1994, processing deli meats. That conflict is recorded, and servers should use 1995.
   Source: https://www.meatpoultry.com/articles/19763-custom-solutions (snippet, summary): "Creekstone Farms was founded by John Stewart in 1994 in Kentucky as Triad Foods, processing deli meats ... The company then expanded into the processing business in 2003, purchasing a 450,000-square-foot processing plant in Arkansas City, Kansas."
4. John Stewart built a reputation in Angus genetics: at one point he owned the highest-marbling bull in the Angus breed, and he sold genetics, bulls and semen to ranchers.
   Source: https://www.provisioneronline.com/articles/116332-focus-on-beef-underlies-creekstone-farms-broadening-scope (snippet, summary): "During Stewart's more than 10 years at the helm, the company focused on developing high-end beef cattle genetics, and at one point Stewart owned the highest marbling bull in Angus breeds. Stewart developed his own line of Angus and sold the genetics, bulls and semen to ranchers."
5. Stewart retired in October 2007.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creekstone_Farms_Premium_Beef (snippet): "Stewart retired from the company in October 2007."
6. Sun Capital Partners owned the company from 2005 and sold it to the Japanese trading house Marubeni Corporation in 2017, for a price reported at about $170 million, with its Australian subsidiary Rangers Valley.
   Source: https://www.foodnavigator-usa.com/Article/2017/07/25/Premium-meatpacker-bought-by-Japanese-trader/ (snippet): "Creekstone Farms was sold to the Japanese trading conglomerate by US investment firm Sun Capital Partners, which has owned the Kansas meatpacker since 2005" and "Marubeni's takeover includes the acquisition of Creekstone Farms' Australian subsidiary Rangers Valley". Also https://www.drovers.com/news/japanese-firm-buys-creekstone-farms-170-million (title): "Japanese Firm Buys Creekstone Farms for $170 Million".
7. Its cattle are bought on verifiable Black Angus genetics, not on hide colour alone.
   Source: https://shop.creekstonefarms.com/blogs/default-blog/blog-creekstone-farms-quality-black-angus-beef (snippet, summary): "The cattle Creekstone Farms procures come from verifiable Black Angus genetics, unlike many Black Angus beef programs which base their programs on hide color."
8. Its cattle-receiving facility was the first built from the ground up to the standards of Dr. Temple Grandin, the livestock-handling designer; its Natural Black Angus Beef carries the Certified Humane Raised and Handled label, verified by Humane Farm Animal Care.
   Source: https://supermarketnews.com/meat/creekstone-farms-announces-natural-black-angus-beef-certified-humane and https://meatpoultry.com/articles/13600-creekstone-gets-humane-certified (snippets, summary): "Creekstone Farms' cattle-receiving facility was the first to be built from the ground up according to the standards of consultant and livestock facility designer, Dr. Temple Grandin." and "Customers now see the Certified Humane Raised and Handled® label on all Creekstone Farms Natural Black Angus Beef products."
9. The BSE story: after BSE (mad cow disease) was found in the US herd in December 2003, Creekstone asked in early 2004 to test every animal it slaughtered. The USDA refused, citing the Virus-Serum-Toxin Act of 1913; Creekstone sued on 23 March 2006, and on 29 March 2007 Judge James Robertson of the US District Court for the District of Columbia ruled the refusal unlawful. The later appeal is not confirmed here, so the app tells only the attempt to test every animal and the court fight, not who finally won.
   Source: https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20061003_RL32414_62b827b71b2e114dc960794f138b9de8f2bd9d2c.pdf and https://agpolicy.org/weekcol/425.html (snippets, summary): "Following the discovery of bovine spongiform encephalopathy (BSE) in the US cattle herd in December 2003, Creekstone Farm Premium Beef decided in early 2004 to test each cow it slaughters for BSE." "The USDA claimed its authority ... came from a 1913 law, the Virus-Serum-Toxin Act (VSTA)" "On March 23, 2006, Creekstone filed a lawsuit" "A March 29, 2007 decision by Judge James Robertson of the United States District Court for the District of Columbia concluded that it is unlawful for USDA to prevent Creekstone from testing its cattle for BSE." An appeal-court case page, https://caselaw.findlaw.com/us-dc-circuit/1354447.html, was listed by the search but not read, so the final outcome is left out.
10. USDA Prime is the top grade: young, well-fed cattle with abundant marbling. Its share of graded beef varies by year and source (one source cites about 10 per cent in 2024 and 2025 by USDA reports; firms quote 2 to 5 per cent), so no share is given.
    Source: https://www.chowhound.com/2089883/what-beef-gets-usda-prime-grade (snippet, summary): "USDA Prime beef must come from young, healthy cattle and exhibit abundant marbling." and "The USDA meat grading reports for 2024 and 2025 show 10% ... However, some steakhouses and beef companies say it's only 2% to 3%, while others say 3% to 5%."
11. Brennan's link: the menu prints Creekstone on three dishes and Prime only on the breakfast hanger. No public statement from the house was found.

Expert knowledge: the hanger steak is one per animal and often sold ungraded, which is why a Prime hanger is worth naming (already marked expert in components-dishes-2026-10-05.md, search 36).

## 2. Vital Farms (the sunny-side-up egg on the Creole-Spiced Hanger Steak)

1. Founded in 2007 by Matt O'Hayer, headquartered in Austin, Texas; it sells eggs and butter.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vital_Farms (snippet): "Vital Farms, Inc. is an American egg and butter brand founded by Matt O'Hayer in 2007 and headquartered in Austin, Texas".
2. It began with 20 hens on a 27-acre farm.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vital_Farms (snippet): "O'Hayer launched Vital Farms with 20 hens on a 27-acre farm." Also CNBC, https://www.cnbc.com/video/2023/10/04/how-vital-farms-turned-27-acres-into-a-450-million-egg-empire.html (title): "How Vital Farms turned 27 acres into a $450 million egg empire".
3. Pasture-raised here means at least 108 square feet of outdoor space per hen, on rotated pasture where hens forage for grasses, succulents and wildflowers; the eggs are Certified Humane, whose pasture standard is the same 108 square feet.
   Source: https://goop.com/wellness/food-planet/decoding-the-carton-how-to-buy-clean-humane-eggs/ and retailer copy (snippets, summary): "Vital Farms Pasture-Raised eggs are Certified Humane and come from hens that have the freedom to forage for local grasses, succulents, and wildflowers year-round while enjoying 108 sq feet each of roaming room in open pastures." "The 108 square feet per hen standard aligns with Certified Humane standards of 108-square-feet per bird." Also https://vitalfarms.com/learn/faqs/are-vital-farms-eggs-still-pasture-raised/ (components-dishes-2026-10-05.md, search 37).
4. The hens live on a network of small family farms: retail copy says "over 300"; earlier research recorded "hundreds", and the current count is likely higher, so say "hundreds".
   Source: retailer copy quoted in the same search (snippet): "Today, we work with over 300 small family farms who give the girls the outdoor lifestyle they deserve."
5. In 2017 it opened its own egg washing and packing plant in Springfield, Missouri, called Egg Central Station, about 153,000 square feet; a second Egg Central Station is being built in Seymour, Indiana.
   Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1579733/000156459021014969/vitl-10k_20201227.htm and Wikipedia (snippets, summary): "In 2017, the company opened its first egg washing and packaging facility in Springfield, MO, coined 'Egg Central Station.'" "The company owns its shell egg processing facility in Springfield, Missouri totaling approximately 153,000 square feet". Second plant: "Vital Farms has a second Egg Central Station rising in Seymour, Indiana" (summary of the Wikipedia snippet).
6. It went public on Nasdaq as VITL on 31 July 2020, at $22 a share, and is a Certified B Corporation.
   Source: https://www.wattagnet.com/articles/40866-vital-farms-raises-nearly-205-million-in-ipo (snippet, summary): "Vital Farms began its first day as a publicly traded company on July 31, 2020, after raising nearly $205 million in an initial public offering ... trading on the Nasdaq exchange under the VITL ticker ... at a price of $22 per share." Wikipedia (snippet): "a publicly-traded company on Nasdaq (VITL) and a certified B Corporation."
7. Brennan's link: the menu prints "Sunny Side Up Vital Farms Egg" on the breakfast hanger only. No other dish names an egg producer, and no public statement from the house was found.

Expert knowledge: hens that forage outdoors often lay eggs with deeper orange yolks; diet drives yolk colour. (Already in the existing component; general, not a Vital Farms claim.)

## 3. The Rohan duck and D'Artagnan (Roasted Rohan Duck Breast, Duck Confit Waffle)

1. The Rohan is D'Artagnan's own proprietary duck: "a proprietary hybrid of several duck breeds including the Heritage Mallard and the Pekin", raised only for D'Artagnan on a farm in New York State.
   Source: https://center-of-the-plate.com/2012/01/10/have-you-met-rohan/ (D'Artagnan's blog, January 2012, snippet, summary): "The D'Artagnan Rohan™ is a proprietary hybrid of several duck breeds including the Heritage Mallard and the Pekin, and is raised just for them on a farm in New York State."
2. It was bred to approximate a style of duck found in Normandy, and named after a family of dukes and princes of the Rohan area of Brittany who made the name, and a native duck, famous.
   Source: https://tastingtable.com/685236/dartagnans-rohan-ducks-hit-the-market (snippet, summary): "A duck named Rohan is a new breed from D'Artagnan that crosses the mallard and the Pekin to approximate a style of duck found in Normandy. The duck is named after a family of dukes and princes who lived in the Rohan area of Brittany, France, and made the name, and a native duck, famous." Note: Tasting Table says "breed"; D'Artagnan's own blog says "hybrid of several duck breeds". The app says "cross", which both support.
3. The birds are raised to 11 weeks in New York's Catskills, five weeks longer than the average factory duck; Ariane Daguin says the extra time deepens the flavour and improves the meat-to-bone ratio.
   Source: Tasting Table, same URL (snippet, summary): "The birds are raised humanely to 11 weeks in New York's Catskills, five weeks longer than the average factory-raised duck's lifespan, and D'Artagnan owner Ariane Daguin insists that the maturity allows the flavors to deepen and makes a better meat-to-bone ratio."
4. Raised cage-free on corn and soy, never given antibiotics, hormones or steroids; the meat is juicy, tender and rose-coloured with a mild taste.
   Source: center-of-the-plate.com, same URL (snippet, summary): "Never administered antibiotics, hormones, or steroids the Rohan duck is raised cage-free and fed a diet of corn and soy. The Rohan boasts juicy, tender, rose-colored meat with a mild taste".
5. Tasting Table's test kitchen: "The meat was intensely rich without being overpowering", with "clean, buttery notes".
   Source: Tasting Table, same URL (snippet, quoted).
6. D'Artagnan was founded in 1985 by Ariane Daguin and George Faison, who met working at a charcuterie shop in New York City; a farmer with the first domestically raised foie gras in the United States needed distribution, and they started the company to sell it, first whole raw lobes to New York chefs.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%27Artagnan_(food_company) (snippet, summary): "D'Artagnan was founded in 1985 by Ariane Daguin and George Faison" "their partnership formed while working together at a charcuterie shop in New York City" "they met a farmer with the first domestically raised foie gras in the United States who was looking for distribution ... D'Artagnan originally sold whole lobes of raw foie gras to the chefs of New York City."
7. Ariane Daguin was born in Auch, in Gascony, the eldest child of the Michelin-starred chef André Daguin, famous in France for foie gras and Gascon cooking. The company is named for the Gascon musketeer d'Artagnan.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariane_Daguin and https://www.thedailymeal.com/meet-culinary-entrepreneurs-ariane-daguin (snippets, summary): "Ariane Daguin was born in Auch, in the Gascony region of France, and is the first child of Michelin-starred chef André Daguin." "Her father, Chef André Daguin, is famous throughout France for his artistry with foie gras and other Gascon specialties." The name: https://center-of-the-plate.com/2020/02/17/what-is-the-story-behind-the-name-dartagnan/ (title only). Expert knowledge: the name honours the Gascon musketeer of Dumas's novel; the app says only "named for the Gascon musketeer".
8. D'Artagnan is headquartered, with its distribution centre, in Union, New Jersey; its first duck and foie gras came from a farm in the foothills of the Catskills.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%27Artagnan_(food_company) and https://provisioneronline.com/articles/103148-the-dartagnan-philosophy-gourmet-food-and-happy-animals (snippets, summary): "has its corporate headquarters and distribution center in Union, New Jersey." "One duck farm, nestled in the foothills of New York's Catskill Mountains, is where it all began for Daguin." "In 1985, Ariane seized an opportunity to market duck and foie gras from the Catskills".
9. Brennan's link: the menu prints "Rohan" on both dishes and does not print D'Artagnan. A third-party write-up of the 80th-anniversary dinner tasting lists "hazelnut-crusted, 14-day dry-aged Rohan duck" with brown butter and sweet potato purée, delicata squash, sage and duck jus, which matches the website version recorded in verified-facts.json and differs from the pasted card's cane syrup peaches.
   Source: https://thecorkscrewconcierge.com/?p=1491 (snippet, summary): "Their 80th Anniversary Dinner Tasting Menu features hazelnut-crusted, 14-day dry-aged Rohan duck, complemented by brown butter and sweet potato purée, local delicata squash, sage, and duck jus."
10. The farm: no source names the farm or family that raises the Rohan. Crescent Duck Farm (Aquebogue, Long Island, started 1908 by Henry Corwin with 30 White Pekin ducks) appeared in the search but is a separate Pekin farm with no stated link to the Rohan; it must not be named as the Rohan's farm.

## 4. Nueske's (applewood-smoked bacon in the Succotash)

1. The family recipes came with Wilhelm and Wilhelmina Nueske, who emigrated from Prussia to Wisconsin in 1887; two generations later, in 1933, R.C. Nueske started selling applewood-smoked meats made to the family recipe.
   Source: https://www.zingermansdeli.com/2019/09/nueskes-applewood-smoked-bacon/ and https://art.zingermans.com/Product/bacon-lovers-gifts/G-CAMP-1 (snippets): "The recipe came from Wilhelm and Wilhelmina Nueske, who emigrated from Prussia to Wisconsin in 1887. Two generations later in 1933, R.C. Nueske, started selling applewood smoked meats made with the family recipe."
2. In 1933, at 19, Robert Carl Nueske left the family farm at Wittenberg, Wisconsin, to his grandparents, parents, two brothers and a sister, traded his plough for a delivery truck and sold the family's sausages and smoked meats to resorts in the north of the state.
   Source: https://www.saveur.com/article/Kitchen/Smoke-and-Sizzle (snippet, summary): "In 1933, at the age of 19, Robert Carl Nueske decided to rustle up a little business for his family's Wittenberg, Wisconsin, farm. Leaving his grandparents, parents, two brothers, and a sister to work the land and raise the animals, the enterprising young man traded his plow for a delivery truck and began peddling the family's homemade sausages and smoked meats to resorts in the northern part of the state."
3. In the 1940s he bought a butcher shop and designed and built six smokehouses.
   Source: Saveur, same URL (snippet, summary): "In the '40s, Nueske expanded the business, buying a butcher shop and designing and building six smokehouses".
4. The bacon is smoked for about a full day over embers of real Wisconsin applewood logs: Zingerman's says "a full 24 hours"; another account gives "a 20- to 24-hour smoking" in "16 steel-lined concrete-block smokehouses heated by open fires of applewood logs".
   Source: https://www.zingermansdeli.com/2019/09/nueskes-applewood-smoked-bacon/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueske%27s_Applewood_Smoked_Meats (snippets, summary): "Nueske's bacon is smoked for a full 24 hours over glowing applewood embers, and the meats are prepared with a 20- to 24-hour smoking in '16 steel-lined concrete-block smokehouses heated by open fires of applewood logs'." "The company only uses real logs of Wisconsin applewood".
5. It is third-generation family owned and run; Tanya Nueske, of the third generation, is chief executive and says the family still smokes as her grandfather did.
   Source: https://insightonbusiness.com/podcast-smoked-since-birth-tanya-nueske-embraces-family-legacy/ and https://www.meatpoultry.com/articles/15033-nueske-s-founded-on-bacon-and-thriving-on-tradition (snippets, summary): "Nueske's Applewood Smoked Meats is a third-generation family-owned and -operated smokehouse located in Wittenberg, Wisconsin." "Tanya Nueske, CEO of Nueske's Applewood Smoked Meats, is part of the 3rd generation ... the family still uses the same methods that her grandfather did for smoking meats, smoking over genuine logs of Wisconsin applewood in custom-built smokehouses."
6. Besides bacon it smokes hams, poultry and sausages.
   Source: same search (snippet, summary): "also creates a variety of smoked hams, poultry, sausages, and other fine smoked meats."
7. Brennan's link: the menu prints "Nueske's Bacon" on the dinner Succotash only. The breakfast Thick-Cut Bacon, the children's bacon and the Roost Burger's bacon carry no name.

Expert knowledge: applewood gives a sweeter, milder smoke than hickory. Pronunciation NOO-skee is often heard; the house dossier records NOO-skeez as contested, so follow the house.

Correction to earlier work: components/dishes.json key nueskes-bacon says "smoked over applewood for at least 24 hours". The sources give "a full 24 hours" and "20- to 24-hour", so "at least" overstates it; say "about 24 hours".
