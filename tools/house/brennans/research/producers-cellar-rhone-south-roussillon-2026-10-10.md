## 1. Domaine Raspail-Ay

1. Raspail-Ay is a family estate in Gigondas, under the Dentelles de Montmirail. Source: research/bottle-facts-cards-a-2026-10-04.json, bin 32733, marked "knowledge": "Domaine Raspail-Ay is a family estate in Gigondas, a Southern Rhône village beneath the limestone ridge of the Dentelles de Montmirail; Gigondas reds are Grenache-led with Syrah and often Mourvèdre." The wine list agrees in its own words: static/shared/packs/brennans-new-orleans.winelist.v1.json, producer Domaine Raspail-Ay: "A traditional Gigondas family domaine." This is the only claim about the estate itself that the repositories support; it is not search confirmed.
2. Gigondas has been a cru since 1971, and its red rests on Grenache. Source: sommelierscodex/js/data-advanced.js, a-nkyxd2ps: "Gigondas was promoted from Côtes du Rhône Villages to cru in 1971 for reds and rosés only; the white joins them from 2023, while the red still requires Grenache at its core." And data-producers.js, p-saint-cosme, traps: "Gigondas has been a cru since 1971, no longer a Côtes du Rhône Villages commune".
3. The Dentelles make Gigondas firmer and fresher than the plain. Source: sommelierscodex/js/data-intro.js, i-4gm04kqo: "Vineyards climbing to the foot of the jagged limestone teeth of the Dentelles de Montmirail give a Grenache red firmer and fresher than the plain below. Which cru?" answer Gigondas, "exp": "Altitude and limestone give cooler nights and better acidity than the valley floor. It is the standard recommendation for a guest who finds Chateauneuf too heavy."
4. The Raspail-Ay red is Grenache-led with Syrah, by the pack; the exact blend is a sommelier question. Source: the pack, w-f53clius, grapes ["Grenache","Syrah"]; Tell me about it: "Gigondas reds are Grenache-led, with Syrah and often a little Mourvèdre; the exact blend of this bottle is a question for the sommelier."
