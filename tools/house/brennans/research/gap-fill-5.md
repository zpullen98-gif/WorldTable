## 1. Conventions used in every record

- say_it: English respelling, stressed syllable in capitals. Hyphens separate syllables.
- Nasal vowels: written "ahn", "awn", "an", "ohn" or "un" followed by the house note "nasal: the n is not sounded, let the vowel go through the nose". A server who simply drops the final n and keeps the vowel long will be understood everywhere.
- German umlauts: ü is "ee" said with rounded lips (write "ü"); ö is "er" without the r (write "ö"); ä is "eh" or "ay" without the glide. "ei" is "eye", "ie" is "ee", a final g in -ig is a soft "ikh".
- Shared fields for every record in this file unless a row says otherwise: source = "expert knowledge, no live source reachable in this run"; source_date = 2026-10-03; status = unverified.
- pronunciation_verdict: where say_it_pdf exists, the verdict judges it (right, refine, change, contested). Where no PDF form exists, "right" means one accepted form, "contested" means more than one form is heard on real floors and the house should choose.
- Spellings match the by-the-glass list as the task prints it, because the app keys lore and producers by exact name.

## 8. Nasal vowel and umlaut primer for the term records

- French nasal vowels appear in: Crémant, Durand, Mâcon, Sauvignon, Blanc, Beaumes-de-Venise (no), Durban, Vincent, Moulin, Issan, Provence (no, final s sounded), Banyuls (light), Grand Cru, négociant, vin doux, Chateaubriand, confit, Orléans, Reims, Maison. Rule for servers: shape the vowel, do not close the lips on the n. If in doubt, say the vowel long and stop.
- German umlauts appear in: Ürzig (ü), Prälat (ä), Spätlese (ä), Rösti (ö, Swiss). Rule: ü = "ee" with rounded lips; ä = "eh" or "ay"; ö = "er" without the r. German "ei" is "eye" (feinherb), "ie" is "ee" (Riesling), and a final -ig is a soft "ikh" (Ürzig).
- Galician x is "sh" (Bruxas, Baixas). Italian "sch" is "sk" (maraschino). Catalan-French Banyuls keeps its final s.
