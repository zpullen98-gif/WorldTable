# Brennan's deep research, 3 October 2026: the dossier

Built on Saturday 3 October 2026 for a server starting at Brennan's, 417 Royal Street, on Monday 5 October. This folder extends what the house already held (the 51 page server guide read from the live menus on 26 September, the page snapshots, the earlier verified research and the shipped pack of 62 dishes, 23 drinks and 20 wines). The great gap going in was the full bottle list. This pass found about 150 bottles on it by name, vintage and price, none of which the pack held before.

## What is in the folder

- `sweep-*.json`: nine research lenses (champagne, burgundy-white, burgundy-red, france-other, old-world, new-world, food, bar, programme). Each record quotes its snippet, URL, site, query and confidence.
- `verify-1.json`, `verify-2.json`, `verify-3.json`: three refuter passes that held, refuted or left unverifiable every one of the 366 sweep records.
- `catalogue.json`: the merged, described catalogue (this pass). 207 items, each with a description of 120 to 200 words a new server can study, suggested pairings (`pairs_with`), every source snippet, the verifier's notes and a status per price. It also carries 48 programme and event facts (`facts`) and the 76 records left out, with the reason (`excluded`).
- `dossier.md`: this file.

## The rule for what got in

An item is in the catalogue when a verifier held it, or when it stands single-source at medium or high confidence. Refuted records never got in. Single-source records at low confidence never got in. Records naming the same dish, drink or wine were merged: the à la carte Redfish Véronique and its tasting course are one item, and three vintages of Dujac Folatières are one item with each price labelled by vintage and marked `verified` or `single-source` on its own.

Status `verified` means at least one record behind the item held; check the per-price status before quoting a price, because some vintages of a verified wine are still single-source.

Three items were derived rather than copied one to one, and each says so in its `verifier_notes`:
- The two Dönnhoff Goldkapsel Auslesen (Niederhäuser Hermannshöhle 2023 at $145, Oberhäuser Brücke 2023 at $135): the sweep record said only "Dönhoff selections" and held; the verifier's fresh Binwise snippet named these two bottles, and the verifier asked for the unnamed record to be replaced by them. They are carried as single-source.
- Piper-Heidsieck 'Brennan's Essential' Brut bottle ($125, $75 at Bubbles): taken from the programme lens's Bubbles at Brennan's fact record.
- Cos d'Estournel 2015 magnum at $600: printed in the verifier's fresh Binwise snippet and added to the Cos item as a single-source price.

## Method and its limits

- Every record stands on a WebSearch result, and the search tool returns a model-written summary over the result pages, not raw page text. A snippet is evidence only for what it says. Direct reads of brennansneworleans.com, hub.binwise.com and most sites were blocked, so the Binwise bottle list was never read line by line; it was reconstructed from summaries of it.
- The index dates of the Binwise and house pages are unknown. Prices and vintages may predate the fall list, and a few low prices ($45 to $65) may be glasses or half bottles. Formats are often assumed (written "bottle (assumed, not stated)") rather than printed.
- The session's search budget (200 calls) was spent by the sweeps and the first two verifiers. The third verifier, and this cataloguer, ran no new searches; the third verifier tested its records against the guide, the page snapshots and the earlier research instead. So many records are "unverifiable" only because no second search was possible, not because anything contradicts them.
- A "held" Binwise record usually means the same list was summarised again by a differently worded query. That confirms what the list printed; it is not a second, independent publisher.
- Descriptions combine the sourced facts with general knowledge of the grape, region, producer, dish or drink. House facts are written as what "the list prints" or "the menu prints". Producer histories and tasting notes are general knowledge, not house claims. `pairs_with` is pairing advice written for the Codex, not the house's own pairings; the house published none for any bottle except the tasting menu pours.
- No allergen content is given anywhere.

## The closer's check, 3 October 2026

Every file in this folder was read again and tested by script. All fourteen JSON files parse. No file carries an em dash, an en dash, any of their entities or a spaced double hyphen (the table rules above are plain Markdown). The closer ran no web search.

What was out of step, and what was done:

- **A refuted price inside the catalogue.** verify-3 refuted the Brennan's Champagne Cocktail at $15.00 (sweep-bar.json records[5]) against the guide's $20, but the catalogue item, built from the champagne lens's unrefuted twin record, still carried $15 as its price. The item now carries $20 as its one price, citing the guide line verify-3 quotes, and is verified at high confidence; the $15 is kept only in the description and the verifier notes as the Bubbles page figure to ask about. The counts moved by one: 83 verified, 124 single-source, 76 high, 127 medium.
- **Two catalogue prices not verbatim in their snippets.** The Pommery sabering spectator ticket read "$20 + tax (gratuity included)" where the snippet prints only "$20 spectator ticket"; it now reads "$20". The Baked Apple's $80 menu price stood on no cited snippet; the guide's line "Breakfast tasting ($80) includes tasting portions and all listed drinks." is now cited on the item.
- **Pairings prices without a cited source.** The 80th Anniversary dinner tasting price on the Hazelnut-Crusted Duck card and the Thanksgiving $80 and $35 on the turkey card now carry `menu_price_source`, quoting the programme and food snippets. The six new dish cards whose first glass is a pack wine (Eggs Benedict through Veal Grillades) now carry `glass_pack_ref` and `glass_price_source`.
- **Pairing names that did not match the catalogue.** Fifty `pairs_with` names on the catalogue's dish items were written in the sweep spelling (double quotes, "1er Cru", no format) and matched no catalogue or pack name; each now carries the catalogue item's exact name, or the pack's for Louis Jadot Beaune 1er Cru 2023. Four wine cards named "Hazelnut-Crusted Duck (tasting course four, if tonight's build)" as a dish; the dish is now the plain name with the condition in a `note`.

After the fixes, every price in catalogue.json appears verbatim in one of the item's own snippets, and every price in pairings.json appears verbatim in the snippets of the catalogue item it cites, in the pack or guide it cites, or in its `menu_price_source`. Every wine and dish a pairing names is a catalogue or pack item. Left as written, by judgement: three dish cards use the pack's name for a dish whose catalogue item is spelt otherwise (New Orleans or Louisiana Bread Pudding, Roost Burger, Fries with Pink Sauce), and the tasting progressions name courses in running prose ("Turtle Soup and Eggs Hussarde", "Duck (either build)", "Bananas Foster"), each of which is a pack dish.
