## 1. Louis Roederer, Reims (Collection 244 and Cristal 2015)

1. The founding and the name: "The house began as Dubois Père & Fils"; one source says the company "was later owned by Nicolas-Henri Schreider, who is considered the official founder in 1776", another that "Dubois Père et Fils was first mentioned in 1760". Wikipedia: "Louis Roederer inherited the company from his uncle in 1833, renamed it eponymously." The wein-plus glossary: Schreider "hired his nephew Louis Roederer (1809-1870) in 1827", and "after Schreider's death in 1833 the heir renamed the company Roederer."
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Roederer and https://glossary.wein-plus.eu/roederer and https://www.aguttes.com/en/artistes/roederer (summary).
2. Cristal and the Tsar: "Cristal was created in 1876 at the behest of Tsar Alexander II, who had deemed it risky to imbibe the same Champagne as his court-goers. The clear-glass bottle was reportedly a response to assassination fears, and supposedly, the flat floor was required to prevent a small explosive device from being buried in the indentation of the bottle bottom." Wein-plus: the champagne "was first delivered to Russia in 1876". Wikipedia: "Tsar Nicholas II nominated Louis Roederer as the official wine supplier to the Imperial Court of Russia"; Aguttes dates the warrant to 1908.
   Source: https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2019/07/idealwine-update-a-clear-winner/ and https://glossary.wein-plus.eu/cristal and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Roederer (summary).
3. After 1917: "As Cristal was made for the exclusive use of the tsars, when the Russian monarchy fell in 1917, the label disappeared. In 1945, however, Roederer started to remake the expression"; another source gives 1945 as "the first tête de cuvée release for sale".
   Source: https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/louis-roederer/ and https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2019/07/idealwine-update-a-clear-winner/ (summary).
4. Camille Olry-Roederer: "Léon Olry-Roederer, who headed the firm, died in 1932 and left the house to his widow, Camille." "Camille took over the reins at Louis Roederer on the death of her husband, Léon Olry-Roederer, in 1932, and remained at the helm until 1975" (one source says she died in 1974 after 42 years). "Camille was smart enough to purchase vineyards in the top Champagne regions whenever she could during her stewardship." The house's press file: she served still wines from Roederer's finest vineyards, "from Avize or Cumières". The house calls itself "this family-owned Champagne House", headed by Camille Olry-Roederer's great-grandson.
   Source: https://winereviewonline.com/the-louis-roederer-dynasty/ and https://www.louis-roederer.com/sites/all/themes/roederer/files/LR_Hommage%20%C3%A0%20Camille_Press%20File_Vintage_EN.pdf and https://thedrinksbusiness.com/?p=499137 (summary).
5. The estate and Cristal's vines: "all the grapes in Cristal & other vintage cuvee's come from their own vineyards"; holdings given as "240 hectares" or "242 hectares" (merchants, undated; not used as present fact); Cristal's grand cru sources named as "Verzenay, Verzy, Beaumont-sur-Vesle, Aÿ and Mareuil-sur-Aÿ for the Pinots, and Avize, Mesnil and Cramant for the Chardonnays"; "today some 45 plots make up the 'Cristal Estates'"; of the 2012: "This banner vintage is the first 100% biodynamic vintage." One source: "all of Roederer's vineyards are now farmed organically, and the parcels destined for Cristal biodynamically."
   Source: https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/louis-roederer/ and https://sf.flatiron-wines.com/collections/discountable/products/louis-roederer-champagne-brut-cristal-2012 and https://thefinestbubble.com/champagne-louis-roederer-cristal-2012-75cl (summary).
6. Cristal 2015: "a blend of 60% Pinot Noir and 40% Chardonnay", "100% biodynamically farmed grapes" (a Wine Enthusiast note calls the vineyards organic); "a dosage of 7 grams per liter", "disgorged in November 2022"; "does not undergo malolactic fermentation, and 25% of the wine is aged in oak"; "fruit from all 45 parcels at the domaine, which has not happened since 2002". Cristal Rosé 2015 is a different wine (56 percent Pinot Noir, 44 Chardonnay).
   Source: https://hedonism.co.uk/products/louis-roederer-cristal-2015 and https://www.jjbuckley.com/wine/2015-louis-roederer-cristal/2015-393-750 and https://mmdusa.net/assets/uploads/materials/sell-sheet/Cristal_2015_Sell_Sheet_10-16-23.pdf (summary).
7. Collection 244: Harvey Nichols: "Louis Roederer has ceased production of its best-selling Brut Premier NV, replacing it with this new multi-vintage cuvée"; the house's sell sheet calls it "a worthy successor to Brut Premier". "The '244' refers to the youngest vintage in the blend, 2019, which was Roederer's 244th harvest"; "the 2019 wines make up 54% of the blend", with "36% perpetual reserve wines from 2012 to 2018 and 10% oak-aged reserve wines"; "the Réserve perpétuelle was created in 2012"; grapes given as 41 percent Chardonnay, 33 Pinot Noir, 26 Meunier.
   Source: https://harveynichols.com/louis-roederer/collection-244-champagne-mv-half-bottle-375ml-954286-na-136517 and https://mmdusa.net/portfolio/champagne-louis-roederer/collection/244 and https://mmdusa.net/assets/uploads/materials/sell-sheet/CLR_Collection_244_Sell_Sheet.pdf and https://comptoirdesmillesimes.com/en/roederer/collection-244-louis-roederer.html (summary).
8. Pichon Lalande: research/components-wines-2026-10-05.md (search 43): Château Pichon Longueville Comtesse de Lalande "was sold to the owners of Champagne Louis Roederer in January 2007"; research/bottle-facts-cards-b-2026-10-04.json (n2278): "a second growth of the 1855 classification in Pauillac", "owned since 2007 by the Champagne house Louis Roederer". The shipped wine list names Deutz as "part of the Louis Roederer group" and Roederer Estate as "the California home of the Champagne house Louis Roederer" (not searched in this pass).

## 2. Veuve Clicquot, Reims (Yellow Label)

1. The founding and the widow: LVMH: "Philippe Clicquot, a textile merchant from Reims, founded the Champagne House" in 1772; "After his son died in 1805, his young widow, Barbe Nicole Clicquot Ponsardin, took over the reins of the House, aged 27." The Oxford Companion entry: she renamed the company Veuve Clicquot Ponsardin.
   Source: https://www.lvmh.com/en/our-maisons/wines-spirits/veuve-clicquot and https://app.ckbk.com/reference/wine05383c22s001e074 (summary).
2. The three firsts: LVMH: she "created the first vintage champagne in 1810"; the house says she "invented the riddling table in 1816" (Millesima credits her "with the help of cellar master Antoine-Aloys de Muller"; the Oxford Companion describes her adapting a piece of her own furniture); in 1818 she "creates the first 'rosé d'assemblage' by blending some of her Bouzy red wines with her champagne". Wikipedia: a process "still used by the majority of champagne producers".
   Source: https://www.lvmh.com/our-maisons/wines-spirits/veuve-clicquot and https://www.millesima-usa.com/blog/veuve-clicquot-champagne-master-of-innovation.html and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_Clicquot_Ponsardin and https://www.mhdkk.com/en/brands/veuve_clicquot/history (summary).
3. Yellow Label: retail listings carry the house line "The love of Pinot Noir since 1772" and the quotation "Our black grapes give the finest white wines", Madame Clicquot, 1825; the blend about 50 to 55 percent Pinot Noir, 15 to 20 Meunier, 28 to 33 Chardonnay, from 50 to 60 crus, with reserve wines 25 to 45 percent of the blend, "aged up to 15 years"; the Yellow Label "was created for a British market that wanted dry wines and was trademarked in 1877"; another merchant: "first produced in 1877", the colour classed as Pantone 137 C.
   Source: https://sainsburys.co.uk/groceries/product/veuve-clicquot-yellow-label-brut-non-vintage-champagne-1-5l and https://zoom.ocado.com/veuve-clicquot-yellow-label-magnum-champagne-nv-1-5l-66285011 and https://us.callmewine.com/champagne-veuve-clicquot-P7808.htm (summary).
4. La Grande Dame: "the cuvée was created in 1962 and first marketed in 1972 to celebrate the bicentenary of the Veuve Clicquot house"; another: "the 1962 vintage as the label's debut year, released in 1972 for the house's 200th anniversary".
   Source: https://www.vinsetmillesimes.com/en/6100-veuve-clicquot and https://caskcartel.com/en-ch/products/1962-veuve-clicquot-la-grande-dame-brut (summary).
5. The Baltic bottles: "In July 2010, divers discovered bottles of Champagne in a shipwreck off the Åland Islands. Following a successful salvage operation of 145 bottles, it was found the bounty included some of the world's oldest Champagnes"; "Forty-six of the bottles are Veuve Clicquot champagne"; Decanter: "some of them were Veuve Clicquot non-vintage from the early 1830s"; at auction in 2011 a Veuve Clicquot bottle fetched €30,000.
   Source: https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2011/04/shipwreck-champagne-to-go-under-the-hammer/ and https://yle.fi/a/3-5347451 and https://www.decanter.com/wine-news/champagne-still-fresh-after-nearly-two-centuries-in-baltic-44084 and https://m.drinksint.com/news/fullstory.php/aid/2163/.html (summary).
6. The owner: "Louis Vuitton bought the Reims house in 1986, and the group that became LVMH was formed the following year" (Wikipedia, Gerrard Seel, Seewines), that is in 1987; retailer pages read October 2026 say it "is owned by luxury giant Louis Vuitton Möet Hennessy (LVMH)".
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veuve_Clicquot and https://gerrardseel.co.uk/collections/veuve-clicquot and https://seewines.com/en/brands/vov-kliko and https://www.vinsetmillesimes.com/en/6100-veuve-clicquot (summary).

research/bottle-facts-cards-a-2026-10-04.json (n589): "Yellow Label is its Pinot Noir-led non-vintage Brut with Meunier and Chardonnay; the label is an orange yellow."

Expert knowledge: Veuve Clicquot is said vuhv klee-KOH (the pack's say line); veuve is French for widow.

## 3. Charles Heidsieck, Reims (Réserve Brut)

1. The founder: several sources give 1851 as the founding year, "when Charles-Camille Heidsieck was about 29"; "a larger than life dandy", and "a New York paper coined 'Champagne Charlie'". Decanter adds that he founded the house with his cousin Ernest Henriot (one source only).
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Heidsieck_(Champagne) and https://comitecolbert.com/en/members/champagne-charles-heidsieck and https://www.decanter.com/premium/charles-heidsieck-collection-crayeres-382294 (summary).
2. America: Wikipedia: he made the 1852 trip to the United States and became "the first merchant to market his own champagne there"; Folio: four separate voyages.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Heidsieck_(Champagne) and https://www.foliowine.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/charles-heidsieck-brand-2023-ss.pdf (summary).
3. New Orleans and Fort Jackson: "Charles Heidsieck, the champagne merchant known as 'Champagne Charlie,' was arrested in Union-occupied New Orleans in 1862 and held at Fort Jackson, Louisiana." He "went south to collect unpaid debts after his New York agent refused payment"; one account says he arrived on 5 May 1862 to find the city fallen to Union forces.
