## 3. Cream, the United States standard

- Heavy cream, in the United States, is cream with not less than 36 per cent milkfat (21 CFR 131.150, the FDA standard of identity; the name "heavy whipping cream" is allowed for the same product). https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/131.150 ; https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2023-title21-vol2/pdf/CFR-2023-title21-vol2-sec131-150.pdf
- Light whipping cream is cream containing not less than 30 per cent but less than 36 per cent milkfat (21 CFR 131.157). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-131/subpart-B/section-131.157
- The menu prints cream as whipped cream on the children's Vanilla French Toast, as chive cream on the Fried Pickles ("Medley Of Housemade Pickles & Chive Cream"), as creamed spinach (its own side and the Eggs Sardou), as the mustard cream sauce of the Poussin à la Moutarde, and as crème fraîche on the Baked Apple and the Steak Tartare Cannoli (guide.txt; pages/brennansneworleans-menus-pasted-2026-10-03.txt). ## 5. Green beans

- The green bean is the immature pod of the common bean, Phaseolus vulgaris, which originated in Central and South America; the species was domesticated twice, about eight thousand years ago, once in Mesoamerica (most likely the Oaxaca valley in Mexico) and once in the Andes, where the site is unresolved between southern Peru and Bolivia with northern Argentina, so the card says Mexico and the Andes rather than Mexico and Peru. The thin French type is sold as haricots verts. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_bean ; https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s13059-017-1190-6 (Genome Biology, 2017, the two domestications) ; https://www.genesys-pgr.org/c/beans
- The stringless bean: Calvin Keeney of Le Roy, New York, called the father of the stringless bean, bred snap beans from 1884 to 1911; the variety that spread his work was the Burpee Stringless Green Pod, released by W. Atlee Burpee in 1894, so the cards say the stringless pod was bred in the nineteenth century and print no single year. https://saveseeds.org/biography/keeney/index.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_bean
- The menu prints green beans once, on the children's "Sautéed Gulf Fish with Potato and Green Beans" (pages/brennansneworleans-kids-menus-2026-10-03.txt). How they are cooked is not printed.

## 6. Housemade pickles, and the Arkansas origin of fried pickles

- The first fried dill pickles sold anywhere were sold in the summer of 1963 by Bernell "Fatman" Austin at his Duchess Drive-In in Atkins, Arkansas, at fifteen cents for an order of fifteen hamburger slices, across the highway from the Atkins Pickle Company. The Duchess closed in 1968; Austin died in 1999. The Hollywood Cafe in Mississippi also claims the invention, but it opened in 1969. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/fried-dill-pickles-2922/ ; https://www.thedailymeal.com/1603848/fried-pickles-arkansas-origin ; https://tastingtable.com/1180318/the-southern-drive-in-where-fried-pickles-found-fame
- The cards print the year 1963 and the town, Atkins, and no person: Austin's name stays here. One newspaper column (Arkansas Online, 18 August 2014, https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2014/aug/18/whats-in-a-pickle/) gives the summer of 1961; the Encyclopedia of Arkansas's 1963 is kept as the reference date.
- The menu prints pickles twice: "Medley Of Housemade Pickles & Chive Cream" on the Fried Pickles and "Pickles" on the Roost Burger (pages/brennansneworleans-menus-pasted-2026-10-03.txt, Bar Snacks). Which vegetables are in the medley is not printed; the guide's fried pickles line (pdf p49) prints the name and the price only.

## 7. Salami

- Salami is the Italian family of cured sausages: fermented, air-dried meat, usually pork, salted and hung to dry, popular for centuries because it keeps at room temperature for weeks. Genoa salami is the style named for Genoa in Liguria, pork with or without beef, seasoned with garlic, black pepper and often red wine; the Ligurian original, the salame genovese of the Val Polcevera and Sant'Olcese, is a mixed beef and pork sausage made today by a handful of small plants, and Fred Plotkin's 1997 Ligurian cookbook notes that the pork-only Genoa salami sold in groceries abroad bears only a distant connection to it, so the card says named for the city, not from Liguria. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salami ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_salami ; https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/6472447e2c1d629bc9794eae (ARPAL and ASL study, 2005) ; https://app.ckbk.com/section/reci10718b01s001ss007/meats (Plotkin)

## 9. Short pastry, and the Louisiana meat pie

- Short pastry is flour and cold fat rubbed to crumbs and bound with cold water; the fat coats the flour so the dough stays tender and crumbly, which is what "short" means. A sweet short dough adds sugar and egg. This is a cook's common knowledge; no maker is named.
- The Natchitoches meat pie, Louisiana's best known hand pie, is a fried half-moon of pastry filled with seasoned pork and beef, documented by 1826 and an official state food (research/components-dishes-2026-10-05.md, item 51). The Roost's Crawfish Meat Pies print "Chive Remoulade" and nothing of the pastry (pages/brennansneworleans-menus-pasted-2026-10-03.txt, Bar Snacks); whether they are fried or baked is not printed. The Lemon Tart prints "sweet pastry crumb" (guide.txt, pdf p35).
