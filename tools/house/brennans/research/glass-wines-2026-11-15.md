# The four new glass wines of the 15 November 2026 sheet: what was searched and what stands

Written 15 November 2026 for the staged chunk `_staging/nov-wines`. The sheet itself is filed as
`pages/brennans-sheets-2026-11-15.txt` (Wines by the Glass, lines 104 to 130); its tasting words are the house's
and are used as printed. Eight web searches were run on the day. A fact is used on a card only where two sources
agree or the producer's own page says it. No living person is named here or on any card: a founder who is alive is
"its founder". Vintage specific cellar work that no source gave for the bottle poured is sent to the sommelier.

## 1. Lemelson Vineyards, Tikka's Run Pinot Gris 2022 (Willamette Valley, Oregon)

Stands (two sources or the producer's own words as retailers quote them):
- Lemelson Vineyards is an estate winery at Carlton, Oregon, in the Willamette Valley, with a gravity flow winery
  (Travel Oregon; Roadtrippers; the Carlton Business Association; Winebow's Upper Left sheet).
- All its vineyards have been farmed organically since the beginning and certified organic by Oregon Tilth since
  2004 (the winery's text as quoted by Bottle Barn and 67 Wine). It grows Pinot Noir, Pinot Gris, Chardonnay and
  Riesling.
- Tikka's Run is named for Tikka, the founder's Great Pyrenees dog, who patrolled the perimeter of the Pinot Gris
  vineyards; the wine is a tribute to her (wine.com, 2013, 2015 and 2019 listings; Bottle Barn).
- Pinot Gris and Pinot Grigio are one grape; the Oregon style is the fuller one (expert knowledge).

Does not stand, so it is on no card:
- The founding year: sources give 1997 and 1999 for the estate and 1999 and 2000 for the winery.
- The 2022 cellar work. One trade sheet reads 80 percent tank and 20 percent neutral oak with six months on the
  lees, under an ambiguous heading; a question for the sommelier.
- The vineyard source of the 2022 (Chestnut Hill Vineyard in the winery's text for later vintages).

## 2. Kokomo Winery, Sonoma County Cabernet Sauvignon 2022

Stands:
- Kokomo Winery is on Dry Creek Road in Healdsburg, in the Dry Creek Valley of Sonoma County, California, at
  Timber Crest Farms (Sonoma.com; Wine Road; Prince of Pinot; Sunset).
- It was begun in 2004 and named for its founder's hometown of Kokomo, Indiana, a Midwestern town (Sunset; Prince
  of Pinot; The Drunken Cyclist; Natalie MacLean).
- The Sonoma County Cabernet Sauvignon draws mainly on Alexander Valley fruit (Bottle Barn's listing of the 2022;
  a 2021 listing; and the house's own glass sheet: "Most of the fruit is sourced from Alexander Valley in Sonoma
  County"). Alexander Valley is the warm inland valley in the north of Sonoma County where Cabernet Sauvignon
  ripens fully (expert knowledge).

Does not stand: the oak and the months in barrel of the 2022 (the one figure found is for the 2017); one retailer
files the 2022 under Sonoma Valley, against the label's Sonoma County. A question for the sommelier.

## 3. Louis Jadot, Puligny-Montrachet Premier Cru Les Pucelles 2008 (poured by Coravin)

Stands:
- Maison Louis Jadot was founded in Beaune in 1859 by Louis Henry Denis Jadot (VinePair; company-histories.com;
  the Burgundy Report profile).
- Les Pucelles is a premier cru of Puligny-Montrachet lying next to the grand cru Bâtard-Montrachet (Jadot's own
  sheet for the wine: "un Premier Cru situé à côté du Grand Cru Bâtard Montrachet"); a second source places it just
  north of Bâtard-Montrachet and Bienvenues-Bâtard-Montrachet (Wilson Daniels). The house sheet: "in very close
  proximity to the Grand Crus of Montrachet".
- Jadot's sheet for the wine: hand harvested, gently pressed, fermented in oak barrels of which a third are new,
  aged about 15 months on fine lees. The cards say barrels and fine lees and leave the figures out, since the sheet
  is not dated to 2008.
- Premier cru is the second rank of Burgundy vineyard, under grand cru (the class sheet filed on the same page).
- The Binwise list of 3 October 2026 carries the wine by the bottle at bin 2144. Its figure there is a bottle
  price and is never used as a glass price.

Does not stand: the meaning of the name Les Pucelles (no source found in the searches run); how the 2008 is
showing, which is the sommelier's to say from the open bottle.

## 4. Domaine de Courcel, Pommard Premier Cru Grand Clos des Épenots 2007 (poured by Coravin)

The glass sheet prints "Les Grands Epenots"; the Binwise list of 3 October 2026 prints "Les Grand Clos des Épenots"
2007 at bin 23632. Settled by search: the domaine's label for this wine reads Pommard Premier Cru Grand Clos des
Épenots (Epenots without the accent on some listings). Decanter lists and reviews the 2007 under that name, as do
Berry Bros. & Rudd, iDealwine, SommSelect and the Canadian importer's 2007 press pages. So the bottle list is
right about the label and the sheet names the larger premier cru the vineyard lies in.

Stands:
- The Grand Clos des Épenots is a monopole of Domaine de Courcel, a walled vineyard of close to five hectares
  (4.89 in most sources, 4.5 in one) in the northern part of the premier cru Les Grands Épenots, on the Beaune side
  of Pommard (SommSelect; Clos des Millésimes; iDealwine; Decanter).
- The estate has been in the de Courcel family since the seventeenth century, some four hundred years (Decanter's
  producer profile; Best of Wines; Alliance Wine). It also holds a plot in the premier cru Les Rugiens.
- Pommard is a village of the Côte de Beaune just south of Beaune, red wine only, Pinot Noir, with no grand cru;
  Les Rugiens and Les Épenots are its best known premiers crus; its reds are firmer and fuller than those of Volnay
  next door (expert knowledge).

Does not stand: whole bunch fermentation (one source) and late picking (none); the estate's size (10.5 or 11
hectares). None is on a card.

## The pairings the two departed wines leave

The Domaine Leflaive Mâcon-Verzé 2022 and the Inglenook Rubicon 2010 are not on the sheet and are filed under
Past wines by the glass. The dishes that poured them are re-paired from the seventeen on the sheet: the Jadot Les
Pucelles for the Louisiana Crab Claws and as the white step up; the Lemelson Pinot Gris for the Crab & Corn
Omelette and as the still white with the Turtle Soup; the de Courcel Pommard for the Roasted Chateaubriand and as
the red step up where a dish warns against young Cabernet; the Kokomo as the Cabernet beside the Chateaubriand and
the fuller glass with the Creole-Spiced Hanger Steak.
