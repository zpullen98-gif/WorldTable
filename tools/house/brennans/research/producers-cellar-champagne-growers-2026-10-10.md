# Producers in the cellar: the Champagne growers (10 October 2026)

## Method

Thirty-two WebSearch calls, standard mode, 10 October 2026, for the producer group champagne-growers of the Sommelier's Codex: JM Sélèque, Pierre Paillard, Chartogne-Taillet, Larmandier-Bernier, Jacques Selosse, Tribaut-Schloesser, Veuve Fourny et Fils and Pierre Péters. That is four a producer, because three family histories (Paillard, Veuve Fourny and Pierre Péters) needed a second and third pass to separate the family's dates from the label's. WebFetch was tried once, on the page that mattered most for an unsettled history (www.skurnik.com/producer/champagne-pierre-paillard/), and failed at the network egress ("getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND www.skurnik.com"), so every passage below is the search engine's snippet or summary of the page named, not a full read of it. Passages marked "(summary)" are the search tool's paraphrase rather than a verbatim quotation; where the tool did not say which of two pages carried a passage, both are named. Where a quoted page carried a dash, it is written here as a colon, a comma or the word "to".

What could not be confirmed, and is therefore left out of every profile or turned into a lineup question:
- The exact founding year of Pierre Paillard (1768, 1786 and 1799 are all printed) and the year the brothers took over (2012, 2016 and 2018 are all printed).
- Who exactly married whom in 1920 at Chartogne-Taillet, and which Fiacre Taillet (fifteenth century, 1485, seventeenth century or born 1700) kept the family register; the 1683 record is in one importer's German-language pages only.
- The first name of the widow behind Veuve Fourny, and when she was widowed (no source gives "Monique" or 1979); whether the Clos Faubourg Notre-Dame has been the family's since the nineteenth century or since 1930.
- Whether Tribaut-Schloesser was founded by René Schloesser (Hachette) or by the couple Jean Tribaut and Suzanne Schloesser (Sommeliers International).
- The current blend and colouring method of the Chartogne-Taillet rosé and the Veuve Fourny Premier Cru Rosé, and the base harvest and dosage of every bottle on our list (each release differs; the back label or the sommelier settles it).
- A pronunciation source for Sélèque, Paillard, Chartogne-Taillet, Larmandier-Bernier, Selosse, Tribaut-Schloesser and Fourny. Only Péters had one (one reference). The pack's own respellings are kept and marked expert knowledge.
- Hectares, scores and current ownership are not stated as present facts anywhere below; where a source gives them they are recorded as that source's dated or undated claim.

## 1. JM Sélèque, Pierry (Solessence)

1. Henri Sélèque and the first vines: "The history of the Seleque family dates back to 1965, when grandfather Henri planted the first vines near Pierry, a small town located in the Marne department. A few years later, in 1969, with the help of the Pierry cooperative, Henri marketed the first bottle." A Spanish-language page: "La finca fue creada en la década de 1960 por Henri Sélèque, originario de Polonia, y su esposa Françoise Bagnost." A Gismondi tasting note gives 1965 as the start of the estate.
   Source: https://us.callmewine.com/winery/j-m-seleque-B1706.htm and https://bottleofitaly.com/en/pages/cantine-magnum-jm-seleque (search 1; the tool did not say which of the two carried the English passage) and https://gismondionwine.com/tasting-notes/201612/champagne-j-m-slque-solessence-brut-nv?note=24911 (search 29, summary).
2. Poland and the cooperative: Henri Sélèque came from Poland to France in 1925, at the age of six; his father-in-law, Jean Bagnost, co-founded the Pierry cooperative in 1956, and the two planted their first vines together in 1965/66; Henri bottled his first wines in 1969.
   Source: https://sur-la-pointe.com/?p=5211 (Portrait: Champagne JMSélèque, dated 29 May 2026) (search 29, summary).
3. Richard Sélèque: "Father Richard, son of Henri and Francoise, took control of the maison in 1975 and, thanks to some studies in oenology, became a true vigneron of Champagne."
   Source: https://us.callmewine.com/winery/j-m-seleque-B1706.htm (search 1).
4. The 2007 split: "As they too were involved in viticulture, Champagne Sélèque was split up in 2007. That year, Richard Sélèque founded Sélèque R & JM."
   Source: https://sur-la-pointe.com/blog/2026/05/29/portrait-champagne-jmseleque/ (search 1).
5. Jean-Marc Sélèque: he studied viticulture and oenology and worked in the Napa Valley and the Yarra Valley before coming home; "In 2008, he returned to his father's estate and became a partner as early as 2009. He increasingly steers the business towards organic farming, and since 2010, partly towards biodynamic methods as well." Clos des Spiritueux says he first trained abroad at Maison Chandon. Cellarhand: "Jean-Marc, the third generation, took over duties in 2008." A forum post: "JM Sélèque est revenu au domaine en 2008 et a connu une ascension critique et commerciale."
   Source: https://sur-la-pointe.com/?p=5211 (search 1 and 29) and https://closdesspiritueux.com/champagne-j-m-seleque-pierry-rouge-2020-coteaux-champenois.html (search 29, summary) and https://www.lapassionduvin.com/champagne/40628-champagne-jm-seleque (search 25).
6. Farming, not certified: he "moved towards an organic cultivation approach, without seeking to go as far as certification", with biodynamic practices from 2010; another account: "organic, and in some cases biodynamic, but non-certified cultivation."
   Source: https://cellarhand.com.au/news/introducing-grower-champagne-from-jmseleque-of-pierry/ and https://sur-la-pointe.com/?p=5211 (search 6, summary).
7. Pierry and the estate: "Pierry is a premier cru village just outside Épernay, known as prime terroir for distinguished Meunier." The estate is based in Pierry, in the Coteaux Sud d'Épernay; Grand Cru Selections gives about 9 hectares in 45 parcels across seven villages, mainly on the southern slopes of Épernay; another page gives about 10 hectares in 48 parcels (undated, so not used as a present fact).
   Source: https://cellarhand.com.au/?p=10167 and https://vinonueva.com/products/j-m-seleque-champagne-soliste-pinot-noir-pierry-premier-cru-les-gayeres-2019 (search 6, summary) and https://grandcruselections.com/champagne-jm-seleque (search 29, summary).
8. Solessence: about 50 percent Chardonnay, 40 percent Meunier and 10 percent Pinot Noir from "Pierry, Moussy, Epernay, Mardeuil, Dizy, Boursault, Vertus"; one source: "50% of the blend is perpetual reserve that goes back to 2004"; vinified "70% Stainless steel vats, 30% oak barrels" by one account and "in equal parts stainless steel and oak" by another; "Dosage: 2g/L, Extra Brut" or 1.5 grams per litre; no fining or filtration. Base harvests differ by release (2022 and 2017 are both printed).
   Source: https://sommselect.com/products/somm2106-jmsnvsol-750 and https://us.callmewine.com/champagne-extra-brut-solessence-j-m-seleque-P21312.htm and https://hedonism.co.uk/product/jm-seleque-solessence-extra-brut and https://nyc.flatiron-wines.com/products/jm-seleque-champagne-brut-solessence-2022-base-nv-750ml.oembed (search 5, summary).
9. The Soliste wines: the wine list's tell says "the Soliste wines are single parcels and single grapes"; a US retailer lists a Soliste Pinot Noir 'Pierry Premier Cru Les Gayères' 2019; the forum notes a rosé with "5% de Coteaux Champenois PN issu des Gayères".
   Source: https://vinonueva.com/products/j-m-seleque-champagne-soliste-pinot-noir-pierry-premier-cru-les-gayeres-2019 (search 6) and https://www.lapassionduvin.com/champagne/40628-champagne-jm-seleque (search 25) and the shipped wine list (producer jm-seleque).

Expert knowledge: Sélèque is said say-LECK and Solessence soh-leh-SAHNSS, as the pack already prints them (no source found).

Brennan's link: Solessence at $100 is one of the gentlest prices among our grower bottles (Charles Mignon 'Premium Réserve' Rosé Brut is also $100 in the grower pages, and the Tribaut-Schloesser half is $50) and a first pick for the Fried Pickles; the pack's card says Chardonnay and Meunier with a little Pinot Noir from seven villages, part in oak, which agrees with items 7 and 8.

## 2. Pierre Paillard, Bouzy (Les Parcelles XVIII)

1. The family's start in Bouzy: "Around 1768, an ancestor, Antoine Paillard, bought a few vineyard plots in Bouzy and became a winegrower." Another page dates it "which began when Antoine Paillard turned his hand to winemaking in 1786", and a third says the family has made wine in the village since 1799. The family arrived in the early 1700s; earlier generations were shepherds, then innkeeper-carters and schoolmasters.
   Source: https://cdn.winescholarguild.com/producer-guide/france/champagne/1652-pierre-paillard and https://www.skurnik.com/producer/champagne-pierre-paillard/ and https://www.decanter.com/premium/brothers-in-arms-champagne-pierre-paillard-527770 (search 2, summary; the tool did not tie each date to one page).
2. Two centuries in Bouzy: "The family was originally comprised of shepherds and schoolmasters, and the Paillards have been crafting wine in the Grand Cru village of Bouzy for more than 200 years."
   Source: https://www.skurnik.com/producer/champagne-pierre-paillard/ and https://closcachet.com.au/blogs/news/domaine-pierre-paillard-bouzy-champagne-extraordinaires (search 7).
3. The brothers: Antoine and Quentin Paillard are the eighth generation; "the current heads of the domaine, brothers Antoine and Quentin Paillard, are the 8th generation of the family and took over in 2018 from their father"; another source: "Brothers Antoine and Quentin took full control of the Domaine in 2012"; a third: "since they took over together in 2016".
   The search 2 summary: "They took over from their father, Benoit, around 2018 according to one source."
   Source: https://www.skurnik.com/producer/champagne-pierre-paillard/ and https://fr.gaultmillau.com/en/wineries/pierre-paillard and https://closcachet.com.au/blogs/news/domaine-pierre-paillard-bouzy-champagne-extraordinaires (search 2 and 7, summary).
4. The label's name: "Pierre's 58-year-old son, Benoit has been master of the keys since 1973." "They have been doing so under the Pierre Paillard label for four generations now." So Pierre Paillard is the father of Benoît, the brothers' father; no result says Pierre was the first to bottle under the name.
   Source: https://grandcruselections.com/champagne-pierre-paillard-new and https://watsonswine.com/brands/champagne-pierre-paillard (search 8, summary; undated, so the age is not used).
5. The vineyard: "a historic 11-hectare vineyard, located entirely on the Bouzy Grand Cru terroir", planted to Pinot Noir (70 percent) and Chardonnay (30 percent) by one source and 60/40 by another (undated, so the hectares are not used as a present fact).
   Source: https://www.skurnik.com/producer/champagne-pierre-paillard/ and https://cdn.winescholarguild.com/producer-guide/france/champagne/1652-pierre-paillard (search 2 and 7, summary).
6. Farming and cellar: Skurnik describes the domaine as certified organic; Gault&Millau lists production as "Organic, Reasoned" and the conversion as part of the brothers' credo. Organic composts, biodynamic-inspired infusions, no synthetic herbicides or insecticides; "chaptalization, enzymes, artificial settling agents and cold stabilization are not included in their toolbox."
   Source: https://www.skurnik.com/producer/champagne-pierre-paillard/ and https://fr.gaultmillau.com/en/wineries/pierre-paillard (search 7, summary).
7. Les Parcelles XVIII: "This is mostly from the 2018 vintage with the addition of 20% Reserve wine" (another source: 30 percent); "The cuvee was assembled with fruit from primarily Bouzy and is made up of 70% Pinot Noir and 30% Chardonnay"; the XIX is a 2019 base and the XX a 2020 base. Dosage: Justerini & Brooks say no dosage and three years on lees; a tasting note says under 1 g/L and four years; a retailer: "Quite a minimal dosage".
   Source: https://dandm.com/products/p-paillard-les-parcelles-xviii-grand-cru-extra-brut-champagne and https://www.justerinis.com/fine-wines/france/champagne/pierre-paillard/les-parcelles-bouzy-grand-cru-xviii/NV and https://gismondionwine.com/tasting-notes/202301/champagne-pierre-paillard-les-parcelles-bouzy-grand-cru-xviii-nv?note=38741 (search 4, summary).
8. The wine list's tell already says "Les Parcelles carries a number for its base year", which items 7 confirm; the pack's cards still call the numeral a question for the sommelier.
   Source: the shipped wine list (producer pierre-paillard) and the pack (w-52frrp7w).

Expert knowledge: Pierre Paillard is said pee-AIR pah-YAR. Bouzy lies on the Montagne de Reims, as the wine list's producer region says, though the Binwise section files the bottle under Vallée de la Marne (the pack already warns of this). Pierre Paillard is a separate grower from Bruno Paillard, a house in Reims.

Brennan's link: Les Parcelles XVIII, $120, is the sweet spot bottle for the Louisiana Oysters; La Grande Récolte 2012 is the vintage-dated bottle beside it.

## 3. Chartogne-Taillet, Merfy (the Rosé Brut)

1. The Taillets in Merfy: "Die Weinbautätigkeit der Familie Taillet im Dorf Merfy, gelegen im Massif de St-Thierry nordwestlich von Reims, wurde erstmals 1683 urkundlich erwähnt." Other pages put a Fiacre Taillet in Merfy much earlier ("Fiacre Taillet était déjà vigneron à Merfy au XVème siècle"; another gives 1485), and the German pages describe a diary kept by "Fiacre Taillet, geboren 1700, über den Weinbau und die Weinherstellung", which can still be seen at the estate; iDealwine: "un registre présentant sa vie, son travail, ainsi que les évènements économiques, politiques et culturels de son époque". A French importer: in the seventeenth century the ancestor Fiacre Taillet kept a register of his plots and wines in the Saint-Thierry massif.
   Source: https://champagner.com/ja/chartogne-taillet-champagner and https://champagner.com/it/champagne-chartogne-taillet and https://www.idealwine.net/chartogne-taillet-a-merfy-champagne-de-vigneron and https://www.hachette-vins.com/guide-vins/producteurs/16127/chartogne-taillet (search 3 and 31, summary).
2. The two families: the German pages say the Chartogne family came to Merfy in 1870 and that a marriage in 1920 joined the two names, which they describe as the founding of Chartogne-Taillet. (The tool's summary names the couple as Marie Chartogne and Étienne Taillet; one source only, so the profile does not name them.)
   Source: https://champagner.com/ja/chartogne-taillet-champagner (search 3, summary).
3. Alexandre Chartogne: he took over in 2006, at 22 by one account and 23 by another, "from his parents Philippe and Elisabeth"; he worked with Anselme Selosse before coming home; Gault&Millau: he belongs to "the generation formed under the influence of Anselme Selosse", and "since 2006, he has been working on this 11-hectare estate in Merfy".
   Source: https://www.decanter.com/wine/producer-profiles/grower-champagne-chartogne-taillet-270567/ and https://shrinetothevine.co.uk/blogs/offers/chartogne-taillet (search 31, summary) and https://fr.gaultmillau.com/en/wineries/chartogne-taillet (search 9, summary).
4. The only grower in the village: Chartogne-Taillet is in Merfy, north-west of Reims, "the only récoltant-manipulant (grower-producer) in the village"; about 11 hectares in about fifteen parcels, across Merfy, Chenay and Saint-Thierry (undated, so the hectares are not used as a present fact).
   Source: https://www.decanter.com/wine/producer-profiles/grower-champagne-chartogne-taillet-270567/ (search 31, summary) and https://www.champagneclub.com/?p=47216 (search 3, summary).
5. Sand over chalk: the Merfy vineyards sit on the southern slopes of the Massif de Saint-Thierry, on sands and clays over chalk; in Les Barres the sand reaches almost three metres down before the roots meet the chalk; Les Orizeaux is more calcareous, its topsoil still sandy, its roots more than four metres deep.
   Source: https://grandsbourgognes.com/en/wine-estates/champagne/champagne-chartogne-taillet-819 and https://www.catawiki.com/ro/l/103817371-chartogne-taillet-les-barres-ampanie-extra-brut-6-sticle-0-75l and https://www.bibendum.com.au/pages/copy-of-commune-of-buttons (search 10, summary).
6. Les Barres: "a parcel of ungrafted Meunier planted in 1952", the sandy soil credited with keeping phylloxera out; Tatler Asia calls it the first wine Alexandre Chartogne made after returning in 2006. Decanter: "Les Barres is crafted from ungrafted Pinot Meunier planted in 1952."
   Source: https://www.tatlerasia.com/profile/chartogne-taillet-les-barres?listId=17 (search 10, summary) and https://www.decanter.com/wine/producer-profiles/grower-champagne-chartogne-taillet-270567/ (search 31, summary).
7. Sainte Anne: "a fine introduction to both the estate and the village."
   Source: https://www.decanter.com/wine/producer-profiles/grower-champagne-chartogne-taillet-270567/ (search 31, summary).
8. The rosé: Gault&Millau's 2021 listing of Le Rosé gives Pinot Noir and Chardonnay half and half, direct pressed and barrel fermented; Skurnik's sheet gives 60 percent Chardonnay and 40 percent Pinot Noir, "with 8% red wine", 60 percent aged in oak; a third retailer adds Meunier. Earlier research (bottle-facts-cards-a) found the colour comes from a little red wine from Merfy Pinot Noir and left the blend to the sommelier.
   Source: https://fr.gaultmillau.com/en/wineries/chartogne-taillet/le-rose-bsa and https://www.skurnik.com/sku/champagne-rose-chartogne-taillet-2/ and https://us.callmewine.com/champagne-rose-chartogne-taillet-P6749.htm (search 9, summary) and research/bottle-facts-cards-a-2026-10-04.json.

Expert knowledge: Chartogne-Taillet is said shar-TOHN-yuh tie-YAY, as the pack prints it. Blanc de noirs means white wine from black grapes, which includes Meunier, so the list's 'Les Barres' Blanc de Noirs and the ungrafted Meunier of item 6 agree.

Brennan's link: the Rosé Brut, $125, is the sweet spot bottle for the Steak Tartare Cannoli; the list also carries Cuvée Saint Anne ($160) and the two single parcels, Les Barres and Orizeaux ($290 each).

## 4. Larmandier-Bernier, Vertus (Longitude)

1. 1971: "it was not until the marriage of Philippe Larmandier and Elisabeth Bernier in 1971 that Champagne Larmandier-Bernier was established"; the families trace back to the French Revolution; Elisabeth brought her Vertus vineyards to the marriage and they built their cellar in Vertus. Another page says Louis Larmandier made Champagne in the Côte Blanche from 1846.
   Source: https://www.comptoirdesmillesimes.com/blog/en/larmandier-bernier-guide-champagne/ and https://larmandier.fr/maison-larmandier (search 11, summary).
2. Pierre and Sophie Larmandier: "The contemporary story really begins when Pierre Larmandier took the reins at the family estate in 1988"; Sophie, his wife, works with him. Gault&Millau: "since 2017, they have been joined by their sons Arthur, then Georges."
   Source: https://www.comptoirdesmillesimes.com/blog/en/larmandier-bernier-guide-champagne/ (search 11) and https://fr.gaultmillau.com/en/wineries/larmandier-bernier (search 27).
3. Organic to biodynamic: "Pierre and Sophie Larmandier began farming organically back in 1992 and were fully biodynamic by 1999"; the estate's own timeline gives 1992 for the end of herbicides and 1999 for the whole estate in biodynamics; certified organic since 2003; indigenous yeasts came with the change. (One retailer dates the biodynamic switch to 2004.)
   Source: https://larmandier.fr/maison-larmandier and https://www.premiumgrandscrus.com/en/196_larmandier-bernier and https://us.callmewine.com/winery/larmandier-bernier-B835.htm (search 11, summary).
4. Longitude, the name: "'Longitude' originates exclusively from the Chardonnay grapes grown on the Côte des Blancs: Vertus, Oger, Avize, Cramant, which form a line close to the 4th meridian."

## 8. Pierre Péters, Le Mesnil-sur-Oger (Cuvée de Réserve magnum)

1. The family in Le Mesnil: one account traces it to a Luxembourg-born ancestor who married into a Le Mesnil landowning family in 1840 and sold the whole harvest; another says the Péters family has been in the village since 1858.
   Source: https://www.skurnik.com/?p=299540 and https://quillandpad.com/?p=62035 (search 23, summary) and https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/pierre-peters/ (search 26, summary).
2. Camille Péters: his grandson Camille chose to make the estate's first Champagne by 1919 and kept bottling at home after the 1929 crisis (another source: first bottlings in 1914, sold to other houses); Camille bought into Les Chétillons in 1930 ("the family acquired 3 plots here in 1930 and has been making this single-vineyard champagne since 1971").
   Source: https://quillandpad.com/?p=62035 and https://grandcruselections.com/champagne-pierre-peters and https://thefinestbubble.com/champagne-pierre-peters-cuvee-speciale-les-chetillons-blanc-de-blancs-grand-cru-2018-75cl (search 23, summary).

   Source: https://quillandpad.com/?p=62035 and https://www.skurnik.com/?p=299540 (search 23, summary).
4. Rodolphe Péters: "Rodolphe Péters took over the running of the family estate from his father, François in 2008, having been involved in the assemblage since 2000"; he is described as the sixth generation.
   Source: https://grandcruselections.com/champagne-pierre-peters (search 23) and https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/pierre-peters/ (search 26, summary).
5. The perpetual reserve: one source: "He initiated the house's 'perpetual reserve' of reserve wines in the late 1990s"; others: "Rodolphe's 'perpetual reserve' which is based on the 1988, 1990, 1993 and 1996 harvests", and a solera "more than 25 vintages deep, dating back to 1988".    Source: https://grandcruselections.com/champagne-pierre-peters (search 23) and https://shop.klwines.com/products/details/1013558 and https://www.bbr.com/products-10008041786-champagne-pierre-peters-grand-reserve-blanc-de-blancs-grand-cru-brut (search 24 and 26, summary).

   Source: https://shop.klwines.com/products/details/1013558 and https://www.bbr.com/products-10008041786-champagne-pierre-peters-grand-reserve-blanc-de-blancs-grand-cru-brut and https://www.nicks.com.au/products/pierre-peters-cuvee-de-reserve-blanc-de-blancs-champagne and https://www.vinsetmillesimes.com/en/8730-pierre-peters (search 24, summary; the tool did not tie each passage to one of these pages).
