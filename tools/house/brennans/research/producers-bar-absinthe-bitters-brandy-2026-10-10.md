## 1. Jade Nouvelle-Orléans absinthe: Jade Liqueurs, distilled at Combier (Origin Story)

1. Jade absinthes are made at the Combier distillery in Saumur, France, by a scientist and absinthe historian who researched old absinthes and their making methods, aiming to recreate the original spirit free from the myths about its effects.
   Source: https://www.masterofmalt.com/distilleries/jade-absinthe-distillery/ (summary): "Jade absinthes are made at the Combier distillery in Saumur, France. T.A. Breaux, a scientist and absinthe historian, researched old absinthes and their making methods. He aimed to recreate the original spirit, free from myths about its effects and the ban in the early 20th century."
2. Jade Liqueurs was founded in 2000; Nouvelle-Orléans followed in 2004, the first of the series.
   Source: https://www.absinthe.de/en/Jade-Liqueurs and https://www.absinthe.de/en/Absinthe-Jade-Nouvelle-Orleans (summary): "Ted Breaux, a New Orleans-born scientist, founded Jade Liqueurs in 2000, and Nouvelle Orleans was born in 2004, the first in a whole series of excellent products." Also: "It was the first Jade Absinthe that Breaux launched."
3. The founder has personally produced every batch since 2004.
   Source: https://www.absinthe.de/en/Absinthe-Jade-Nouvelle-Orleans (summary): "He has personally produced every batch since 2004."
4. The style is rooted in the absinthes that made the Sazerac and the Absinthe Frappé famous.
   Source: https://www.alandia.de/absinthe-jade-nouvelle-orleans.html (summary): "Its stated style is rooted in the original Absinthes that made the Sazerac cocktail and Absinthe Frappé famous."
5. The recipes are said to be reverse-engineered from pre-ban absinthes.
   Source: https://bourbonblog.com/?p=22079 (summary): "reverse engineered by distiller Ted Breaux using the same botanicals and flavor profiles as pre-ban absinthes of the early 1900's." The later bottlings 1901 and Esprit Edouard are tied more explicitly to particular pre-ban bottles (https://www.absinthe.de/en/Absinthe-Jade-1901, summary).
6. The alembics: copper bain-marie stills built in the 1870s, acquired from one of the original absinthe distilleries in Pontarlier.
   Source: https://www.diffordsguide.com/en-au/beer-wine-spirits/1572/jade-nouvelle-orleans-absinthe-superieure and absinthe.de (summary): "Jade absinthes are made at Distillerie Combier using original 1,150-litre copper bain-marie alembics, built in the 1870s and acquired from one of the original absinthe distilleries in Pontarlier, France." Another listing gives "two 1100-liter copper Absinthe alembics from one of the greatest Absinthe distillers in Pontarlier around the time of the early bans" (masterofmalt.com, summary). The capacity differs between sources, so no capacity is used in app prose.
7. Combier's old Pernod-era stills made it the natural choice for reproducing nineteenth-century absinthe.
   Source: absinthe.de (summary): "Combier's old Pernod distilleries made it the natural choice for reproducing 19th-century absinthe."
8. Colour and profile: the light green comes from a second infusion of plants in the distillate; a peppery spice and a fresh profile; 68 percent alcohol.
   Source: https://www.alandia.de/absinthe-jade-nouvelle-orleans.html and absinthe.de (summary): "its light green color comes from a second infusion of plants in the resulting distillate." "It has a peppery spice and a fresh profile, reflecting New Orleans' cocktail culture." "The strength is listed as 68% ABV."
9. The name nods to the founder's Louisiana roots and New Orleans' absinthe history.
   Source: absinthe.de (summary): "Nouvelle-Orléans absinthe nods to Breaux's Louisiana roots and New Orleans' absinthe history."
10. From the earlier pass (research/components-drinks-2026-10-05.md, search 7): an absinthe supérieure at 68 percent, wormwood and other plants macerated in wine alcohol and distilled in old alembics; a tribute by a native New Orleanian to the city's nineteenth-century absinthe culture.

Ownership: Jade Liqueurs is the founder's own company (fact 2); it is distilled under contract at Combier. Combier's own ownership and founding year were not searched here.

Brennan's link: printed only in the Origin Story (pdf p39), with Dudognon Cognac and barrel-aged Peychaud's. Whether the bar rinses the glass with it is not printed.

Expert knowledge: an absinthe rinse coats the glass and leaves aroma; the Classic's rinse is Herbsaint (bar-master-review-2026-10-04.md, "Herbsaint and absinthe").

## 2. St. George Absinthe Verte: St. George Spirits (Thompson's Dream)

1. Founded in 1982 as an eau de vie distillery.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George_Spirits (summary): "St. George Spirits was founded in 1982 by Jorg Rupf as an eau de vie distillery."
2. In 2004 it moved into a 65,000-square-foot airplane hangar on the former Alameda naval air station.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George_Spirits (summary): "In 2004, St. George Spirits moved into its current location, a 65,000-square-foot (6,000 m2) airplane hangar on the former Alameda naval air station." Also caskcartel.com listings: "moved to its current location at the Alameda Naval Air Station (in a massive airplane hangar!) in Alameda, California, in 2004."
3. The founder handed the master distiller's role to his successor in 2010, after 28 years.
   Source: https://www.masterofmalt.com/distilleries/st-george-spirits/ and caskcartel.com (summary): "Master distiller Rupf handed the role to Lance Winters in 2010, after 28 years in the position."
4. The label was refused several times, the federal label office objecting to the monkey image and the name Verte, before approval in early December 2007; it was the first American-made absinthe on the market in almost a century.
   Source: a 2007 New York Times report, as summarised with https://punchdrink.com/articles/the-rise-of-american-absinthe-st-george-delaware-phoenix/ (summary): "the federal agency that approves alcohol labels rejected the label several times, objecting to the monkey image and the 'Verte' name, before granting approval." "The agency finally granted approval to St. George Absinthe Verte, the first American-made absinthe on the market in almost a century."
5. It went on sale on 21 December 2007, the first legal domestic absinthe in 95 years; the first 1,800 bottles sold out in six hours.
   Source: https://punchdrink.com/articles/the-rise-of-american-absinthe-st-george-delaware-phoenix/ (summary): "Punch places the launch on December 21, 2007, when St. George Spirits ... was releasing America's first legal domestic absinthe in 95 years." "they ended up selling out of their first 1,800 bottles in six hours." Drinks International (https://drinksint.com/news/archivestory.php/aid/194/.html, summary): "the first US company since 1912 to make and sell absinthe in the US"; on sale "during the Christmas holidays in limited markets".
6. The method, from the producer: wormwood, fennel and star anise infused in brandy and distilled; the distillate then infused with mint, tarragon, Genovese basil, lemon balm, hyssop and stinging nettle; 60 percent.
   Source: https://stgeorgespirits.com/spirits/absinthe-verte (summary): "Wormwood, fennel, star anise infused in brandy and then distilled. The resulting distillate is infused with mint, tarragon, Genovese basil, lemon balm, hyssop, and stinging nettle." "ABV: 60%."
7. The second infusion gives the green; copper pot stills.
   Source: https://theliquorbarn.com/collections/absinthe/products/st-george-absinthe-verte-750ml (summary): "After distillation through copper pot stills, the spirit undergoes a secondary botanical infusion," which gives the green colour.
8. A brandy base instead of beet alcohol; wormwood does not cause hallucinations; 120 proof, so dilute with water.
   Source: https://www.saveur.com/article/Wine-and-Drink/One-Good-Bottle (summary): "it owes its unusual mellowness to a base of a brandy (instead of beet alcohol) infused with 11 herbs and spices." "wormwood, an herb all absinthes contain, does not cause hallucinations." "diluting with water, which turns the liquid opaque and releases its aromatic oils, is a good idea."
9. Star anise departs from the green anise of classic European recipes.
   Source: blackwellswines.com listing (summary): "a deliberate departure from the traditional green anise used in classic European recipes."
10. The recipe was developed over eleven years; made by the team behind Hangar One Vodka.
   Source: caskcartel.com listings (summary): "the recipe was developed over an eleven year period." https://www.sonomamag.com/chasing-the-green-fairy/ (2008, summary): "it's distilled by the same folks who make Hangar One Vodka."
11. Wikipedia's snippet dates the release to December 2009; the 2007 reports above are used instead (see Method).

Ownership: not found (search 21).

Brennan's link: printed only in Thompson's Dream (pdf p39), "St. George absinthe rinse", in place of the Classic's Herbsaint.

From dossier-the-bar.md section 6 (high): Lucid, made in France, was first on the American market in 2007; St. George was the first American-made absinthe since the ban. So "first American-made", never "first absinthe".

   Source: https://thirstyinla.com/2010/07/22/have-bitters-will-travel-the-bitter-truth-travelers-set/ (summary): "Its first releases came out in August 2006 (Orange Bitters and Old Time Aromatic Bitters)."

## 4. Angostura aromatic bitters: House of Angostura (Birdcage, Brennan's Champagne Cocktail)

1. Dr Johann Gottlieb Benjamin Siegert, born in 1796, a surgeon in Venezuela; the company says he perfected the Amargo Aromatico formula by 1824 for soldiers' medical use.
   Source: https://angosturabitters.com/our-story/ (summary): "Johann Gottlieb Benjamin Siegert was born in 1796 and became a surgeon in Venezuela." The brand says he perfected the "Amargo Aromatico" formula "by 1824 for soldiers' medical use".
2. He settled in the town of Angostura, now Ciudad Bolívar, around 1820, serving under Simón Bolívar; a German surgeon general in Bolívar's army.
   Source: https://m2magazine.co.nz/angostura-bitter-truth and https://cocktail-society.com/barkeeping/what-are-angostura-bitters/ (summary): "He settled in the town of Angostura, now Ciudad Bolívar, around 1820, where he served under Simón Bolívar." "The recipe was developed as a tonic by Johann Gottlieb Benjamin Siegert, a German surgeon general in Simón Bolívar's army in Venezuela."
3. Wikipedia says he began selling it in 1824 and set up a distillery in 1830, citing an 1876 court case.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angostura_bitters (summary): "he began selling it that year and set up a distillery in 1830, a date it attributes to an 1876 court case."
4. In 1875 the family business moved to Port of Spain, Trinidad, as J.G.B. Siegert & Hijos; Difford's ties the move to unrest in Venezuela and dates Dr Siegert's death to 1870.
   Source: https://angosturabitters.com/our-story/ and https://www.diffordsguide.com/en-au/producer/1188/angostura/history (summary): "the family business relocated that year under the sons' name J.G.B Siegert & Hijos." "Difford's Guide ties the move to unrest in Venezuela." "Difford's says he died in 1870." Bartender.com (summary): the sons moved after Dr Siegert died.
5. The label names gentian as the only botanical; the full recipe is a trade secret, no longer held only by the family.
   Source: https://culinarylore.com/drinks:do-angostura-bitters-contain-angostura and Wikipedia (summary): "the only botanical ingredients listed on the label are gentian and 'natural flavors.'" "The exact herbs and other ingredients used is a trade secret." "it's no longer restricted to members of the Siegert family, but has been passed on to company executives."
6. The oversized label: by company mythology, labels too large were delivered from the printer and used all the same.
   Source: https://www.traveldistilled.com/the-story-of-angostura-bitters/ (summary): "company mythology has it that at one point, labels too large were delivered from the printer, but they used them all the same: the oversized label became part of the identity."
7. The label still shows a medal from the 1873 Vienna World's Fair.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angostura_bitters (summary): "the medal is still depicted on the oversized label, which commemorates an 1873 Vienna World's Fair award." (Search 16 found no second source for the medal.)
8. Rum since 1947 through Trinidad Distillers Limited; listed in 1982 as Angostura Holdings; distilling and bottling in Laventille, Trinidad.
   Source: https://cocktailwonk.com/2020/03/age-angostura-part-one.html and https://bevvy.co/articles/angostura-rums-nothing-bitter-about/18051 (summary): "Angostura has been in the business of rum making through its production company, Trinidad Distillers Limited, since 1947." "The company also became Angostura Holdings Ltd. when it was listed on the Trinidad and Tobago Stock Exchange in 1982." "fermenting, distilling, ageing, blending and bottling alcoholic beverages, mainly rum, in Laventille, Trinidad."
9. CL Financial bought Angostura and Trinidad Distillers in 1997.
   Source: https://www.wipo.int/ipadvantage/en/details.jsp?id=5553 (summary): "CL Financial, one of the largest privately held corporations in the Caribbean, purchased Angostura and Trinidad Distillers in 1997."
10. Present ownership: Rumpro holds about 44.97 percent (a 2023 company statement); Rumpro belongs to the CL Financial group, in compulsory liquidation since a High Court order of September 2017; the government's National Investment Fund Holding Company holds 29.97 percent.
   Source: https://angostura.com/angostura-clarifies-recent-statements-on-its-shareholding/ (summary): "Rumpro directly holds 92,551,212 shares in Angostura which represents a shareholding of approximately 44.97%." https://guardian.co.tt/business/angostura-shareholders-want-focus-on-984m-debt-6.2.2653633.b8daaeea0e (2025, summary): "Rumpro is owned by the CL Financial group, which is in compulsory liquidation, having been ordered to be wound up by the High Court in September 2017." "The National Investment Fund Holding Company is Angostura's second largest shareholder at 29.97 per cent, and it is wholly owned by the government."
11. The bicentennial was celebrated from 2024.
   Source: https://www.brandlandusa.com/2024/01/20/angostura-begins-its-bicentennial-celebration-in-2024/ (title): "Angostura begins its bicentennial celebration in 2024."
12. Angostura Orange Bitters were introduced in 2007 (section 8).

Brennan's link: the Birdcage ("a smoked Old Fashioned", pages/brennansneworleans-luxury-roost-bar-cocktails-2026-10-03.txt) and the Brennan's Champagne Cocktail (Angostura on a demerara sugar cube, the menu paste).

Expert knowledge: the bitters are named for the town, and the company itself says they hold no angostura bark; that second point was not confirmed by a search here, so the app says only that the name is the town's and the recipe is secret. In the Champagne Cocktail the soaked cube keeps the drink fizzing from the bottom (dossier-the-bar.md, the Champagne Cocktail).

## 5. Dudognon Cognac: Maison Dudognon (Origin Story)

1. The family has grown vines at Lignières-Sonneville since 1776; the estate they work today was bought in 1852.
   Source: https://cognac-ton.nl/en/homepage/brands-and-producers/home-page-general/brand-information/pages-c-f/dudognon/ (summary): "Already in 1776 this family started to grow wines in Lignières-Sonneville. The estate were they are now was bought in 1852."
2. The vineyard, Le Davore, on a chalky hillside, tucked away from the village.
   Source: https://caravanwinesandspirits.com.au/pages/cognac and premiumgrandscrus.com (summary): "The winegrowing estate called Le Davore, occupied by this family since 1776, is tucked away from the village," on a chalky hillside.
3. A bouilleur de cru in Grande Champagne.
   Source: cognac-ton.nl (title): "Dudognon, bouilleur de Cru in Lignières-Sonneville (grande champagne)".
4. Paulin Dudognon won a gold medal in 1898, the house's first formal recognition.
   Source: https://caravanwinesandspirits.com.au/products/cer-dudpaulin (summary): "the house was first formally recognised in 1898 when Paulin Dudognon won a Gold Medal."
5. Raymond Dudognon took over from his father Paulin in 1946 and was the first to sell cognac under the family name; another source dates the change to 1970, when the family stopped supplying the big houses.
   Source: cognac-ton.nl (summary): "Raymond Dudognon took over from his father Paulin in 1946. He was the one who started to sell cognacs under his own name." Another listing: "the family stopped supplying the big houses to sell his Cognacs under his own name" in 1970.
6. Raymond died in 2002; his property was divided among his three daughters and he chose one as his successor; she runs the house with her husband and their son.
   Source: cognac-ton.nl and https://www.skurnik.com/producer/maison-dudognon/ (summary): "Raymond died in 2002, and was succeeded by Claudine Dudognon, who is married with Gerald Buraud. Their son Pierre is also involved." "Raymond's property was eventually divided amongst his three daughters, and he chose daughter Claudine as his successor."
7. No sale found: every source still names the family as owners (undated).
   Source: search 14 (summary): "The results don't show any sale of the Dudognon estate. Every source still names the Dudognon-Buraud family as the owners, though none is dated."
8. Water is the only additive; cask oak is air-dried for five years next to the chai.
   Source: https://www.skurnik.com/producer/maison-dudognon/ (summary): "the only additive used in Dudognon Cognac is water." "the oak for their barrels is air-dried for five years next to the chai, and ... water is the only secondary ingredient at Dudognon."
9. Roughly 100 barrels a year on two alembic stills; wood-fired copper stills.
   Source: search 14 (summary): "The results describe wood-fired copper stills"; "the house distill roughly 100 barrels per year on two alembic stills."
10. Bottlings by age, as retailers list them: VSOP 10 Years, Napoleon 15 Years, Vieille Réserve 20 Years, Paulin 60 Years, Réserve des Ancêtres.
   Source: caravanwinesandspirits.com.au product titles ("Dudognon Cognac VSOP 10 Years", "Dudognon Cognac Napoleon 15 Years", "Dudognon Cognac Vieille Reserve 20 Years", "Dudognon Grand Champagne Cognac 60 Years Paulin") and nicks.com.au ("Dudognon Reserve des Ancetres Cognac").
11. From the earlier pass (components-drinks-2026-10-05.md, search 10): a traditional family estate distilling only its own grapes, Ugni Blanc among them; chalk soils; the 10-year Réserve and 20-year Vieille Réserve.

Brennan's link: printed in the Origin Story without a bottling.

Expert knowledge: Grande Champagne, the first cru of Cognac, takes its name from chalky open country; it has nothing to do with the sparkling wine region (also in bar-dudognon-cognac). With water the only addition, the house adds no caramel, sugar or boisé (dossier-the-bar.md section 7 says the same).

## 6. Daron Fine Calvados: Maison Ferrand, not Christian Drouin (To Each His Own)

## Verification 2026-10-10

An adversarial check of every profile, fact, note, line to say and catalogue entry above, against the sources recorded here and against twenty fresh WebSearch calls (standard mode, 10 October 2026). Passages are the search tool's summaries of the pages named, not full reads. What changed in absinthe-bitters-brandy.json is listed under each item; anything not listed was found consistent with its source.

Searches run: (V1) Jade Nouvelle-Orléans release order and year; (V2) St. George Absinthe Verte botanicals; (V3) the St. George label and the TTB; (V4) Jade Liqueurs history and the Pontarlier alembics; (V5) The Bitter Truth Creole Bitters, strength and launch; (V6) the Angostura oversized label and the 1873 Vienna medal; (V7) Daron and Maison Ferrand; (V8) Angostura shareholding after 2025; (V9) the Dudognon family dates; (V10) Dr Siegert's dates and the 1875 move; (V11) Fee Brothers founding and Prohibition; (V12) St. George Spirits founding, hangar and master distiller; (V13) Fee Brothers Black Walnut ingredients; (V14) the St. George release and sell-out; (V15) Angostura Orange Bitters and angostura bark; (V16) the 1,800 bottles; (V17) Raymond Dudognon's birth and Paulin; (V18) Lucid and the 2007 approval; (V19) Dudognon stills and additives; (V20) the Calvados Pays d'Auge rules.

1. Jade (V1, V4). Confirmed: Jade Liqueurs founded in 2000 by the founder (absinthe.de, nicks.com.au); Nouvelle-Orléans the first Jade release, Jade 1901 the second, "reverse-engineered from sealed antique bottles" (https://www.nicks.com.au/products/jade-1901-absinthe-700ml); the founder's late-1990s gas chromatography of sealed bottles of the 1901 Pernod Fils absinthe (same); copper alembics built in the 1870s and acquired from one of the original absinthe distilleries in Pontarlier (https://www.diffordsguide.com/en-au/beer-wine-spirits/1572/jade-nouvelle-orleans-absinthe-superieure); 68 percent. NOT confirmed by a second source: the release year 2004 rests on absinthe.de alone, so the JSON now attributes it ("a specialist retailer dates it to 2004") and the founded field says 2000 only; the catalogue drops the year and calls 1901 and Esprit Edouard "other bottlings", not later ones, since only the order of the first two was found. Reattributed: "the brand says" became "reviewers" (bourbonblog.com) and "its sellers" (alandia.de), because neither passage is the maker's own page. The note on legality now says "in the United States", since the 2007 return was American.

2. St. George (V2, V3, V12, V14, V16, V18). Confirmed: founded in 1982 as an eau de vie distillery; the 65,000-square-foot hangar at the former Alameda naval air station from 2004; the founder handed the master distiller's role on in 2010 after 28 years (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George_Spirits, https://www.masterofmalt.com/distilleries/st-george-spirits/). The label went through repeated TTB refusals, reported as seven, over the monkey, read as implying mind-altering qualities, and over the name Verte when a sample tested amber; label approval 12/2007 (https://www.printmag.com/id-mag/wormwood/, https://bevlaw.com/?p=9966). On sale 21 December 2007, the first legal domestic absinthe in 95 years, the first 1,800 bottles gone in about six hours, by the master distiller's own recollection (https://punchdrink.com/articles/the-rise-of-american-absinthe-st-george-delaware-phoenix/). Lucid, made at Combier in France, was approved for American distribution on 5 March 2007, the first absinthe cleared since the 1912 ban (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucid_Absinthe, https://www.diffordsguide.com/g/1102/absinthe/us-legalisation-of-absinthe); the approval rested on thujone compliance, not a repeal. Botanicals: Difford's and Punch also list meadowsweet (and Punch Artemisia pontica and a Chardonnay brandy base) where Skurnik does not, so the existing component's meadowsweet stands. Changed: the history no longer says the authorities "confirmed absinthe could be sold again" but that it returned once regulators accepted a properly made one met the thujone limit; the catalogue's "federal ban was lifted" became "absinthe returned to American shelves in 2007". fixExisting now asks bar-st-george-absinthe to say American-made, so no guest hears it as the first absinthe sold in America.

3. The Bitter Truth (V5). Confirmed: founded in Munich in 2006 by two bartenders; Creole Bitters 39 percent on the maker's page (https://the-bitter-truth.com/bitters/creole-bitters/); a retailer reports Creole and Celery Bitters won Best New Product at Tales of the Cocktail in 2010 (nicks.com.au, retailer claim only). No launch year found, so "joined the range around 2010" became "new by 2010". Refuted as stated: "called Creole because Peychaud's is a Sazerac Company brand name" gave a reason the maker has not given; the fact now says only that Peychaud's is a Sazerac Company brand and The Bitter Truth sells its New Orleans-style bitters as Creole Bitters.

4. Angostura (V6, V8, V10, V15). Confirmed: Dr Siegert 1796 to 1870 (the Siegert Square listing, https://buzz.tt/venue/siegert-square-1059); the 1875 move to Port of Spain by his sons as Dr J.G.B. REFUTED: the oversized label as "a printer's mistake, by the company's telling". REFUTED as stated: "Paulin's son Raymond" and "took over from his father Paulin".
