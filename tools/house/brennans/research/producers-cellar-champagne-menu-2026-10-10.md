## 1. Piper-Heidsieck, Reims (the house Champagne, the Cuvée Brut magnum)

1. The founding: "Piper-Heidsieck is a Champagne house founded by Florens-Louis Heidsieck in 1785 in Reims, France." One account says he presented his champagne to Queen Marie Antoinette in 1788; other sources place the presentation around the founding year.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper-Heidsieck and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florens-Louis_Heidsieck and https://glassofbubbly.com/the-history-of-piper-heidsieck/ (summary).
2. The nephew and Piper: "His nephew Christian Heidsieck joined the company in 1800." "Henri-Guillaume Piper joined in 1815", another source says he "was involved in the family business that year". "Christian Heidsieck took over the control of Heidsieck & Co after Florens-Louis' death in 1828, while Piper handled sales."
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper-Heidsieck and https://www.vinovest.co/blog/piper-heidsieck and https://glengarrywines.co.nz/piperheidsieck (summary).
3. How the names were joined: "Christian died, and she married his partner in wine, Henri-Guillaume Piper, and the Piper Heidsieck Champagne house was born." One source dates Christian's death to 1835; another says the house takes its full name from the 19th century, when Christian Heidsieck's widow married Henri-Guillaume Piper.
   Source: https://glassofbubbly.com/?p=15849 and https://sommeliers-international.com/en/node/11659 (summary).
4. The Fabergé bottle: "In 1885, to celebrate the 100th anniversary of this landmark event, the House unveiled a limited series of bottles branded the Cuvée du Centenaire. For this special occasion, Pierre-Karl Fabergé ... was commissioned to adorn the bottle." "This project never came to fruition owing to the fragile nature of the glass bottles and the tools of the time"; the house produced labels reproducing the design.
   Source: https://glassofbubbly.com/the-history-of-piper-heidsieck/ and https://thefinestbubble.com/champagne/brands/champagne-piper-heidsieck (summary).
5. The Cuvée Brut: the producer's sheet gives "Pinot Noir can make up to 50-55% of the blend, Pinot Meunier comprising 30-35%, Chardonnay 15-20%, and reserve wines around 20-25%"; "composed of more than 100 crus from around the region and is aged for a minimum of 24 months on lees"; "the precious reserve wines create a consistent flavor profile year after year"; the house calls it "a classic, well-structured, and fruit forward champagne".
   Source: https://grape-to-glass.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Piper-Heidsieck-Cuvee-Brut-NV-2024.pdf and https://www.wine.com/product/piper-heidsieck-cuvee-brut/20128 and https://mtouton.com/products/france/champagne/13977-NV/piper-heidsieck-cuvee-brut-sleeve/print (summary).
6. Essentiel Extra Brut: a Pinot Noir-led three-grape blend (one review 50 Pinot Noir, 30 Meunier, 20 Chardonnay); reserve wines between about 15 and 35 percent, Liberty Wines "a minimum of at least 30% reserve wines"; dosage "generally 4 to 6 g/L"; "the normal Brut NV has between 8-10g/L dosage whereas 'Essentiel' has 5-6g/L"; one review: "exactly the same base wine as the Piper-Heidsieck Brut NV, but with a further 12-18 months of lees ageing". The drinks business reported in 2019 that Piper-Heidsieck created a bespoke Champagne for the Wright Brothers restaurants (55 Pinot Noir, 30 Meunier, 15 Chardonnay).
   Source: https://libertywines.co.uk/our-portfolio/PH102-piper-heidsieck-essentiel-extra-brut and https://grape-to-glass.com/index.php/piper-heidsieck-essentiel-extra-brut-nv/ and https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2019/07/piper-heidsieck-creates-bespoke-champagne-for-wright-brothers/ (summary).
7. The house Champagne in public: the June 2024 NOWFE kick-off, "Hosted by Brennan's and Folio Fine Wines", Tuesday 4 June 2024 in the courtyard and Roost Bar, poured three Champagnes: "Charles Heidsieck Brut Réserve NV, Piper-Heidsieck Rosé Sauvage NV, and Brennan's private-label Piper-Heidsieck Essential", with a sabering demonstration. A Brennan's menu pairs the Eggs Hussarde with "Brennan's Essential Champagne by Piper-Heidsieck". Folio Fine Wine Partners added Piper-Heidsieck and Rare Champagne to its portfolio in November 2020.
   Source: https://nowfe.com/nowfe-kick-off-celebration and https://www.brennansneworleans.com/events/nowfekickoff and https://www.myneworleans.com/nowfe-kicks-off-celebration-at-brennans-june-4/ and https://wineindustryadvisor.com/?p=47061 (summary).
8. Ownership, recorded and not used as a present fact: "Piper-Heidsieck was acquired on July 8, 2011, by the French luxury group EPI ... controlled by the Descours family"; before that Rémy Cointreau owned it from 1988.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piper-Heidsieck (summary).
9. The three Heidsieck houses: the Codex library's Charles Heidsieck entry (p-charles-heidsieck) carries the trap "Three separate houses carry the name: Charles Heidsieck, Piper-Heidsieck and Heidsieck & Co Monopole"; the Vranken-Pommery search (section 3, item 4) confirms Heidsieck & Co Monopole is its own house, founded in 1785 by Florens-Louis Heidsieck by its own account.
10. Pronunciation (carried from master-review-somm-2026-10-04.md): "the house's own people say PEE-pair HIDE-seek; PIE-per HIDE-seek is what most English speakers say and is accepted" (Wine Spectator, "How do you pronounce Piper-Heidsieck?"). The pack respells it PIE-per HIDE-sek.

Brennan's link: the house Champagne, Brennan's Essential by Piper-Heidsieck Extra Brut NV, by the glass ($28 on Binwise, $30 on the menus) and by the bottle ($140 on Binwise); the Cuvée Brut in magnum on Birthday Bubbles and in 3 L on the list; Piper-Heidsieck Brut 2014 and 'Reserve' Brut 2018 on the bottle list.

## 2. Rare Champagne, Reims (the Coravin glass and the 2013 bottle)

1. The first vintage: "Their prestige cuvée, Rare, was first produced in 1976 when an unprecedented heat wave struck the region." It is imagined as a "jewelry cuvée" for exceptional occasions, made only in very special years.
   Source: https://glassofbubbly.com/?p=15849 and https://www.idealwine.com/uk/domain/8827-Rare-Champagne.jsp (summary).
2. The vintages: a retailer lists "1976, 1979, 1985, 1988, 1990, 1998, 1999, 2002, 2006, 2007, 2008, 2012, 2013 & 2015"; another says "Rare Champagne has only released 16 prestige cuvées across 14 vintages across Brut and Rosé over 48 years". Vintages are declared only in years "where Nature unleashes its forces"; a sommelier piece says the team seeks "challenging and highly characterful years with seasons marked by extremes".
   Source: https://thefinestbubble.com/champagne/brands/champagne-champagne+rare and https://thewineindependent.com/articles/twi-drinks-3/ and https://cuws.org.uk/?p=939 (summary).
3. Its own house: "Rare became its own brand in September 2018, previously labelled, marketed and sold under the Piper-Heidsieck name, and it is now managed separately. It becomes the third Champagne house in the EPI group, alongside Piper-Heidsieck and Charles Heidsieck."
   Source: https://thefinestbubble.com/champagne/brands/champagne-champagne+rare (summary).
4. The dress: "In the 1980s, the Parisian workshop Arthus-Bertrand refines the design into the now instantly recognisable golden, leafy 'crown' which embraces the lower part of the bottle." One source says that in 1985 Van Cleef & Arpels made a single jewelled bottle in gold, diamonds and lapis lazuli for the Rare 1976 (not used: one source only).
   Source: https://thefinestbubble.com/champagne-rare-brut-millesime-2012-75cl and https://glassofbubbly.com/the-history-of-piper-heidsieck/ (summary).
5. Rare Millésime 2013: "70 percent Chardonnay and 30 percent Pinot Noir from across 11 crus, including seven grand cru and three premier cru sites", mainly in the Montagne de Reims; the Chardonnay from Villers-Marmery, Vaudemanges, Verzy, Ambonnay, Avize, Chouilly, Oger and Montgueux, the Pinot Noir from Verzy, Verzenay, Ambonnay, Bouzy and Tauxières (Foliowine technical sheet).
   Source: https://www.foliowine.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/rare-millesime-2013-ts.pdf and https://www.foliowine.com/wine/rare-millesime-brut/ and https://vinepair.com/review/rare-champagne-millesime/ (summary).
6. Lees and dosage of the 2013: "aged on the lees for a minimum of eight years before disgorgement in December 2022, followed by an additional year under cork before release"; another gives "Disgorgement Date: Spring 2022 Dosage: 9g/L". The house's general minimum for Rare is given by one source as nine years on lees. Dosage 9 g/L is Brut (the search summary's "extra brut" is wrong: Extra Brut is 0 to 6 g/L).
   Source: https://www.foliowine.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/rare-millesime-2013-ts.pdf and https://hedonism.co.uk/products/rare-champagne-2013 and https://shop.klwines.com/products/details/1713996 (summary).

Expert knowledge: the French say Rare as RAHR (the pack's own say line); an English speaker may simply say the word.

Brennan's link: Rare Brut 2012 by the glass under the Coravin Sparkling stopper, $75 (the current glass page prints a 2013 Rare Millésime at the same price); Rare 2013 by the bottle, $650; Rare 1998 in 1.5 L, Rare 2008 in 6 L, Rare Rosé 2012 in 6 L and 2014 in 1.5 L on the list.

## 3. Charles Lafitte (the tasting pour)

1. The name and the brand: "The « Belle Idée » was born in Cognac in 1848 and was created by Jean-Baptiste Lafitte." "One had to wait until 1983 for the first bottles covered with black silver paper to appear on the European market." Another source says that in 1983 the house began making its own Champagne rather than only supplying cognac spirits used in Champagne dosage.
   Source: https://champagner.com/en/charles-lafitte-champagne/ and https://champagner.com/en/charles-lafitte-champagner (summary).
2. The group's own date: the Vranken-Pommery milestones page gives 1983 for Charles Lafitte "and says the house was created after the group bought a cognac house"; a retailer: "Champagne Charles Lafitte was added to the portfolio, followed by the acquisition of Château des Castaignes in Champagne." "I found no source for an 1834 date."
   Source: https://vrankenpommery.com/our-milestones and https://champagner.com/en/vranken-champagne/ (summary).
3. The 1834 and George Goulet: "In 1834, François André Goulet diversified his activities, starting to produce Champagne wines in barrels and bottles." The "1834" name in the Charles Lafitte range honours that Goulet lineage. "Charles Lafitte acquired the George Goulet champagne house" in 2003, a date that marked the brand's twentieth anniversary. Charles Lafitte's own site says it was "founded in 1848 in Épernay" (read as marketing copy: it conflicts with the Cognac origin).
   Source: https://www.enviedechamp.com/en/47-lafitte-champagne and https://champagner.com/en/charles-lafitte-champagne-2/ (summary).
4. The group: "Creation of the Champagne House named after his founder", dated 1976 on the group's site (Hachette: "Le Champagne Vranken a été fondé en 1976"); Heidsieck & Co Monopole, "Founded in 1785 by Florens-Louis Heidsieck ... owned by Vranken-Pommery Monopole since 1996"; "The acquisition of the renowned champagne house Pommery in 2002 brought Vranken-Pommery Monopole to second place among the world's largest champagne producers." An October 2025 press release: LANSON-BCC agreed to buy 100 percent of Heidsieck & Co Monopole, the transfer effective 1 January 2026, so the group named in research/components-wines-2026-10-05.md (search 14) no longer holds it.
