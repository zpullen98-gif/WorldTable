## 1. The Maillard reaction (the searing and browning technique card)

- Louis Camille Maillard, a French physician and chemist, born in 1878 at Pont-à-Mousson and died in Paris on 12 May 1936, described in 1912 that mixtures of amino acids and sugars turn intensely brown on heating; he was trying to reproduce protein synthesis and thought the finding mattered for medicine. The reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars that makes the browned compounds (melanoidins) of cooked food now carries his name. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Camille_Maillard ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maillard_reaction ; https://www.wgbh.org/news/2015-11-23/100-years-ago-maillard-taught-us-why-our-food-tastes-better-cooked
- The mechanism was set out in 1953 by John E. Hodge of the United States Department of Agriculture. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maillard_reaction
- Respelling used on the card: Maillard, my-YAR (French final consonant silent, stress on the last syllable, in the style of the pack's burr BLAHN).

## 2. Baking soda and baking powder (the quick bread technique card)

- Baking soda is sodium bicarbonate, a base that gives off carbon dioxide when it meets an acid; baking powder is baking soda mixed with a dry acid salt (classically cream of tartar) and cornstarch. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baking_powder ; https://jwu.pressbooks.pub/bakingscienceleaveners/?p=22
- The first commercial baking powders were English: Borwick's, launched in 1842, and the pharmacist Alfred Bird's single-acting powder of 1843 (one source gives 1846, so the card says the 1840s and names no maker). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baking_powder ; https://blog.thenibble.com/2018/05/14/food-101-baking-powder-history-where-would-we-be-without-it/
- Eben Norton Horsford of Harvard received an American patent in 1856 for a baking powder using monocalcium phosphate in place of costly cream of tartar; it was soon sold as Rumford Baking Powder, and in the 1860s Horsford developed the first double-acting powder. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baking_powder ; https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/great-uprising-how-powder-revolutionized-baking-180963772/ ; https://historycambridge.org/innovation/Baking%20Powder.html
- Buttermilk's acid reacting with sodium bicarbonate is the lift of a soda bread or a biscuit (section 4 below).

## 3. Corn (the corn ingredient card)

- Maize was domesticated in the Balsas River valley of southern Mexico from Balsas teosinte (Zea mays ssp. parviglumis) about 9,000 years ago; stone milling tools from the region carry traces of maize, and early farmers seem to have chosen the plant for its storable starch. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5150420 ; https://elifesciences.org/articles/5861 ; https://evolution.earthathome.org/grasses/andropogoneae/maize-domestication/
- Grits are almost always made from dent corn, a field corn with a softer, floury starch, not from sweet corn. Hominy is corn treated with an alkali (lye or calcium hydroxide), which softens the kernel and removes the hull; dried and ground it becomes hominy grits, while plain grits are simply ground dried corn. Stone-ground grits keep the germ; instant grits are precooked and dried. https://www.tastingtable.com/2070773/types-of-grits-southern-cooking-explained ; https://www.chowhound.com/1674623/difference-between-hominy-vs-corn ; https://culinarylore.com/specialty-foods:difference-between-grits-and-polenta/
- Brennan's: the guide prints Cheddar Grits (pdf p30), the fried pimento cheese grit cake on the New Orleans Shrimp & Grits (pdf p21), the cornbread and chive waffle on the Duck Confit Waffle (pdf p20) and roasted sweet corn on the Crab & Corn Omelette (pdf p22). The Succotash prints glazed summer vegetables and no corn; the guide's own line says the traditional dish is corn and lima beans (pdf p31), so whether tonight's carries corn is a lineup question.

## 4. Buttermilk (the buttermilk ingredient card)

- Traditional buttermilk is the thin liquid left after churning cream into butter, soured by the bacteria already in the cream; because pasteurisation kills those bacteria, nearly all commercial buttermilk today is cultured, that is, pasteurised low-fat milk soured on purpose with lactic acid bacteria such as Lactococcus lactis, which turn lactose into lactic acid and thicken the milk. Cultured buttermilk is thicker and more acid than the churned kind. https://www.cooksillustrated.com/how_tos/6549-conventional-vs-real-buttermilk ; https://www.finedininglovers.co.uk/explore/articles/what-is-buttermilk ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buttermilk
- Its acid reacts with sodium bicarbonate to release carbon dioxide, the lift of soda bread and biscuits; in a biscuit test tasters could not reliably tell cultured buttermilk from the churned kind. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buttermilk ; https://www.cooksillustrated.com/how_tos/6549-conventional-vs-real-buttermilk
- Brennan's: the guide prints buttermilk twice, the Buttermilk Biscuit (pdf p30) and the buttermilk ice cream on the New Orleans Bread Pudding (pdf p35). Which buttermilk the kitchen buys is not printed.

## 5. Summer vegetables in Louisiana (the summer vegetables ingredient card)

- An LSU AgCenter horticulturist: okra seeded directly into the garden in midsummer comes into production in late August or early September and produces until the weather cools in late October or early November; eggplant and summer squash are picked immature and lose quality quickly if left on the plant. https://www.lsuagcenter.com/profiles/rbogren/articles/page1499436125196 ; https://apps.lsuagcenter.com/news_archive/2014/June/get-it-growing/Harvest-homegrown-vegetables-properly-.htm

## 6. Pasta shells (the pasta shells ingredient and the cooking pasta technique cards)

- Conchiglie takes its name from the Italian for seashell, conchiglia (a Greek root shared with the English conch). The shape is traced to southern Italy, the Puglia region in particular, in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries; its ridged exterior and shell shape need shaped machine dies, and it is traditionally made from durum wheat semolina. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conchiglie ; https://www.delallo.com/blog/what-is-conchiglie
- Respelling used on the card: conchiglie, kon-KEEL-yeh.

## 8. Glazing (the glazing technique card)

- To glaze (glacer) vegetables the French way: cook them in a pan with water partway up, butter, a little sugar and salt until the water is gone and the sugar left behind forms a syrup that coats them; stopped there it is a white glaze (glacer à blanc), cooked on until the sugar colours it is a blond or brown one (glacer à brun). Carrots Vichy are the classic example, and Escoffier gave the method to small turnips. https://thehungryhutch.com/?p=160 ; https://www.pardonyourfrench.com/vichy-carrots-french-style-glazed-carrots/ ; https://www.recipetineats.com/french-glazed-carrots-carottes-vichy-recipe/
- Brennan's: glazed summer vegetables on the Succotash (pdf p31), roasted and glazed summer squash on the Roasted Chateaubriand (pdf p24), Madeira-glazed broccolini on the Creole Hanger Steak (pdf p28) and cane syrup-glazed peaches on the Roasted Rohan Duck Breast (pdf p6). What each glaze is made with is not printed.

## 9. Rendering fat (the rendering technique card)

- Rendering is melting animal fat out of its surrounding tissue with low, steady heat, leaving clean liquid fat and crisp solids; bacon cooked low and slow is the everyday case. Rendered pork fat is lard, beef fat tallow, poultry fat schmaltz. Dry rendering heats the fat alone; wet rendering adds water to hold the temperature down. https://www.chowhound.com/2204279/what-happens-when-you-render-fat/ ; https://scienceinsights.org/what-does-it-mean-to-render-fat-in-cooking/ ; https://www.tastingtable.com/1274482/low-slow-frying-method-all-bacon-fat
- Brennan's: the kitchen dossier's cook's notes on the thick-cut bacon (a low, slow render so the fat turns glassy before the edges burn) and the Rohan duck breast (start skin down in a cold dry pan, pour off the fat as it renders) are in research/dossier-the-kitchen.md, sections 7 and 3; the Duck Confit Waffle's leg is cooked slowly in rendered fat (the confit card). Whether the kitchen keeps its rendered fat for other cooking is a chef's question.

## 10. Blanching and shocking (the blanching technique card)

- Blanching is a brief boil followed by an immediate plunge into iced water, the chilling step called shocking or refreshing. Heat deactivates the enzymes that break down chlorophyll, so a blanched green holds its colour; without the ice bath the vegetable cooks on in its own heat. https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/food-network-essentials/what-is-blanching ; https://reluctantgourmet.com/?p=315 ; https://www.thekitchn.com/how-to-blanch-vegetables-home-108570
- Brennan's: the kitchen dossier's cook's note on the Eggs Sardou (creamed spinach: blanch, shock, squeeze hard, chop, bind with reduced cream or a light béchamel) is in research/dossier-the-kitchen.md, section 6; the Brabant potatoes component in components/dishes.json reads the cubes as blanched or par-fried before frying. Which vegetables are blanched ahead on a given night is the chef's answer.
