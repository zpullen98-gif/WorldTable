## 1. What the PDF actually says about the floor

The floor dossier reported that the guide carries no steps of service and no org chart. That is true of an end-to-end sequence, but the guide does carry a scattered set of floor instructions worth collecting, because a new server will be measured against them.

Roles the PDF names or implies:
- (pdf p49) "ask at training who performs it (server or captain)". This is the only mention of a captain. It establishes that the guide's author expected a captain tier to exist but could not confirm who flames.
- (pdf p24, p48) "I can bring our sommelier for a red Burgundy from upstairs" and "I'd love to bring our sommelier over to pick one with you". The guide treats a sommelier as a floor resource the server can summon. No name, no shift pattern.
- (pdf p39) "ask the bar about the mix". The bar is a confirm point for the Bloody Mary mix; no bar lead is named.
- (pdf p5, p25, p26, p32, p39, p40, p34, p35) "lineup" is referenced repeatedly as the pre-shift meeting where the server confirms: filet temperature practice, whether the Chateaubriand is carved tableside, whether the papillote is opened at the table, what goes in Maggie's Mushrooms, the Sazerac build, the Blender's Reserve story, Cherries Jubilee minimum. (expert) In New Orleans houses "lineup" is the pre-service meeting run by a manager or chef; the PDF does not say who runs it.
- (pdf p47, p50) "I'll let the kitchen know", "always confirm with the kitchen". The kitchen is the allergy confirm point and the server flags the ticket.
- (pdf p49) "Chef Kris Padalino (executive chef since June 2025)". The only named person in the guide.
- No mention at all of: general manager, maître d', host, front waiter, back waiter, food runner, busser, bartender by name, pastry chef, wine director, dining room manager.

Steps of service the PDF does carry (per dish, not as a sequence):
- (pdf p38) Brandy Milk Punch: "Offer it at the greeting; it's the first sale of the morning." Script ends "Shall I start you with one?" This places the eye-opener offer with the server at the greeting.
- (pdf p5) Take filet temperature when you take the tasting order, not when the course fires.
- (pdf p34) Bananas Foster: "Seed it with the entrée order." Minimum two per order.
- (pdf p42) Charles Lafitte Brut on the tastings: keep the open bottle stoppered and iced between courses; pour just before the Hussarde or the oysters land.
- (pdf p6, p7, p25) Tasting pours: pour just before the course lands; open the Paul Hobbs 30 to 60 minutes ahead; Louis Jadot 20 to 30 minutes ahead.
- (pdf p12) Crab claws: bring the shell plate and napkins at the same time as the pour, "per house standard".

- (pdf p33, p34) Bananas Foster: pour the dessert wine before the flambé starts so no glass is near the flame.

### 3.1 Bananas Foster flambé
- Performer: unknown, ask the house. (pdf p49) "ask at training who performs it (server or captain)". (expert, general) In the grand-house model the captain or a senior server trained on the cart flames; some houses let any server who has passed a cart check do it. Not a Brennan's fact.
- Day and time: (pdf p33) breakfast and lunch $14, dinner $14, and the fifth course of the breakfast tasting. (pdf p49) 2026 is the dish's 75th year; "the restaurant calls it the most-ordered item on the menu".
- Equipment: (pdf p34) the service note refers to a cart, a pan, a burner, a small vessel for measuring rum, a lid or cover, and a fire extinguisher. (expert, general) The classic guéridon set-up is a long-handled flambé pan over a portable burner on a wheeled cart; the fuel type (butane cartridge or gel fuel) at Brennan's is not published in anything read.
- Recipe statement: (pdf p33) menu line: bananas, butter, brown sugar, cinnamon, rum, housemade vanilla ice cream, flambéed tableside, minimum two per order. (pdf p34) "The printed menu says rum; Brennan's own published recipe also uses banana liqueur; follow the house procedure you're trained on."
- Safety rules exactly as the PDF prints them (pdf p34), punctuation adjusted only to remove dashes: (1) Set the cart where nothing hangs overhead and away from drapes, décor and air vents; clear menus, napkins and paper from it. (2) Guests seated and back from the cart; no one leaning in; watch children, loose sleeves and long hair. (3) Measure the rum into a small vessel first; never pour from the bottle near a flame. (4) Pull the pan off the flame to add rum, then tilt the far edge toward the flame to ignite, away from you and the guests. (5) Never add alcohol to a lit pan. (6) Keep a lid or cover within reach to smother, and know where the extinguisher is. (7) Let the flame burn out before plating; turn the burner off before you leave the cart. Also: "Flambéing doesn't remove all the alcohol; tell guests who avoid it." (pdf p47) scripted answer: "Most of it burns off, but not all of it."
- Note on provenance: (pdf p34) the list is prefaced "follow the house procedure you're trained on", so these are the guide author's rules, not a published Brennan's procedure. The house procedure remains an open question.
- Wine on the cart: (pdf p34) pour the Auslese before the flambé starts so no glass is set down near the flame.
- Minimum: (pdf p34, p47) two per order; the scripted reason is "made in one pan at the table for at least two people".

### 3.6 Coravin pours
- Rare Brut 2012: (pdf p41) $75 a glass, "Poured with a Coravin so a single glass is possible"; serve 46 to 50 F in a tulip or white-wine glass; "Coravin-preserved: reseal right after each pour and present the bottle to the guest". (pdf p41) the restaurant's own drinks page lists a 2013 Rare Millésime at the same price; check which vintage is open.
- Leflaive Mâcon-Verzé 2022: (pdf p43) $40 a glass; "Poured by Coravin: needle through the cork, pour, and return the bottle to the cooler. Present the label." (pdf p23) "present the bottle and pour through the needle; no cork is pulled".
- Device: unknown, ask the house. (expert, high, about Coravin products in general, not about Brennan's) Coravin's needle systems (the Timeless line, Model Two, Model Six, Model Eleven and similar) pass a hollow needle through a natural cork and replace the wine with argon; Coravin itself states they are not for sparkling wine, because the bottle pressure would push wine out through the needle. Coravin sells a separate Sparkling system: the bottle is opened in the normal way, a clamp stopper is fitted, and CO2 from a charger re-pressurises the headspace; nothing passes through the cork. Coravin's Pivot is a third family: a stopper plus argon for still wines over a few weeks, also not for sparkling.
- (expert inference, medium) Read together, the PDF's two descriptions are consistent with two different devices: a needle system for the still Leflaive ("needle through the cork", p43) and a sparkling stopper system for the Rare Brut ("reseal right after each pour", p41, with no mention of a needle). Which models the house owns, how many, and who may operate them (server, bartender, sommelier) are open questions.
- Performer: unknown. (expert, general) Houses commonly restrict Coravin to the bar or the sommelier because a bad needle angle or a crumbly cork ruins a $400 bottle; the PDF's wording ("pour through the needle", p23) reads as if the server pours, but that is an inference from a training document, not a published policy.
