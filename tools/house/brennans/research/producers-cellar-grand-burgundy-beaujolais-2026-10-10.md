## 2. Domaine de la Romanée-Conti, Vosne-Romanée (Échezeaux 2015, La Tâche 2012, Romanée-Conti 2014)

1. The record: r "Vosne-Romanée", sub "Côte de Nuits", founded "1869". Holdings: "Two monopoles, Romanée-Conti (1.81 ha) and La Tâche (6.06 ha), plus parcels in Richebourg, Romanée-Saint-Vivant, Grands Echezeaux, Echezeaux, Corton, Corton-Charlemagne and Le Montrachet; run as a societe civile since 1942 by the de Villaine and Leroy/Roch families."

   Source: p-dom-romanee-conti (style, t).
3. Monopoles and shared crus: "Romanée-Conti and La Tâche are monopoles; Richebourg and Romanée-Saint-Vivant are not." The advanced bank: "Give the two monopole grands crus owned entirely by Domaine de la Romanée-Conti." Answer "Romanée-Conti and La Tâche"; "Six others are shared; these two are DRC's alone."
   Source: p-dom-romanee-conti (traps); sommelierscodex/js/data-advanced.js, a-bg3f0jo1.
4. The name: "Bought by the Prince de Conti in 1760, which is when it took his name." The wine: "The single wine of the vineyard, a few hundred cases a year."
   Source: sommelierscodex/js/data-producers.js, w-romanee-conti (holdings, wines); p-dom-romanee-conti (wines: "The 1.81 ha monopole, bought by the Prince de Conti in 1760").

   Source: w-romanee-conti (holdings, traps).

Traps: "La Tâche is larger than Romanée-Conti, not smaller"; "Les Gaudichots still exists as a separate premier cru alongside the grand cru".
   Source: sommelierscodex/js/data-producers.js, w-la-tache (holdings, style, traps); p-dom-romanee-conti (wines: "6.06 ha monopole, enlarged by the absorption of Les Gaudichots in 1933"). ## 3. Domaine Armand Rousseau, Gevrey-Chambertin (Clos Saint-Jacques Premier Cru 2019)

1. The record: r "Gevrey-Chambertin", sub "Côte de Nuits", founded "early 20th century". Holdings: "The reference holding in Gevrey: parcels of Chambertin and Chambertin-Clos de Bèze, the monopole Ruchottes-Chambertin Clos des Ruchottes, Mazis-Chambertin, Charmes-Chambertin and the largest share of the premier cru Clos Saint-Jacques."
   Source: sommelierscodex/js/data-producers.js, p-dom-armand-rousseau (founded, holdings).
2. The style: "Destemmed, new oak reserved for the grands crus; deep, savoury Gevrey built on tannin and length rather than sweetness." Tasting tag: "Gevrey's spine: black cherry, licorice, iron and a long savoury tail".
   Source: the same record (style, t).
3. Why it matters: "One of the estates that pioneered domaine bottling in the 1930s, and the yardstick for Chambertin against Clos de Bèze." The introductory bank: "Parcels in both Chambertin and Chambertin-Clos de Beze put one Gevrey-Chambertin family at the head of nearly every list for the village" (answer: Domaine Armand Rousseau); "The domaine was bottling and selling under its own name in the 1930s, when most Burgundy still left the cellar in cask for a merchant to raise and name."
   Source: p-dom-armand-rousseau (why); sommelierscodex/js/data-intro.js, i-jjpzo43d.
4. The wines: "Chambertin-Clos de Bèze", "The estate's most complete wine, from the older of the two crus"; "Chambertin", "Denser and more tannic than the Beze, and slower to open"; "Gevrey-Chambertin Clos Saint-Jacques", "Premier cru routinely argued deserving of grand cru"; "Ruchottes-Chambertin Clos des Ruchottes", "A walled monopole inside the grand cru".
