## 1. Taittinger: Champagne Taittinger, Reims (Flamingo)

1. 1734: Jacques Fourneaux started a Champagne wine trading business and worked with Benedictine abbeys that owned top vineyards; in the 1820s he joined with Antoine Forest and the firm traded as Forest-Fourneaux.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taittinger and https://www.premiersgrandscrus.com/en/champagne/3882-taittinger-comtes-de-champagne-2012-champagne.html (summary): "Jacques Fourneaux started a Champagne wine trading business and worked with Benedictine abbeys that owned top vineyards." "Fourneaux later joined with Antoine Forest in the 1820s, and the combined firm traded as Forest-Fourneaux."
2. 1932: Pierre Taittinger bought the Château de la Marquetterie from Forest-Fourneaux; one source gives 1931 as the year he bought the house itself; the 1932 date appears in most sources.
   Source: https://www.premiumgrandscrus.com/en/champagne/4448-taittinger-collection-vasarely-1978.html and https://www.caymancompass.com/?p=372831 (summary): "Pierre Taittinger bought the Château de la Marquetterie from Forest-Fourneaux." "One source gives 1931 as the year he bought the house itself (Cayman Compass)."
3. The cellars: from 1945 to 1960 the house moved its cellars into the Saint-Nicaise abbey, built in the 13th century over Gallo-Roman chalk pits, dated to the 4th century by most sources.
   Source: https://www.premiumgrandscrus.com/en/29_taittinger and https://thechefsforum.co.uk/?p=45448 (summary): "From 1945 to 1960, François Taittinger ran the house and moved its cellars into the Saint-Nicaise abbey, built in the 13th century over Gallo-Roman chalk pits." "The chalk pits are dated to the 4th century by most sources, though one gives the 2nd century."
4. The 2005 sale and the 2006 buyback: "Champagne Taittinger was sold in July 2005 by the Taittinger family, along with its subsidiary, Société du Louvre, to the U.S. private investment firm Starwood Capital Group." The family "re-acquired the house of Champagne Taittinger in 2006 after securing financial support from the Crédit Agricole bank and also the backing of trade organisations." Wine Spectator: the deal was put together with Crédit Agricole du Nord-Est and the d'Aulan family, who once owned Champagne Piper-Heidsieck. One iDealwine article dates the Starwood purchase to 2006.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taittinger and https://www.winespectator.com/articles/keeping-it-in-the-family-14922 and https://www.idealwine.net/zoom-sur-la-maison-taittinger-toujours-en-marche/ (summary, quoted as given).
5. The family today: "Today Taittinger remains family-owned and is headed by" a member of the family "who became president on 1 January 2020"; another guide says the family "later clawed back control from the Bank, leaving them with a minority share."
   Source: https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/taittinger/ and https://www.cellartours.com/france/french-wineries/taittinger (summary).
6. Vineyards: "today they cultivate 288 hectares spread over 34 crus among the best appellations"; another source gives about 290 hectares.
   Source: https://www.idealwine.info/champagne-taittinger-always-moving-forwards/ (summary).
7. Brut La Française: one retailer lists "40% Chardonnay (unusually high for non-vintage Champagne), 35% Pinot Noir, and 25% Meunier, sourced from over 35 villages"; a trade piece calls it "the signature cuvée, assembled from more than 25 crus and incorporates a high percentage of Chardonnay, for a non-vintage champagne."
   Source: https://nyc.flatiron-wines.com/collections/pinot-noir/products/taittinger-taittinger-brut-la-francaise-nv-750ml and https://issuu.com/racssalcedo/docs/explore_champagne_sparkling_lowres/s/16753547 (summary).
8. La Française and the Brut Réserve: "Taittinger's 'la Française' bottling of non-vintage Brut is the same cépages as the wine above, but spend an additional year aging on its fine lees prior to disgorgement." Difford's: the Brut Réserve is aged on the lees for a minimum of 3 years before release, and lists it as 40% Chardonnay, 30% Pinot Noir and 30% Meunier.
   Source: https://www.jjbuckley.com/wine/taittinger-brut-la-francaise/9998-6684-750/ and https://www.diffordsguide.com/beer-wine-spirits/601/taittinger-brut-reserve-nv-champagne (summary).
9. Public link to Brennan's: a 2017 French Quarter guide listed Taittinger La Française Brut among the wines featured during the Bubbles at Brennan's hours, with Delamotte Brut, Henriot Souverain Brut, Krug Grande Cuvee Brut and Billecart Brut Rosé. The Roost Bar page says the bar serves fizzy Champagne cocktails at any hour of the day.
   Source: https://frenchquarterly.com/node/402 and https://brennansneworleans.com/roost-bar (summary).

Expert knowledge: Taittinger is said roughly teh-tan-ZHAY.

Brennan's link: the Flamingo prints Taittinger La Française Champagne, poured only in the Roost Bar and Lounge; Birthday Bubbles lists Taittinger 'La Francaise' Brut NV as a 3 L double magnum, and the Bubbles at Brennan's rosé bottles include Taittinger 'Prestige' Rosé Brut NV. The Codex section on the cellar will go deeper; this section is kept bar-facing.

## 2. Chandon: Domaine Chandon, Yountville, and Chandon Argentina, Mendoza (Chandon Garden Spritz)

1. Chandon Argentina: founded in 1959 by Moët & Chandon at Agrelo, Luján de Cuyo, Mendoza; "the first subsidiary of the company outside France was opened, named Bodegas Chandon Argentina"; Mhdkk adds it was the first French capital winery in South America.
   Source: https://argentina.guides.winefolly.com/wineries/chandon/ and https://mercado.com.ar/revista/numero-1109/festejo-con-burbujas/ and https://mhdkk.com/en/brands/chandon/history (summary).
2. Chandon California: "CHANDON California was founded in 1973, betting on the emergence of a sparkling-wine market"; "It was initially named M & H Vineyards to honor the parent company Moët-Hennessy." The LVMH page dates the idea to 1968, when the house's chairman, at a distributors' conference in California, "found himself in the wrong room".
   Source: https://www.napawineproject.com/chandon-california and https://www-v2.lvmh.com/en/our-maisons/wines-spirits/chandon/chandon-california (summary).
3. First French sparkling wine house in California: Wine Spectator says that in 1973 it started producing méthode Champenoise sparkling wines, "becoming the first French sparkling wine house in California"; other sources say the first French owned sparkling wine producer in the U.S.
   Source: https://www.winespectator.com/articles/domaine-chandon-founder-john-wright-dies-at-78-46677 (summary).
4. John Wright: he oversaw the "Wine America" report that caught Moët Hennessy's attention, found the terroirs and "oversaw all aspects of the winery's design, from buying grapes to building a facility, with Moët & Chandon providing technical guidance. For the first few vintages the company worked out of the Trefethen winery before moving into a beautiful facility in Yountville in 1977." Wine Spectator's headline: "Domaine Chandon Founder John Wright Dies at 78".
   Source: same, and https://napawineproject.com/domaine-chandon (summary).
5. Ownership: "Today, CHANDON California is owned by Louis Vuitton Moët Hennessy (LVMH)."
   Source: https://www.napawineproject.com/chandon-california (summary).
6. Chandon Brut: non-vintage, made in the traditional method; retailers list 70% Chardonnay and 30% Pinot Noir; a Somm Journal review gives 73% Chardonnay, 26% Pinot Noir and 1% Pinot Meunier; the producer's notes say the Chardonnay comes from Chandon's vineyards in southern Napa, Mt. Veeder and Carneros; "a dry, classic California sparkling wine with notes of green apple, pear, and citrus, plus some brioche."
   Source: https://m.wine.com/product/chandon-brut/273 and https://bottlebarn.com/products/chandon-brut (summary).
7. The Garden Spritz is Argentine: developed by Chandon's winemaking team in Argentina; "invented in Argentina and now marketed all over the world"; Chandon's own page: "The Garden Spritz is crafted in the original home of Chandon: Mendoza, Argentina"; the liqueur "was inspired by the well-known Argentinian penchant for the moreish bitter flavors of yerba maté and Fernet"; "Chandon Garden Spritz takes Chandon's sparkling wine and adds a house-made 'Garden liqueur,' made by Chandon's winemaking team in Argentina." Chandon's pages say it was developed over four years of orange studies.
   Source: https://www.chandon.com/en-us/savour-tips-pairings-section-1/GardenSpritz.html and https://www.chandon.com/en-US/gardenspritz-blog.html and https://m.wine.com/product/chandon-garden-spritz/770712 and https://www.leaderreunion.fr/en/chandon-garden-spritz-le-gout-de-largentine/ (summary).
8. The United States launch: a May 2021 press release datelined Napa Valley, California: "CHANDON is excited to announce its first foray into a new category of luxurious 'ready-to-drink' sparkling aperitifs with the debut of the CHANDON Garden Spritz."
   Source: https://wineindustryadvisor.com/2021/05/03/chandon-debuts-the-first-sip-of-summer/ and https://bevinfogroup.com/2021/05/04/chandon-garden-spritz/ (summary).
9. From the sibling file of the same day (producers-bar-liqueurs-aperitifs-2026-10-10.md, section 11): the Garden liqueur is made from locally sourced, handpicked Valencia oranges, fresh herbs and spices; the fruit comes from a family farm in Entre Rios, north-east Argentina; the base wine is made by the Charmat method; the suggested serve is over ice with a sliver of orange and a sprig of rosemary.
10. The earlier pass (bar-master-review-2026-10-04.md) noted that the Chandon Brut sold in the United States is the California wine, so the menu's "Chandon Brut sparkling wine" and the ready-made Garden Spritz from Mendoza are not necessarily the same wine.

Brennan's link: the Chandon Garden Spritz prints "bitter orange liqueur" and "Chandon Brut sparkling wine" at Bubbles at Brennan's. The same group's Moët & Chandon 'Imperial' Rosé Brut NV is among the Bubbles rosé bottles, and Moët & Chandon 'Nectar Imperial' Rosé Demi Sec NV is on the Champagne list. No public link between Chandon and Brennan's was found (search 23). The existing component bar-spritz-story places the drink in the aperitivo family made famous by Aperol, so the guest who orders an Aperol spritz is the guest to offer it to.

## 3. Elio Perrone, Castiglione Tinella (OBX)

1. The family and the place: the Perrones have made Moscato in Castiglione Tinella "since the end of the nineteenth century"; another source credits "four generations of viticulturalists" with building the estate.
   Source: https://www.callmewine.com/en/perrone-elio-B230.htm and https://weibel.inteco.ch/en/winzer/elio-perrone (summary).
2. The present generation: most sources say the son took over from his father, whose name the estate carries, in 1989 ("who took over the reins of the winery in 1989"); one Winebid listing says the father retired in the late 1990s.
   Source: https://shopify.callmewine.com/en/winery/perrone-elio-B230.htm and https://Winebid.Com/BuyWine/Producer/Elio-Perrone/5815 (summary).
3. Moscato first: "Moscato forms the major part of the estate's production." The vineyards "are distributed over two different areas, with the Isola d'Asti holdings where Barbera plays the leading role"; Winebid says the estate also has vineyards in Isola d'Asti "where the Perrones grow Barbera, Chardonnay and Brachetto". A search result title reads "2007 Elio Perrone Moscato d'asti Magnum".
   Source: https://www.callmewine.com/en/perrone-elio-B230.htm and https://Winebid.Com/BuyWine/Producer/Elio-Perrone/5815 and https://www.grandcruwijnen.nl/en/2007-elio-perrone-moscato-d-asti-magnum (summary).
4. Reputation: Winebid reports that the Perrone winery has "been a Moscato standard bearer" for some years, according to Gambero Rosso.
   Source: https://Winebid.Com/BuyWine/Producer/Elio-Perrone/5815 (summary).
5. Bigaro, the wine: "a 50/50 blend of Brachetto and Moscato, a softly sweet, gently effervescent salmon-colored sparkler", sold as a Piedmont DOC wine from the Asti area; made "by crushing the two grapes together and filtering to stop fermentation, which leaves a light fizz."
   Source: https://www.rarewineco.com/growers/elio-perrone-990/bigaro-24017/ and https://shop.klwines.com/products/details/1851680 and https://nyc.flatiron-wines.com/products/elio-perrone-bigaro-rose-2025-750ml (summary).
6. The alcohol: the Rare Wine Co. and its technical sheet say the wine is "fermented to ~5% alcohol"; retailers say the 2025 is fermented to just 5%; a Creamwine listing for the 2024 gives 7% ABV.
   Source: https://rosenthalsale.rarewineco.com/media/184187/italy-perrone-tech-sheet-0224.pdf and https://creamwine.com/product.php?id=21460 (summary).
7. Taste and service: tasters describe "red berries and rose from the Brachetto, with peach and orange blossom from the Moscato"; retailers recommend serving it well chilled, as an aperitif or with fruit desserts; Creamwine spells it Bigarò and calls it one of Piedmont's best aperitivo wines.
   Source: https://www.wine.com/product/elio-perrone-bigaro-2023/1735613 and https://creamwine.com/product.php?id=21460 (summary).
8. The name: "None of the results say whether 'Bigarò' comes from a word meaning 'mottled' or 'spotted,' so that etymology remains unconfirmed" (search 10). No result mentioned the importer the earlier pass named.

Brennan's link: the OBX prints Gentilly Gin, peach, lemon and Elio Perrone Bigaro at Bubbles at Brennan's. No public link found. The existing component bar-sparkling-wine contrasts the traditional method with tank-fermented sparkling wines such as Prosecco; the Bigaro is neither Champagne nor Prosecco.

## 4. Wölffer Estate Vineyard, Sagaponack, Long Island (Personality)

1. The founder: Christian Wölffer, a German-born investor, "began buying Sagaponack farmland in 1978 for a horse farm, and only later became involved in wine"; "The Sagaponack property, which was the original vineyard acquired in 1988, was previously a potato field."
   Source: https://27east.com/food-drinks/wolffer-estate-vineyard-celebrates-30-years-1386190 and https://peconiclandtrust.org/our-work/projects/wolffer-estate-vineyards (summary).
2. The name: sources date the founding to 1987 or 1988; "When Christian Wölffer founded the winery, it was originally named Sagpond Vineyards. The property was renamed Wölffer Estate Vineyard in 1997 after the opening of a brand new, 12,000-square-foot facility."
   Source: https://www.27east.com/?p=1386190 and https://issuu.com/thenorthshoreian/docs/northshoreian_march_2009/10 (summary).
3. The winemaker: "Christian Wölffer enticed" the winemaker "to come to Long Island from his native Germany in 1992"; he is now a partner.
   Source: https://vinepair.com/articles/wolffer-estate-wines-2018/ (summary).
4. His death and the owners today: he died on New Year's Eve 2008 in Brazil, at about 70, at a beach in Paraty, about 150 miles west of Rio de Janeiro (a boating or a swimming accident by different accounts); "Today, the estate is owned and operated by Mr. Wölffer's children" and the winemaker partner.
   Source: https://www.winespectator.com/articles/wolffer-estate-vineyards-owner-dies-4514 and https://vinepair.com/articles/wolffer-estate-wines-2018/ (summary).
5. Scale: "the 175-acre Sagaponack estate grows 55 acres of vines and also is home to Wölffer Stables"; the Peconic Land Trust gives 115.6 acres under its conservation easement.
   Source: https://vinepair.com/articles/wolffer-estate-wines-2018/ and https://peconiclandtrust.org/our-work/projects/wolffer-estate-vineyards (summary).
6. Rosé: VinePair's article is titled "9 Things You Should Know About Wölffer Estate Rosé".
   Source: https://vinepair.com/articles/wolffer-estate-wines-2018/ (title).
7. Non-alcoholic history: the estate "says it began making non-alcoholic options in 1996, with Verjus first, and it now also sells Petite Rosé alongside Spring in a Bottle."
   Source: https://wolffer.com/news/wolffer-meet-demands-non-alcoholic-market/ (summary).
8. Spring in a Bottle: "Wölffer Estate Vineyard has launched Spring in a Bottle, a non-alcoholic, low-calorie sparkling rosé" (August and September 2021 coverage); "first introduced as a rosé in 2021 after extensive research by" a co-owner; the launch price was $18. "The blend of pinot noir, merlot and saint laurent varietals is sourced from a certified organic vineyard in Rheinhessen, Germany. 11. How dealcoholised wine is made: a review covers "reverse osmosis, osmotic distillation, vacuum distillation, spinning cone columns, pervaporation, and diafiltration". Spinning cone column: "a vertical column with alternating rotating and stationary metal cones"; "The first pass strips off the most volatile aromas, which are later blended back. The second pass, at a slightly higher temperature, removes nearly all the alcohol." Vacuum distillation: "Operating temperatures of 40°C or below allow fast, near-complete alcohol removal with low thermal damage." Reverse osmosis: "Wine is pushed under high pressure through membranes", which separate ethanol and water from the larger compounds; the ethanol is removed and the rest recombined.
    Source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12004437/ and https://www.vineyardmagazine.co.uk/?p=12278 and https://www.oiv.int/node/3970 and https://oeno-one.eu/article/view/8488 (summary).

Expert knowledge: alcohol carries body and warmth in wine, so a dealcoholised wine tastes lighter than the wine it was made from, which is why a mixed drink often adds sugar and fruit, as Personality does with tart cherry and brown sugar syrup.
