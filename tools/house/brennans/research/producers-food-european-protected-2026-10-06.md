## 1. Grana Padano (Creole Caesar, Shells & Cheese)

1. The Consorzio dates the cheese to 1135 at the Abbey of Chiaravalle, a few kilometres south of Milan, where the monks called it caseus vetus, old cheese.
   Source: https://www.granapadano.it/en-en/the-art-of-production/history-and-heritage/ (summary): "Grana Padano cheese originated in 1135 in the Abbey of Chiaravalle in the Italian Po Plain, a few kilometers south of Milan. The monks called it caseus vetus (old cheese)."
2. Bernard of Clairvaux chose to reclaim the marshland south of Milan and found Chiaravalle Abbey there; the Cistercian monks drained and farmed the land.
   Source: https://www.granapadano.it/en-en/the-art-of-production/history-and-heritage/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiaravalle_Abbey (summary): "Bernard of Clairvaux decided to reclaim the marshy area south of Milan and found a new abbey there, which became Chiaravalle Abbey, where the history of Grana Padano begins in 1135."
3. The popular name won out over caseus vetus: grana, for its grainy, crumbly texture, and Padano, for the Po Valley.
   Source: https://turismo.mantova.it/en_GB/cosa-mangiare/112/grana-padano-dpo (summary): "Over time the popular name prevailed, which referred to the grainy and crumbly texture of the cheese: hence the name 'grana padano' ('grainy cheese from the Po Valley')."
4. The Consorzio di Tutela was founded on 18 June 1954, uniting producers, refiners and merchants.
   Source: https://www.efanews.eu/item/41663-consorzio-grana-padano-celebrates-70-years.html and granapadano.it (summary): "On June 18, 1954, the Consorzio (consortium) for the protection of Grana Padano was established, uniting all producers, refiners, and merchants of this cheese type."
5. The European Union recognised it as a DOP (Protected Designation of Origin) in 1996.
   Source: https://www.granapadano.it/en-en/the-art-of-production/history-and-heritage/ (summary): "In 1996, Grana Padano received recognition of Protected Designation of Origin (DOP) from the European Union."
6. Aged at least nine months; at nine months every wheel is tested with a hammer, a needle and a probe, and fire-branded if it passes. Some wheels age beyond 24 months.
   Source: https://www.granapadano.it/ press kit and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grana_Padano (summary): "On the ninth month of ageing, all Grana Padano wheels are examined with the traditional inspection tools: a hammer, a needle, and a probe. If the wheels pass all the tests, they will be fire-branded." Also gardasee.de (summary): "aging for a minimum of 9 months, with some cheese wheels maturing for more than 24 months."
7. A wheel weighs 24 to 40 kg.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grana_Padano (summary): "It weighs 24 to 40 kg (53 to 88 lbs) per wheel."
8. It is the world's best-selling PDO cheese, with over 5.2 million wheels made in 2022, about 44 per cent exported.
   Source: https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/grana-padano-turnover-37-billion-revenue-more-abroad-than-in-italy-now-also-touches-volumes-AFM7SwcD and https://www.esmmagazine.com/uk/grana-padano-sees-production-growth-despite-pandemic-130235 (summary): "the world's best-selling PDO cheese with over 5.2 million wheels (around 38kg each) produced in 2022 with over 2.3 million exported (around 44%)."
9. How it differs from Parmigiano Reggiano: a larger area (farms in 33 provinces across Lombardy, Veneto, Piedmont, Emilia-Romagna and Trentino Alto Adige) against Parma, Reggio Emilia, Modena and parts of Mantua and Bologna.
   Source: https://www.parmigianoreggiano.com/product-guide-parmigiano-reggiano-grana-padano and https://www.granapadano.it/en-en/?p=86432 (summary): "Grana Padano can be produced from farms located in 33 provinces between Lombardy, Veneto, Piedmont, Emilia-Romagna and Trentino Alto Adige."
10. Grana Padano allows silage in the cows' feed and lysozyme, a protein from hen's egg white, as a preservative; Parmigiano Reggiano allows neither. Grana Padano is inspected at nine months, Parmigiano Reggiano at twelve.
   Source: https://shop.silvanoromaniparma.com/blog/Parmigiano-Reggiano-Grana-Padano-differences and https://saucydressings.com/blog/difference-between-parmigiano-reggiano-and-grana-padano/ (summary): "Parmigiano-Reggiano Consortium requires a nutrition of cows only consisting of fresh fodder and hay, while Grana Padano Consortium allows also silage." "Lysozyme, a protein extracted from the albumen of hen eggs, is used as fermentation inhibitor." "In the case of Parmigiano Reggiano DOP, the quality inspection is carried out at the end of the twelfth month of ageing."
11. Grana Padano dairies may make other cheeses and work twice a day; Parmigiano Reggiano dairies make only that cheese, once a day.
   Source: same pages (summary): "Grana Padano cheese can be produced in dairies manufacturing other kinds of Italian cheeses, and these dairies carry out two manufacturing operations per day."

Brennan's link: the menu prints Grana Padano on the Creole Caesar and the Shells & Cheese. No maker is named.

Note for the kitchen (from fact 10): lysozyme comes from egg; any egg allergy question about a dish with Grana Padano goes to the kitchen, never answered at the table.

Expert knowledge: pronounced GRAH-nah pah-DAH-noh (already in dossier-the-kitchen.md).

## 2. "Parmesan" (Eggs Sardou, Parmesan creamed spinach)

1. In the European Union, a 2008 ruling of the European Court of Justice held that only Parmigiano Reggiano may be sold as "parmesan".
   Source: https://www.thetakeout.com/1779626/parmesan-vs-parmigiano-reggiano-cheese/ and https://www.foodrepublic.com/1500507/difference-between-parmesan-parmigiano-reggiano (summary): "In 2008, the European Court of Justice made a decisive ruling that only cheeses bearing the PDO 'Parmigiano Reggiano' can be sold under the denomination 'parmesan'."
2. In the United States "Parmesan" is a generic name: a hard, granular, grating cheese from anywhere may use it.
   Source: same (summary): "In America, parmesan is not regulated... According to the FDA, as long as a cheese has an easy-to-grate granular texture with a hard and brittle rind, it doesn't matter where it comes from: It can be called parmesan in the United States."
3. The full name Parmigiano Reggiano is protected in the United States too.
   Source: same (summary): "in the U.S., the name Parmigiano Reggiano is protected: only the real cheese product that passed DOP inspection can be labeled as such."
4. Parmigiano Reggiano is made only in Parma, Reggio Emilia, Modena and parts of Mantua and Bologna, inspected at twelve months, with no preservatives.
   Source: section 1, facts 9 and 10.

## 3. Mimolette (Shells & Cheese fondue)

1. Traditionally made around Lille, said to have been created at the request of Louis XIV under Jean-Baptiste Colbert's mercantilist policies, to replace the popular Dutch Edam.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimolette (snippet): "Mimolette is a cheese traditionally produced around the city of Lille, France... originally made by the request of Louis XIV, who, in the context of Jean-Baptiste Colbert's mercantilistic policies, was looking for a native French product to replace the then very popular Edam."
2. To set it apart from Edam it was first coloured with carrot juice and later with annatto, which gives the orange.
   Source: same: "To make it distinct from Edam, it was first colored using carrot juice and later seasoned with annatto to give it a distinct orange color."
3. The origins are placed in the seventeenth century. Another telling: Colbert's embargo on foreign goods left the people of Flanders without their Dutch-style cheese, so they made it themselves.
   Source: https://www.davidlebovitz.com/making-mimolette (summary): "Mimolette traces its origins to the seventeenth century, when Colbert, the French contrôleur general, placed an embargo on foreign goods coming into France. The people of Flanders, deprived of a style of cheese for which they had acquired quite a fondness, responded by making this Dutch-type cheese for themselves."
4. Cheese mites, which the French call little affineurs, work the rind, eating into the crust to give its grey, lunar surface and helping it ripen.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimolette: "The grey-colored rind of aged Mimolette occurs from cheese mites that are added to the surface of the cheese, which serve to enhance its flavor." And https://culturecheesemag.com/cheese-iq/mimolette/ (summary): "These 'little affineurs', as the French call them, create tiny holes essential to the cheese's ripening... the cheese mites 'attack' the cheese, eating into the crust, which gives Mimolette a lunar-like surface."
5. In 2013 the US FDA detained about a ton of Mimolette over mite levels above six per cubic inch; the restriction was lifted in 2014.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimolette and https://www.dairyreporter.com/Article/2013/08/06/Mimolette-cheese-mite-be-restricted-by-FDA/ (summary): "In 2013, the Food and Drug Administration detained about a ton of the cheese... the cheese was above the standard of six mites per cubic inch. The restriction was lifted in 2014."
6. Also called Boule de Lille; the Normandy cooperative Isigny Sainte-Mère has made it since 1951, recruiting master cheesemakers from the north, and sells ages up to 24 months: young, semi-young, mature and extra-mature.
   Source: https://www.isigny-ste-mere.com/en/?p=5733 (summary): "Mimolette, also known as 'Boule de Lille', arrived at Isigny in 1951... the Cooperative recruited Master Cheesemakers from the North of France." "Up to 24 months aging is available at Isigny Ste-Mère."

Brennan's link: the menu prints "Mimolette Fondue" on the Shells & Cheese. No maker or age is named; Isigny Sainte-Mère is named here only as a known maker, never as Brennan's supplier.

Expert knowledge: pronounced mee-mo-LET.

## 4. Marcona almonds (Crab & Corn Omelette, Gulf Fish en Papillote)

1. A Spanish variety native to the province of Alicante, grown also in southern Catalonia, Castellón and Valencia, and called the queen of almonds.
   Source: https://pablogarrigos.com/en/almendra-marcona-la-joya-de-la-corona-de-los-turrones-de-jijona-y-alicante/ (summary): "Native to the province of Alicante, Spain, the Marcona almond tree is celebrated globally for producing the 'Queen of almonds'." And https://www.treesofantiquity.com/products/marcona-almond-tree-antiquity (summary): "In Spain, the variety is found in Southern Catalonia, Castellón, Valencia and Northern Alicante."
2. Shorter, wider and rounder than standard almonds, with almost no bitterness.
   Source: https://www.chowhound.com/food-news/243319/what-is-the-difference-between-almonds-and-marcona-almonds/ (summary): "The Marcona is shorter, wider, and rounder than standard commercial almonds... the Marcona has virtually no bitterness."
3. It has been used for marzipan and turrón since the 14th century; Jijona turrón grinds it to a paste, Alicante turrón uses it whole and toasted.
   Source: https://pablogarrigos.com/en/almendra-marcona-la-joya-de-la-corona-de-los-turrones-de-jijona-y-alicante/ (summary): "It has been used since the 14th century for elaborating marzipans, turrones of the highest quality." "in Jijona it is ground to a fine paste, while Alicante nougat uses whole and toasted marcona almonds."
4. Usually served blanched, roasted or fried, with salt and olive oil.
   Source: https://www.chowhound.com/food-news/243319/ (summary): "often served blanched, roasted, dusted with sea salt, and tossed with extra virgin olive oil." Also components-dishes-2026-10-05.md search 54 (despanabrandfoods.com): "usually blanched, fried in oil and salted."

Brennan's link: the menu prints Marcona on both dishes. No grower or packer is named.

The growing area named in fact 1 runs along Spain's Mediterranean coast; Spaniards call it the queen of almonds (fact 1).

Not used in app prose: one source derives the name from an Arabic word meaning round; a single retail page, so left out.

## 5. Castelvetrano olives (Gulf Fish en Papillote)

1. The variety is Nocellara del Belice, from the Valle del Belice in south-western Sicily; one way of preparing it is named for the comune of Castelvetrano, and those olives are sold as Castelvetrano olives in the United States.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocellara_del_Belice (snippet): "Nocellara del Belice is an olive cultivar from the Valle del Belice area of south-western Sicily... As a table olive it may be treated by various methods, one of which is named for the comune of Castelvetrano in the Valle del Belice; these may be marketed as Castelvetrano olives in the United States."
2. Awarded a Protected Designation of Origin in 1998; it holds two, Nocellara del Belice DOP table olives and Valle del Belìce DOP olive oil (at least 70 per cent Nocellara).
   Source: same: "In 1998 Nocellara del Belice was awarded the Protected Designation of Origin." "'Valle del Belìce' extra-virgin olive oil, which is pressed from a minimum of 70% Nocellara del Belice olives."
3. The Castelvetrano method debitters the fruit completely, then keeps it in brine; the Sevillian method debitters partly and ferments.
   Source: https://grasasyaceites.revistas.csic.es/index.php/grasasyaceites/article/view/1350 (summary): "The 'Castelvetrano' (sweet) method involves the complete debittering of fruit, which is then preserved in a saline solution. The olives treated with the 'Sevillian' (semi-sweet) system undergo partial debittering, with subsequent fermentation."
4. Picked young and washed in lye, unfermented, which removes bitterness and keeps the bright green colour and buttery taste.
   Source: https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/5826-castelvetrano-the-green-olive-you-should-eat-cooks-country and https://www.murrayscheese.com/castelvetrano-olives (summary): "harvested young and not cured. Instead, they are washed in a lye and water solution for a few weeks to eliminate their bitter taste." "cured without fermentation, a key step that locks in its bright, emerald-green color."

Brennan's link: the menu prints "Castelvetrano olives". Whether they are DOP Nocellara del Belice or the variety from elsewhere, and whether pitted, is a kitchen question.

Expert knowledge: pronounced kah-stel-veh-TRAH-noh. A Sicilian olive, as fact 1 places it.

## 6. Luxardo (Cherries Jubilee, the Luxardo sauce)

1. Founded in 1821 by Girolamo Luxardo in Zara, then in the Kingdom of Dalmatia under the Habsburgs; he had moved there in 1817 as consular representative of the Kingdom of Sardinia.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girolamo_Luxardo (snippet): "The Luxardo firm was founded in 1821 by Girolamo Luxardo in the city of Zara, at the time in the Kingdom of Dalmatia of the Habsburg Empire. Luxardo had moved there with his family in 1817, as the consular representative of the Kingdom of Sardinia."
2. His wife, Maria Canevari, made rosolio maraschino at home; the distillery was founded to make it, and within eight years the Emperor recognised it as superior.
   Source: same: "His wife Maria Canevari produced liqueurs at home, specializing in 'rosolio maraschino'... Within eight years of existence, the Luxardo Maraschino was recognized a superior product by the Emperor."
3. Michelangelo Luxardo, third generation, built a new distillery in 1913.
   Source: same: "In 1913 a new distillery was built by Michelangelo Luxardo, who was of the third generation."
4. The first Luxardo maraschino cherries, candied in marasca syrup, were made at Zara around 1905.
   Source: https://cluboenologique.com/story/luxardo-maraschino-cherries-behind-the-cocktail-garnish/ (summary): "The first Luxardo Maraschino Cherries were produced at the Luxardo plant in Zara around 1905, likely to be the first of their kind in the world." And retailer copy (wine.com, summary): "cherries candied with Marasca syrup, a fruit cultivated and processed by Luxardo for distilling its renowned Maraschino, and for preparing, by infusion, the Cherry liqueur 'Sangue Morlacco'."
5. Allied bombing of Zara from November 1943 to October 1944 destroyed 80 per cent of the city, and the factory with it.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Zadar_in_World_War_II (summary): "lasted from November 1943 to October 1944. Over the course of the bombing, 80% of the city's buildings were destroyed." And https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maraschino (summary): "The factory was destroyed in the 1943 bombings."
6. Pietro Luxardo and his brother Nicolò, with Nicolò's wife, were killed by Tito's partisans; Giorgio Luxardo was the only one of the fourth generation to survive.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girolamo_Luxardo and https://www.ilnordest.it/societa/fratelli-nicolo-pietro-luxardo-fucilati-titini-verita-ozna-zara-austt80q (summary): "Pietro Luxardo and his brother Nicolò with his wife were among inhabitants of the town who were killed by Tito's communist partisans." "Only one member of the fourth generation, Giorgio Luxardo, survived and fled to Italy."
7. Giorgio, with Nicolò III of the fifth generation, re-founded the firm briefly in Venice, then at Torreglia near Padua, where the first marasca orchard was planted in 1947; it now has 20,000 trees and the firm is still family owned.
   Source: https://www.luxardo.it/?p=388 and https://www.diffordsguide.com/producer/1181/girolamo-luxardo/strory (summary): "Giorgio, the only surviving fourth generation brother, with the help of Nicolo III (the young fifth generation) temporarily re-founded the business in Venice before Giorgio found the perfect location in Torreglia, Padova. The Euganean Hills provide an ideal climate for growing Marasca cherries, so it is here in 1947, they planted the first orchard. Today that orchard boasts 20,000 cherry trees and is where the still family-owned and run business still continues to operate."
8. Giorgio is said to have crossed the Adriatic with a single cherry sapling.
   Source: https://vinepair.com/articles/ntk-luxardo/ (summary): "Giorgio managed to escape the war with just one cherry sapling when he crossed the Adriatic Sea to northeast Italy's Veneto region." (A story told by the brand and the trade press; tell it as a story.)
9. The firm marked 200 years in 2021.
   Source: https://imbibemagazine.com/luxardo-maraschino-liqueur/ (title): "Luxardo's Legacy: 200 Years of Making Iconic Maraschino Liqueur"; https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/luxardo-festeggia-200-anni-e-rilancia-ampliando-distilleria-e-nuovo-museo-AD664ZIB (title, summary): Luxardo celebrates 200 years, expanding the distillery and opening a new museum.

## 7. 'Nduja (Louisiana BBQ Lobster)

1. A spicy, spreadable pork sausage from Spilinga, in the province of Vibo Valentia, Calabria.
   Source: https://www.tastingtable.com/1138765/the-history-of-nduja-sausage-and-what-makes-it-a-unique-ingredient/ (summary): "'Nduja sausage hails from the Calabrian town of Spilinga in the district of Vibo Valentia."
2. Made from fatty trimmings, fatback, belly and shoulder, minced fine with dried Calabrian chilli at around a third of the weight, salted, cased, lightly smoked and aged for months.
   Source: https://www.calabriatheotheritaly.com/what-is-nduja/ (summary): "made from fatty trimmings of the pig, fatback, belly and shoulder, minced fine with a very large proportion of dried Calabrian red chilli, around a third of the weight, plus salt, packed into natural casing... lightly smoked over local wood, and aged for several months."
3. Born as a frugal way to use the fatty, less prized parts of the pig left to the poor.
   Source: https://www.lovefood.com/news/66997/what-is-nduja (summary): "'Nduja was born as a frugal way to use the fatty, less prized parts of the winter-slaughtered pig that the poor were left with after the better cuts went to the landowners."
4. Its origin is argued: one theory says the Aragonese brought it in the 16th century; another derives it from the French andouille, introduced under Joachim Murat's rule of Naples in the early 19th century.
   Source: https://www.tastingtable.com/1138765/ (summary): "one theory is that it is of Spanish origin, a sausage brought to Calabria in the 16th century by the occupying Aragons, while others say it derives from the spicy French andouille sausage introduced during Joachim Murat's brief rule of the Kingdom of Naples at the beginning of the 19th century."
5. Spilinga holds a 'nduja festival each 8 August, since 1975 (search summary; page not individually identified, so not used in app prose).

Brennan's link: the tasting menu prints "'nduja, White Bean Stew". No maker is named.

The andouille link is worth a New Orleans server's attention: the same French sausage name sits behind Louisiana andouille. Told as a theory only.

## 8. Burrata (Creole Tomato Tostada)

1. A fresh cheese of the Murgia in Puglia, made from cow's milk, rennet and cream.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrata (snippet): "Burrata is a dairy product of Murgia, in Puglia in southern Italy, produced from cow's milk, rennet, and cream."
2. Its birth is placed at Andria: some date it to about 1900 at the Bianchino brothers' farm (Lorenzo and Vincenzo); more recent records credit Lorenzo Bianchino of the Piana Padura farm in 1956.
   Source: same: "may have origins dating back to about 1900, produced at the Bianchino brothers' (Lorenzo and Vincenzo) farm in the town of Andria. However, more recent records have shown that Lorenzo Bianchino, of the Piana Padura farm, first developed the product in 1956."
3. The story: during a heavy February snowfall he could not reach market, so he wrapped curd, scraps of mozzarella and the cream from the morning milk inside a mozzarella skin.
   Source: https://italysegreta.com/burrata-in-puglia-retains-its-simple-origins/ and https://forbes.com.br/forbes-life/2016/11/conheca-a-historia-da-burrata-o-queijo-que-veio-da-neve/ (summary): "during a heavy February snowfall was unable to travel to the local market and decided to combine the curd, leftover scraps of mozzarella, and cream from the top layer of morning milking."
4. Burrata di Andria became a PGI (IGP) in November 2016.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrata (summary): "In November 2016, burrata di Andria became a protected geographical indication (PGI) product."

Brennan's link: the menu prints "Creamy Burrata". Whether it is Burrata di Andria IGP, another Italian burrata or an American one is not public. Lineup question.

## 9. Aged Sherry (Turtle Soup)

1. Sherry must come from the triangle of Jerez de la Frontera, Sanlúcar de Barrameda and El Puerto de Santa María, in the province of Cádiz.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherry_Triangle (summary): "Sherry must legally come from the Sherry Triangle, an area in the province of Cádiz between Jerez de la Frontera, Sanlúcar de Barrameda, and El Puerto de Santa María."
2. The Jerez denominación de origen, established in 1933, was the first in Spain.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherry (snippet): "In 1933 the Jerez denominación de origen was the first Spanish denominación to be officially recognised in this way, officially named D.O. Jerez-Xeres-Sherry."
3. Ageing is by criaderas y solera, historically only within the three towns.
   Source: https://ec.europa.eu/geographical-indications-register/ (summary): "The all-important ageing process of these protected wines, known as the criaderas y solera, could only be carried out within the Zona de Crianza, the three towns of the Sherry Triangle." (The sherry.wine page "The sherry triangle evolves, from three to ten" reports a later change, unread.)
4. Made mostly from Palomino, from light Manzanilla and fino to oxidatively aged Amontillado and oloroso.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherry (snippet): "produced in a variety of styles made primarily from the Palomino grape, ranging from light versions such as Manzanilla and fino, to darker and heavier versions that have been allowed to oxidise as they age in barrel, such as Amontillado and oloroso."
5. Brennan's published turtle soup recipe uses dry sherry and hard-boiled egg; the flavour "is heightened by a touch of sherry".
   Source: https://www.saveur.com/article/Recipes/Brennans-Turtle-Soup/ and https://thelocalpalate.com/recipes/brennans-turtle-soup (summary): "The flavor of this silky, rich soup is heightened by a touch of sherry." Ingredients include "dry sherry", "hard-boiled eggs and lemon"; one version finishes with "eggs, spinach, lemon zest, lemon juice and sherry".

Brennan's link: the menu prints "Aged Sherry". The wine list's Sherries are Bodega Barbadillo "Pale Cream", Bodega Valdespino "VOS Solera 1842" Palomino and La Cigarrera Manzanilla; the bar pours Pedro Ximénez in the Banane au Chocolat. Which, if any, goes into the soup is a lineup question; the published recipe says only dry sherry.

## 10. Madeira (Madeira-glazed broccolini, Creole Hanger Steak)

1. Madeira, the Portuguese island, was discovered by João Gonçalves Zarco and Tristão Vaz Teixeira in 1419; settlers brought vines, and the wine trade grew in the 16th century.
   Source: https://www.portugal.com/food-drink/madeira-wine/ and https://www.madeirawineanddine.com/?p=290 (summary): "first discovered by the Portuguese navigators João Gonçalves Zarco and Tristão Vaz Teixeira in 1419... The first settlers brought grapevines with them, and the wine industry wasn't well-established until the 16th century."
2. Wines crossing the equator in ships' holds were heated and improved; Madeira makers learned to reproduce the voyage.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira_wine (summary): "Madeira wines crossed the Atlantic on sea voyages, where they were submitted to high temperatures causing evaporation and concentration, transforming their aromas and color... wines exposed to equatorial voyage heat developed superior flavor and stability."
3. Fortified with grape spirit, then aged by canteiro (casks warmed by the sun in lofts) or estufagem (tanks heated to at most 50°C for at least 90 days), pioneered in 1794 by Pantaleão Fernandes.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira_wine and https://www.winewithseth.com/winewiki/madeira-production-estufagem-vs-canteiro/ (summary): "Estufagem uses stainless steel tanks heated to a maximum of 50°C for a minimum of 90 days." "The process was pioneered in 1794 by Pantaleão Fernandes." "The canteiro ageing method uses large casks stored in specialized warehouses heated by the sun."
4. Why it keeps: already heated and oxidised, canteiro wines survive decades, even after opening.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira_wine (summary): "they are long-lived in the bottle, and those produced by the canteiro method will survive for decades and even centuries, even after being opened."
5. The four noble grapes name the styles: Sercial (dry), Verdelho (medium dry), Bual (medium sweet), Malmsey from Malvasia (sweet).
   Source: https://www.diffordsguide.com/encyclopedia/43/bws/madeira-fortified-wine and https://www.dummies.com/article/madeira-styles-and-grape-varieties-139652 (summary): "Sercial (dry), Verdelho (medium dry...), Bual (medium sweet), and Malmsey (sweet)." "Made from Malvasia grapes, Malmsey is dark amber, sweet."
6. It was the favourite wine of the American colonies, beloved of the Founding Fathers (Benjamin Franklin, John Adams, Alexander Hamilton), and the signers toasted the Declaration of Independence with it in 1776.
   Source: https://www.decanter.com/learn/independence-day-celebrations-madeira-and-the-founding-fathers-483328/ (summary): "As the American colonies didn't yet have a wine industry, it greatly depended on imports and Madeira was by far the most popular... when the Declaration of Independence was signed... it was toasted by the signatories with a glass of Madeira."

Brennan's link: the menu prints "Madeira-glazed Broccolini". The wine list carries Broadbent "Single Cask" Malvasia 2009, Broadbent "Single Cask" Verdelho 1997 and Henriques & Henriques "Founder's Solera" Verdelho 1894. The kitchen's cooking Madeira is not named; never say the glaze is made from a list bottle.

An adversarial check of every claim in the group's producer profiles, with fourteen further WebSearch calls (standard mode). Passages are the search tool's summaries of the pages named. What changed, and why:

1. Grana Padano's area is 32 provinces, not 33 (section 1, fact 9 corrected).
   Source: https://www.granapadano.it/en-en/the-art-of-craftsmanship/the-iconic-product/grana-padano-and-parmigiano-reggiano-particular-aspects-and-specifications/ (summary): "The production area for Grana Padano DOP includes 32 provinces and five different regions: Piedmont, Lombardy, Veneto, Emilia Romagna, and Trentino South Tyrol." 2. Grana Padano's DOP in 1996 confirmed: Commission Regulation (CE) no. 1107/96 of 12 June 1996. Minimum ageing nine months against Parmigiano Reggiano's twelve confirmed.
   Source: https://www.granapadano.it/en-en/?p=18006 and https://www.granapadano.it/en-en/?p=86432 (summary).
3. Lysozyme is not in every Grana Padano; the egg note now says so, and egg questions still go to the kitchen.
   Source: https://www.granapadano.it/en-en/the-art-of-craftsmanship/the-iconic-product/ingredients/ and university abstracts (air.unimi.it, summary): "lysozyme is not contained in all types of Grana Padano on the market, such as Trentingrana."    Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/part-133/subpart-B and https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1999-04-06/html/99-8440.htm (summary): "Parmesan cheese is the food prepared from milk and other ingredients, characterized by a granular texture and a hard and brittle rind that grates readily. It contains not more than 32 percent of moisture." "In 1973 the FDA reduced the minimum curing time of Parmesan cheese to 10 months from 14 months. 5. Mimolette: the note "not cheddar dye" was wrong, since cheddar is also coloured with annatto. Corrected.
   Source: https://cheese.com/q-and-a/why-is-cheddar-cheese-orange/ and https://news.cornell.edu/node/301436 (summary): "The orange color in cheddar cheese comes from the South America-grown red annatto seed."
