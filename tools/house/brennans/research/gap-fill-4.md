### 1.3 Reading the award history
- First year held: 1983 (search). Last year of the first run: 2005 (search). (expert) Wine Spectator announces its restaurant awards each summer, so the 2005 award was in hand before Katrina struck on 29 August 2005. The cellar loss came after the 2005 award, which is why "1983 to 2005" and "lost to Katrina" are both true.
- Years lapsed: 2006 to 2020 inclusive, that is no Grand Award for fifteen award years (search: "more than 15 tumultuous years"). Whether Brennan's held a lower tier (Best of Award of Excellence) in some of those years, for instance after the 2014 reopening, is not in any snippet. (expert, low) It is plausible but unverified.
- Year regained: 2021 (search, three Wine Spectator items and the house press release). Brennan's was one of three restaurants to win a new Grand Award in 2021 (search, article title "3 Restaurants Win New Grand Awards in 2021").
- Tier in 2026: the winespectator.com restaurant-awards detail page for Brennan's carried the title "Brennan's Restaurant, New Orleans - Grand Award" in the October 2026 snippet. (expert) That page title reflects the current listing. No 2026 award text was read, so confidence is medium, not high. The whereyat.com article on the 2025 New Orleans winners appeared in the link list but its text about Brennan's was not captured.
- Counts printed: "more than 15,000 bottles" (Wine Spectator 2021); "17,000-bottle inventory" (Wine Spectator, by wording); "nearly 1,400 labels and over 15,000 bottles" (Wine Room page); "more than 3,000 selections" (Wine Spectator Tidwell profile); "35,000 bottles" lost in 2005 (Wikipedia and NPR). ### 2.3 Expert knowledge, labelled, never a fact about Brennan's
- (expert, high) Rare was created by Piper-Heidsieck as its prestige cuvée with the 1976 vintage. The bottle carries a gilded tiara by Arthus-Bertrand, recalling the vine tiara Florens-Louis Heidsieck presented to Marie Antoinette in 1785. Since 2018 Rare has been presented as a house in its own right, Rare Champagne, within the EPI group (Descours family), which also owns Piper-Heidsieck and Charles Heidsieck. The PDF's "began as Piper-Heidsieck's prestige cuvée" is right and slightly dated.
- (expert, medium) Rare declares a vintage only in exceptional years. Vintages I know for the white Rare Millésime: 1976, 1979, 1985, 1988, 1990, 1998, 1999, 2002, 2006, 2008 and 2013. Vintages I know for Rare Rosé: 2007, 2008, 2012 and 2014. Rare Millésime blends are Chardonnay-led, about 70 percent Chardonnay and 30 percent Pinot Noir; Rare Rosé 2012 is also Chardonnay-led, roughly 56 percent Chardonnay and 44 percent Pinot Noir. Rare cuvées spend eight years or more on lees before release; Rare Millésime 2008 was released in 2018 and Rare Millésime 2013 in the early 2020s. Dosage sits in the brut range in single figures of grams per litre; I will not print a number without a tech sheet.
- (expert, low) Whether a white Rare Millésime 2012 exists: I cannot confirm one. The sommelier dossier listed 2012 among declared vintages; the floor dossier said a 2012 blanc is not known. Both are expert positions. A 2012 blanc released after the 2013 would be out of sequence, which argues against it, but Rare has released out of sequence before (the Rosé 2008 after the Millésime 2008 is a different cuvée, so that is not a precedent). The three readings of "Rare Brut 2012" on Binwise are: a Rare Rosé 2012 with the colour dropped; a Rare Millésime 2013 mistyped; or a 2012 blanc I do not know. The PDF's own instruction, check which bottle is open, is the only safe line.
- (expert, medium) Cellar master. Régis Camus was chef de cave of Piper-Heidsieck and Charles Heidsieck from 2002, handed Charles Heidsieck to Cyril Brun in 2015 and Piper-Heidsieck to Émilien Boutillat in 2018, and has been the chef de cave of Rare Champagne since the house was set up in 2018. He was named Sparkling Winemaker of the Year at the International Wine Challenge eight times. (expert, low) Whether he still holds the Rare post in 2026, or has retired and been succeeded, I cannot verify; the requested search "Régis Camus retire Rare" did not run. ### 3.3 Expert knowledge, labelled
- (expert, high) Piper-Heidsieck spells the cuvée "Essentiel", the French word. The range began with Essentiel Cuvée Réservée, an Extra Brut version of the house Cuvée Brut aimed at the on-trade, launched around 2016 under Régis Camus; Essentiel Blanc de Blancs followed.
- (expert, medium) Essentiel Cuvée Réservée is Pinot Noir-led with Meunier and a smaller share of Chardonnay (the house Cuvée Brut is roughly 55 percent Pinot Noir, 30 percent Meunier, 15 percent Chardonnay, and Essentiel is built on the same base), aged about four years on lees against about two for the Cuvée Brut, dosed at about 5 to 6 grams per litre (hence Extra Brut), and carries its disgorgement date on the label.
- (expert, medium) The PDF's grape order (Pinot Noir, Meunier, Chardonnay) and the Extra Brut designation match Essentiel exactly. The most economical reading is that "Brennan's Essential by Piper-Heidsieck" is Essentiel Cuvée Réservée under a co-branded label, a thing Piper-Heidsieck does for partners. That is an inference. A bespoke blend is possible but less likely. Until a bottle is seen, the server line should be "labelled for us", not "made for us".
- (expert, low) "Bone-dry" on p41 overstates an Extra Brut at 5 to 6 grams; "very dry" is safer.

## 7. Coravin

- (pdf, p41 heading) "By the glass (Rare on Coravin)." (pdf, p41) Rare "Coravin-preserved: reseal right after each pour and present the bottle." (pdf, p43 heading) "Chardonnay and fuller whites, by the glass (Leflaive on Coravin)." (pdf, p43) Leflaive "Poured by Coravin: needle through the cork, pour, and return the bottle to the cooler." (pdf, p49) "pours Rare Brut 2012 and Leflaive Mâcon-Verzé by Coravin."
- (search) No recorded snippet mentions Coravin at Brennan's, and all Coravin queries hit the budget. No published mention of Coravin Sparkling at Brennan's was found.
- (expert, high) Coravin's needle systems (the Timeless range and the Pivot) are for still wine; the maker says they must not be used on sparkling wine because of bottle pressure. Coravin Sparkling, launched in 2021, is a separate device: the cork is removed, a clamp stopper is fitted, and the bottle is re-pressurised with CO2 between pours. So "needle through the cork" fits the Leflaive, and "Coravin" for the Rare can only mean the Sparkling stopper, where the cork is pulled. The PDF's "reseal right after each pour" is consistent with that device. Which device the house owns is unknown.
