Fun Facts With Chef Kevin

  traditional smoked-meat pickling spices on a steak for his lunch
- In the late 1800's, steak tartare was originally called beefsteak a L'Americaine before a version was created by

### C2. The Burgundy class sheet (three pages, in the order printed)
- Aligoté is the second most (if a distant second) important white variety in Burgundy. Aligoté is usually used for
  varietal white wines, particularly in the commune of Bouzeron, in the Côte Chalonnaise.
- Bâtonnage is the French term for stirring settled lees back into wine. To remind you, "lees" are the sediment of
  winemaking, usually made up of dead yeast and bits of grape seeds and solids. Winemakers sometimes like to keep some
  of these solids in contact with the wine as a way to extract flavor, aroma and texture. The solids can then be
  filtered or fined out before bottling, or the wine can be racked, leaving the solids behind.
- Beaujolais in southern Burgundy is the home of the Gamay grape. The wines are almost universally inexpensive and
  range in quality from the basic Beaujolais Nouveau (a wine released shortly after harvest) to the more complex wines
  of the 10 Beaujolais cru villages.
- Bourgogne AOC This is the basic level of appellation in Burgundy. Wines from any area in the entire region of
  Burgundy may be labeled as Bourgogne AOC. This is typically the most inexpensive offering in a producer's portfolio,
  and it represents basic varietal quality without definition of site or terroir.
- Bouzeron is a village in the Chalonnaise known for producing Aligoté
- Chablis is the northernmost region in Burgundy, just a handful of miles south of the Aube in Champagne, focuses on
  white wine production.
- Chardonnay Is the principal white grape of burgundy and is the most planted grape variety as well, accounting for
  almost half the planted land. Known as Beaunois (from Beaune) in the Yonne, Chardonnay is a natural crossing of
  Pinot and Gouais Blanc, and its modern name may derive from the commune of Chardonnay, in the Mâconnais.
  Chardonnay's cradle is likely somewhere among the fields of Burgundy, and it may be quite ancient. Confusion has
  reigned, however: the variety was once known as Pinot-Chardonnay and has frequently been mistaken for Pinot Blanc.
- Côte de Beaune is the southern part of the Côte d'Or. It starts North of the town of Beaune and extends to the
  village of Santenay.
- Côte Chalonnaise A larger, cooler region to the south of the Côte d'Or, this region can be a good source for value
  wines, from the villages of Rully, Givry, Montagny, and Mercurey. The village of Bouzeron is unique in Burgundy,
  producing white wines from the local Aligoté grape rather than Chardonnay.
- Côte du Nuit is a region located in the northern part of the Côte d'Or the limestone ridge that is at the heart of
  Burgundy. It extends from Dijon to just south of the Nuits-Saint-Georges
- Côte d'Or The "golden slope" of Burgundy begins just south of the city of Dijon and occupies a narrow vertical
  corridor. The Côte d'Or is divided into two sectors: the Côte de Nuit and the Côte de Beaune. Burgundy's top
  vineyards and most famous Villages are located here.
- Domaine is a winery that produces wine from vineyards under its ownership.
- Gamay is a sibling of Chardonnay and a progeny of Pinot and Gouais Blanc. An old variety, Gamay has been the
  subject of repeated condemnations, starting in 1395 with Philip the Bold's ban on cultivation within the environs
  of Burgundy, and Gamay wines have historically received faint praise at best. Today, however, the wines of
  Beaujolais, Gamay's home, are better than ever, and the best are an alternative in quality to midtier examples of
  Pinot Noir.
- Mâconnais: A large region south of the Côte Chalonnaise, the Mâconnais is predominantly a white wine area. Its
  Chardonnay is generally of everyday quality. Pouilly-Fuissé is its most notable appellation.
- Monopole is an area controlled by a single winery and can be as small as a named vineyard (lieu-dit) or as large
  as an entire appellation d'origine (AOC). Frequently this is mentioned on the label as it is rare for only one
  winery to produce all the wine from an area entitled to a certain name. Each wine is sold by only one company.
- Négociant is a merchant who assembles the produce of smaller growers and winemakers and sells the result under its
  own name
- Sauvignon Blanc This Bordeaux variety is planted in the Yonne department in the vineyards of Saint-Bris AOP
- Passe-Toute-Grains AOP Is a blended wine of gamay and Pinot Noir.
- Village AOC Many prominent villages in Burgundy have AOC status. This assures the wine was produced from grapes
  grown in that particular village. The village AOCs of the Côte d'Or are the most renowned, and the best villages
  have a classic style profile associated with them.
- Premier Cru AOC Within each of the better villages, there are certain single vineyards believed to produce superior
  wines, and accorded additional respect in the AOC laws. These are known as premier crus or 1er crus. If the term
  appears on a label, the wine is likely to be superior in quality to a village-level wine, and it is undoubtedly
  higher in price and scarcer in quantity. Premier cru wines often include the name of a single premier cru vineyard.
- Grand Cru AOC Grand crus are single vineyards so highly regarded in quality that they are granted their own AOC
  status. They range in size from 2 to nearly 400 acres. Grand cru AOCs (Chambertin, Montrachet, and la Romanée)
  command so much fame and prestige that the villages around them (Gevrey-Chambertin, Puligny-Montrachet,
  Chassagne-Montrachet, and Vosne-Romanée) adopted the vineyards' names.

### C3. Introductory Wine Vocabulary (five pages photographed; one page between Chaptalization and Fortified wine did not arrive)
Page 1
- ABV Abbreviation of alcohol by volume, generally listed on a wine label
- acidity The liveliness and crispness in wine that activates our salivary glands
- aeration The deliberate addition of oxygen to round out and soften a wine
- aging: holding wine in barrels, tanks, and bottles to advance them to a more desirable state
- alcohol: ethanol (ethyl alcohol), the product of fermentation of sugars by yeast
- AOC Abbreviation for Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée, (English: Appellation of controlled origin), as specified
  under French law. The AOC laws specify and delimit the geography from which a particular wine (or other food
  product) may originate and methods by which it may be made. The regulations are administered by the Institut
  National des Appellations d'Origine (INAO)
- Aromatic Is used to describe a wine with very noticeable and distinctive aromas.
- astringent: tasting term noting the harsh, bitter, and drying sensations in the mouth caused by high levels of
  tannin
- barrel Is the the oak container used for fermenting and aging wine
- Barrique Is a 225-liter oak barrel used originally for storing and aging wines, originating in Bordeaux

Page 2
- Bâtonnage is the French term for stirring settled lees back into wine. To remind you, "lees" are the sediment of
  winemaking, usually made up of dead yeast and bits of grape seeds and solids. Winemakers sometimes like to keep some
  of these solids in contact with the wine as a way to extract flavor, aroma and texture. The solids can then be
  filtered or fined out before bottling, or the wine can be racked, leaving the solids behind.
- body Is the tactile sensation describing the weight and fullness of wine in the mouth. Wine can be light, medium,
  or full-bodied.
- Botrytis Is a beneficial mold that pierces the skin of grapes and causes dehydration, resulting in natural grape
  juice exceptionally high in sugar. Botrytis is largely responsible for the world's finest dessert wines. (see
  "noble rot")
- BRUT A label designation for a dry sparkling wine, especially a champagne.
- Carbonic maceration Whole, uncrushed grapes are fermented in a sealed vat containing a layer of carbon dioxide.
  This results in fruity, soft and distinct red wines. These wines have little tannin and are immediately drinkable.
  This is the method used throughout France's Beaujolais region.
- Cava Spanish term for a sparkling wine made according to the traditional method.
- Cepage French term for grape variety. When it appears on a wine label it will usually refer to the varieties used
  to make the wine.
- Chaptalization The practice of adding sugar (from sugar beets or sugarcanes) to the grape must prior to fermenting,
  to compensate for low sugar content/potential alcohol in the grapes.

- Pétillant French term for a lightly sparkling wine
- Premier cru French term for a "First growth". Used mostly in conjunction with the wines of Burgundy and Champagne

- Tannins Are the phenolic compounds in wines that leave a bitter, dry, and puckery feeling in the mouth.

  climate, and the plant's genetics.
- Varietal Wines made from a single grape variety.
- Vendange tardive French term denoting a late harvest wine.

- Vinification Is the process of making wine
