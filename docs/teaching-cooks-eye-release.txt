THE COOK'S EYE: CONNECTED VISUAL TRAINING
23 October 2026

The first implementation from the World Table visual audit connects existing
artwork to the place where a learner reads or recalls the subject, then adds six
studies where a visible difference helps explain a professional cooking method.

Find the collection in Brennan's > Kitchen studies > Illustrated kitchen studies,
or in the Plates library (/plates). The Cook's Eye is the first companion group.
Its studies also appear on matching technique pages, Brennan's dish cards,
Cuisine lessons and revealed study cards. Fieldbook connections use reviewed
card/lexicon identities; shared words do not create automatic matches.

The six studies
1. Brown butter: melted, nut-brown and scorched milk solids.
2. Custards: pouring anglaise, starch-thickened pastry cream and chilled cremeux.
3. Meringue: soft, medium and stiff peaks, with recipe-specific endpoints.
4. Puff pastry: raw lamination, open baked layers and a lower, closer result.
5. Braising, confit and rendering: different cooking media and purposes.
6. Agnolotti del plin: filling, folding, pinching, separating and checking seams.

Each has a numbered text key, Observe/Explain/Apply, a question with a concealed
explanation, and links to primary teaching sources. Whole illustration/View
controls enlarge a reviewed area on a phone. The enlargement dialog retains the
complete original image. Ungraded recall hides the key, answers and revealing
alt text until requested. It does not change a learner's saved grades.

Accuracy boundaries
The imagery is generated and illustrative. It is not Brennan's approved plating,
portion, recipe or production method. House applications use saved menu facts.
The custard study does not stand in for a baked bread-pudding endpoint. A photo
cannot establish temperature, tenderness, safe storage or an exact ingredient
list. Recipe-specific checks remain in readable text. The duck-confit image was
corrected to show a covering fat surface; the pasta image was corrected so the
separated pieces preserve the pinched form established in the sequence.

Connections and recovery
Fieldbook readings now open their reviewed studies in place; ingredient-only
matches lead to the existing ingredient guide. Cuisine lessons reveal studies
in place and card backs link to them after the answer is shown. Dish comparisons
show each dish's distinct final-plate illustration with the existing caption.
Images load only when a study opens; the readable key remains without artwork.
Failed image loads retain a retry control. Day/night styling uses house tokens.
The 12 new WebPs total 894,872 bytes, with 1536x1024 masters and 420x280 thumbs.
They remain outside the offline shell's precache and use the existing bounded
runtime image cache. No course, card, dish, grade or study identity was replaced.

Editorial changes are recorded separately in culinary-visual-corrections-2026-10-23.txt.
Exact generation/edit prompts and visual checks: teaching-cooks-eye-prompts.txt.
Master hashes and encoding details: teaching-cooks-eye-assets.json.

Validation
Source data is re-emitted through build:data and build:cuisine. Validation covers
exact references, asset pairs, view bounds, sources, unchanged sealed data,
read/reveal/return flow, image failure recovery, lazy loading, keyboard controls,
phone layouts in both services, and offline study access. Release results and
commit identifiers are recorded in the task's completion message.
