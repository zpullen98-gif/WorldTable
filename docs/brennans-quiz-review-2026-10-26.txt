BRENNAN'S QUIZ LEARNING REVIEW
26 October 2026

Purpose
Make every revealed answer a useful learning doorway in World Table,
Bartender's Ledger and Sommelier's Codex. Keep the live round easy to use:
answer, read the feedback, open Learn why, then continue or follow the
specific reading and return. Do not reveal teaching context before the answer.

Findings
The existing collection already contains substantial kept descriptions,
component explanations, producer stories, wine profiles and service notes.
Several quiz screens reduced these to the question and correct label.
World Table's older dishes quiz relied on option colour for feedback.
Reading links and question identity were not consistently carried through
rounds, especially the Ledger pairing adapter. These are the main changes
in this edition, rather than adding unsupported menu facts.
The separate legacy dishes quiz could treat duplicate descriptions or
ingredient sets as a unique answer. Its question selection now suppresses
those ambiguous forms and selects distinct names without a resampling loop.
The shared engine audit below tests a different generator.

Reading principles
Learn why opens in place after an answer, including a correct answer.
Its full reading draws from the exact record involved, with a descriptive
link to the relevant dish, drink, wine or supporting reference where available.
Kept marks only: an unaccepted draft must not become the house's answer.
Imported text is escaped, and explanatory links must use real record IDs.
Examples of suitable cocktail families are comparisons, not the house recipe.
Pairings are the house's saved first picks, not a claim that every other wine
would be inappropriate. Saved prices and availability are not live inventory.
Missing measures, glassware, garnish or production details remain missing.
No preparation, allergen or alcohol claim is inferred from an illustration.

Interaction principles
Visible textual feedback accompanies option colour. Native disclosures have
a keyboard action and a visible focus indicator, and controls retain a
minimum 44-pixel target. Inline reading does not grade a second time.
Following a reading and returning should restore the live question, answer,
position and score. Previous records and stable item identities are retained.
Phone layouts are checked in both service palettes.

Shared engine audit
The current saved house pack has 70 dishes, 32 cocktails and 271 wines.
The shared engine's fifteen multiple-choice families were exercised through
ten deterministic complete rounds each, producing 19,320 dealt questions.
Every dealt question had four distinct options, exactly one matching answer,
a nonempty stem and a resolvable house record ID. No conflicting options were
found among exact matching stems with different answer labels in this run.
This is a structural and exact-duplicate audit, not a claim that wording
similarity establishes culinary truth or pairing exclusivity.

These are overlapping teaching families, not additional menu items.

Evidence
The item explanations remain tied to the saved house pack and its kept
research, including the existing food, wine and bar reviews. No new source
is used to silently replace the user's restaurant menus. Source-specific
UI and browser checks are recorded with the three implementations, and the
final coordinated publication is checked by the public site's nine gates.
The audit output and release verification are saved in the task's
outputs/brennans-quiz-review-2026-10-26 directory.
