VISUAL ACCEPTANCE
All six final compositions were checked against the 27 numbered teaching keys.
- Blanching: raw beans, boiling water, ice water, then drainage and a finished
  portion. The image cannot establish texture, holding safety or exact timing.
- Coating: the chicken flour/egg/crumb/cooked sequence is separate from the
  courgette batter example. The thermometer displays no invented reading.
- Sugar: cooled soft-ball, firm-ball and brittle-thread samples remain separate
  from the pale/amber/dark caramel row. The image contains no generated numeric
  temperature instructions. The HTML provides the source-attributed guides.
- Choux: the revised second view shows a tapered hanging V, replacing the
  earlier continuous ribbon. The baked puff shows an empty cavity; filling is
  not confused with the shell.
- Panisse/grits/polenta: the earlier pearl-like maize textures were rejected.
  The final revision uses irregular dry fragments and cohesive creamy cooked
  textures with small grains. It is approved as a general selected comparison,
  not as proof that all grits are white or all polenta is yellow. The creamy
  grits image is not the house's fried pimento-cheese grit cake.
- Beurre blanc: the reduction, gradual butter addition and cohesive sauce are
  distinct from the last view's visible pools of separated fat.
Focus windows retain the corresponding numbers and food subjects. Alt text was
aligned to the actual vessels and revised choux shape. The images teach the
specified general distinctions; they do not certify house plating, allergens,
temperatures or food safety.
